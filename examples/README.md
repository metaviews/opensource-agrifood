# Open Agrifood Examples

This directory is the curated collection of open and commons-oriented projects relevant to agrifood.

It is not a global directory, vendor catalogue, or list of every project that uses the word “open.” Each record earns its place by showing a meaningful open layer and helping answer a research question about control, access, maintenance, governance, or benefit.

The Agrifood AI Field Guide remains a discovery and comparative resource: https://agrifood.metaviews.ca/. New records should cite original project, institutional, academic, standards, legal, or policy sources wherever possible.

## Record statuses

- `lead`: discovered but not yet sufficiently checked;
- `candidate`: relevant project with a basic source record;
- `curated`: source-grounded record completed with limitations;
- `featured`: selected for comparative synthesis;
- `archived`: inactive, superseded, or no longer fits the collection.

## Inclusion test

A project should normally satisfy at least three of these conditions:

1. It has a direct agrifood relationship or is clearly relevant enabling infrastructure.
2. Something meaningful is open: software, model, weights, dataset, standard, governance arrangement, biological material, or public infrastructure.
3. A reliable source describes the project.
4. It illuminates a question important to Open Source Agrifood.
5. It is possible to state what the project does not demonstrate.

“Open” is recorded asset by asset. Open code does not imply open data; open weights do not imply open training data; public-interest framing does not automatically establish open source.

The current set is a starting backbone, not a fixed target of seven or ten examples. Further examples should be added when they correct a meaningful coverage gap, introduce a distinct governance or technical pattern, or materially improve the comparative argument. In particular, the collection should not be allowed to become AI-heavy by default; non-AI agrifood examples may be needed to establish the wider field.

## Next curation work

- Review the seven initial records for consistency and comparative usefulness.
- Identify missing examples by analytical and regional gap, not by an arbitrary quota.
- Add farmer-, community-, Indigenous-, cooperative-, and regionally rooted examples where they strengthen the collection.
- Promote records to `featured` only when they support a comparative synthesis.
- Keep unresolved leads in the research gap register rather than expanding this collection indiscriminately.
