# SEARICE

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Southeast Asia (Philippines-based; regional programmes)
- Project: https://www.searice.org.ph/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the biological-commons cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

SEARICE (Southeast Asia Regional Initiatives for Community Empowerment) strengthens farmer-led seed systems in Southeast Asia: farmers as custodians of biodiversity and key actors in resilient food systems, against dependence on commercial seed and restrictive plant-variety protection regimes.

## Open layer

Community-managed biological commons and farmer-led seed governance: community seed banks, participatory plant breeding (PPB) and variety selection (PVS), farmer field schools, and policy advocacy for farmers' rights to save, use, exchange, and sell seeds.

## What is actually open

- SEARICE CONSERVE: a community-based seed research centre preserving over 1,100 rice varieties, maintained, evaluated, and multiplied by farmers and researchers collaboratively.
- Community seed banks managed by farmers, with training in seed selection, breeding, conservation, and storage.
- Participatory plant breeding co-develops locally adapted varieties; PVS lets farmers' priorities (taste, yield, pest resistance, cooking quality) guide selection; women and youth are named as leaders in these processes.
- Policy layer: SEARICE opposes UPOV-based plant variety protection and encourages sui generis PVP systems that respect seed sovereignty; it reports influencing legislation and ordinances that embed seed rights.

## Governance and control

Regional NGO working through partnerships with local governments, universities, and farmer associations. Farmers hold the design position in seed management; SEARICE is the facilitating organisation, not a farmer-owned body itself.

## Evidence of use

- The site documents current programmes (Farmer Field Schools, PPB/PVS, CSBs, a FAO Benefit-Sharing Fund project dated 2025).
- The community seed bank literature treats SEARICE-type institutions as central to farmer seed security (Vernooy et al., cited 130+ for the CSB climate-resilience article; the FAO/GLIS records SEARICE's community-managed seed systems).
- Policy impact claims (legislation influence) are organisation-reported.

## Maintenance and funding

NGO-funded via partnerships and project grants (e.g., FAO Benefit-Sharing Fund). Long-term CSB sustainability beyond project support is a documented concern in the literature (Vernooy 2024).

## What this case demonstrates

SEARICE is the strongest located regional institution for farmer-led seed systems and community seed banks in Asia: participatory breeding, community conservation at scale (1,100+ rice varieties), and anti-UPOV policy advocacy. It adds the community-institution layer to the collection's biological-commons set — distinct from licence-based systems (OSSL/OSSI) and Indigenous territory governance (Potato Park).

## What it does not demonstrate

- It is not an open-licence system; community seed banks are managed commons with community-defined access.
- CSB counts, conservation scale, and policy outcomes are organisation-reported.
- It is a facilitating NGO, not farmer-owned governance.
- Sustainability beyond project funding is unresolved (G-BC-03).
