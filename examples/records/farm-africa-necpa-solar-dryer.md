# Farm Africa / NECPA Low-Cost Solar Dryer

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: Lira, northern Uganda (North East Chilli Producers Association, NECPA); design intended for replication by local artisans
- Project: Farm Africa + NECPA chilli value-chain project; design document: https://www.farmafrica.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/solar-dryer-design.pdf
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the Global South farm-level open hardware cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

Smallholder chilli farmers in northern Uganda lose up to 35% of fruit and vegetable production to poor post-harvest handling; commercial solar dryers cost ~150,000 UGX (~US$50) with ~15kg/week capacity — beyond reach of most smallholders. Farm Africa and NECPA developed and published a low-cost solar dryer design (wooden poles, UV polythene sheeting, black polythene heat absorption) that local artisans can build.

## Open layer

Openly published design for farm-level post-harvest hardware, with a deliberate distribution model: local artisans trained to build the dryers as a business, demand aggregation through NECPA extension officers, and a self-sustaining artisan economy.

## What is actually open

- The design document is published openly on Farm Africa's website (July 2024), describing the design principles (light-to-heat conversion, heat trapping, convection), the materials (readily available, lightweight, cheap, locally workable), and the distribution model.
- No explicit open licence is stated on the document; no maintained design repository or build-tooling package exists. Reuse, derivation, and redistribution terms are therefore unstated — the archetype of the "openly published, not open-licensed" NGO design category.
- The project context is documented: 3-year project, ~3,000 farmers in the sub-counties of Barr, Amach, and Aromo; funded by aBi Development Ltd and the Ajahma Charitable Trust.

## Governance and control

NGO (Farm Africa) with a producer association (NECPA) as implementation partner. Farmers and artisans are participants in the distribution model; the design itself is not under farmer governance.

## Evidence of use

- The document reports the design's adoption rationale and the distribution model (120 farmer groups reachable via extension officers; artisans keep records of every dryer sold).
- Traditional-dryer cost figures (~US$50) and post-harvest loss figures (35%) are cited from project context.
- Actual build counts and farmer-level outcomes were not verified in this pass.

## Maintenance and funding

Project-funded (aBi Development, Ajahma Charitable Trust); the sustainability plan makes artisan businesses self-sustaining through sales. Post-project design maintenance and update responsibility are not documented.

## What this case demonstrates

The Farm Africa/NECPA solar dryer is the archetype of NGO-published open designs in the Global South: a real, field-oriented, locally buildable farm tool design published openly — and the clearest instance of the licence-clarity gap. It is "practically open" without being "open-licensed", which is exactly the boundary this project records.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish an open licence; terms are unstated (candidate status).
- Build counts and farmer-level outcomes are unverified.
- It is a design document, not a maintained open-hardware project with versioning and contribution infrastructure.
- The distribution model's sustainability beyond the project period is projected, not demonstrated.
