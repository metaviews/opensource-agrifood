## Problem addressed

No public Canadian dataset identifies investor or institutional farmland ownership nationally (G-OSA-35), and the transfer wave proceeds unseen. This brief demonstrates that a small academic team can produce that picture from public records: complete land-titles data for all Saskatchewan rural municipalities (Information Services Corporation, December 31, 2023), a methodology the lead authors pioneered in 2014.

## Open layer

Free to read and download; **no copyright or licence statement in the PDF text layer** (verified 2026-11-18). The analysis is open knowledge in the free-to-read sense only — the method itself is not licensed for reuse, which is precisely the gap the succession program's L6 layer would close.

## What is actually open (findings, verified from the PDF)

- **Large-scale private concentration is accelerating:** 36 owners holding 601,448 acres in 2018 → 84 owners holding 1.44 million acres in 2023 — 2.4% of all Saskatchewan farmland, +140% in five years. Mega-farms lead: Monette Farms (SK/AB/MB/BC/Arizona/ND), Primrose Livestock, Kambeitz Farms — owned figures understate total land base, which typically includes rented land.
- **Investor ownership stabilized, then declined:** increased 2014–2018, declined 2018–2023. CPPIB (157,000+ acres at peak) completely divested Saskatchewan farmland by 2023, netting $390 million (reported 2022). Avenue Living became the second-largest investor owner. Threshold: investors 3,000+ acres; large non-investor owners 10,000+ acres.
- **No evidence of non-Canadian ownership** among investors or other large-scale owners — an evidence-based correction to the "foreign ownership" frame that dominated the province's Fall 2025 legislature debates (following the Fall 2024 Auditor General report on residency-requirement verification).
- Saskatchewan frame: 34,000 farms, $21.5B revenue (2024), farmland values averaging +11%/yr 2005–2024.

## Governance and control

University policy school (Johnson Shoyama Graduate School of Public Policy); named researchers (André Magnan, Darrin Qualman, Emily Duncan, Annette Aurélie Desmarais — the last also President of the National Farmers Foundation). Data from ISC public titles; investor identification via Corporate Registry and internet searches.

## Evidence of use

Follows the team's 2014 precedent analysis; fed the 2024–2026 public debate on farmland ownership (Auditor General report, legislature exchanges). It is cited in this corpus's succession program as the method precedent for an open-licensed, repeatable farmland-ownership analysis.

## Maintenance and funding

Policy-school publication; brief dated March 30, 2026; no stated update schedule.

## Why it belongs

It converts the land scan's most quoted absence — "the public cannot see it" — from a lament into a method. Every input it uses (titles records, corporate registry) is public; the missing layer is licensing and repeatability, not data. For the succession program's L6 and any future `docs/policy/` design document, this brief is both evidence (concentration is the actual growth story, not foreign ownership) and prototype (the pipeline an open layer would productize).

## What it does not demonstrate

An open licence on the method; national coverage (Saskatchewan only, one snapshot year); rented-land concentration (explicitly excluded from its owned-land figures); provinces with closed parcel data (Ontario's commercial lock-up) remain unanalyzed by any equivalent.
