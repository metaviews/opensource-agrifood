# Ag Data Commons

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States; USDA-funded or USDA-co-funded research, with public reuse potential
- User guide: https://www.nal.usda.gov/services/agdatacommons
- Portal: https://agdatacommons.nal.usda.gov/
- Field-guide context: new discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

Ag Data Commons addresses the discoverability, access, curation, and reuse of agricultural research data and data products.

## Open layer

Public agricultural data access, metadata, repository services, curation, and FAIR-oriented research infrastructure.

## What is actually open

The USDA National Agricultural Library describes Ag Data Commons as a catalogue and repository for publicly accessible data produced by USDA-funded or USDA-co-funded research. Public users can view and download resources without accounts. Curators review submissions for completeness and accuracy before publication.

## Governance and control

The system is a public-sector infrastructure with defined eligibility rules. Submissions generally require USDA affiliation or funding, public access, and accepted data, data-product, or non-executable-software types. The infrastructure therefore provides public access within a bounded institutional scope rather than a universal agricultural commons.

## Evidence of use

The portal is live and provides browse, search, and download access. The public documentation establishes curation and access procedures; patterns of downstream reuse and farmer/community benefit require separate evidence.

## Maintenance and funding

Ag Data Commons is maintained through the USDA National Agricultural Library. Its durability is institutionally anchored, but its scope and continued access depend on public-sector policy and funding.

## Why it belongs

Ag Data Commons is a useful contrast with GODAN. GODAN primarily coordinates and advocates for open agricultural data; Ag Data Commons provides a curated catalogue and repository tied to a public research-funding system.

## What it does not demonstrate

Public cataloguing and download access do not guarantee consistent licences, interoperability, reuse, farmer control, or benefit outside the USDA-funded research ecosystem.
