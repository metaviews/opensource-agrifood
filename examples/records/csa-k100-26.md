# CSA K100:26 — Food loss and waste: Terminology and measurement

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: Canada (National Standard of Canada — Domestic)
- Project: CSA Group, K100:26
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the grocery concept project (`research/grocery-feedback-2026-08.md` §2/§8; provenance `grocery/research/food-waste-canada.md`); verified 2026-09-02

## Problem addressed

Canada's first national food-loss-and-waste (FLW) terminology and measurement standard: common definitions, measurement and characterization requirements and guidance, data collection and monitoring, environmental-impact evaluation, a five-step measurement plan, and a destination hierarchy for where diverted food goes.

## Open layer

A published measurement standard — a shared data/measurement language, not open source. It belongs to the corpus's standards/data-infrastructure scope: the retail-side counterpart to the farm-side data standards (AgGateway, WAGRI, OADA). It is not open-licensed.

## What is actually open

Nothing is open in the licensing sense, but access is partial: the text is sold via the CSA Store (reseller copies ~US$190 printed), while CSA offers free read-only "View Access" restricted to viewers in Canada — the standard is free to read inside its home jurisdiction and paid everywhere else. First edition, 35 pages, published 2026 (CSA news release 2026-05-12; some coverage says April 2026; an update/erratum is already flagged on the store page).

## Governance and control

Prepared by the Technical Committee on Food Loss and Waste under the Strategic Steering Committee on Agriculture and Agri-food, developed in compliance with Standards Council of Canada requirements and published as a National Standard of Canada (Domestic). CSA Group controls the text and revenue; committee participation determines content.

## Evidence of use

Published and promoted by CSA Group; regulatory adoption is prospective, not active — the standard is voluntary, with a stated path for governments to reference it in future legislation and discussion of inclusion in Canada's national food security strategy's food-waste pillar (FoodNX interview with committee vice-chair Lori Nikkel, 2026-05-20).

## Why it belongs

It documents how the measurement layer gets built at national scale when the instrument itself is a paid product: the vocabulary is meant to be universal, the text is not. Direct counterpart to the corpus's pattern of open *data* inside closed *infrastructure*, here at the standard layer — and the Canadian home-jurisdiction item for the retail/waste cell.

## What it does not demonstrate

A paywalled standard does not create a commons; whether the terminology is taken up under open licences by implementers, or the standard achieves regulatory adoption, is unverified. Its measurement language may circulate in secondary literature regardless of the text's access terms.
