# Standard Data Platform for Autonomous Agriculture

- Status: `lead`
- Region / reach: Canada; partners and grower validation in British Columbia, Alberta, and Saskatchewan
- Project: https://digitalsupercluster.ca/projects/standard-data-platform-for-autonomous-agriculture/
- Field-guide context: Canadian discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

The project sought to integrate farm, field, soil, water, terrain, machinery, weather, and crop data into operational planning for precision and autonomous agriculture.

## Open layer

The project describes a data commons framework, data aggregation, digital-twin models, and interoperable planning concepts. It does not yet establish that the resulting software, data, or models are open source.

## Governance and control

The project’s public description raises data-commons and anonymisation language while also describing commercial platforms and private-sector partners. This makes control, commercialisation, and public-funding terms central unresolved questions.

## Evidence of use

The project reports validation with growers in British Columbia, Alberta, and Saskatchewan. It also reports Agrilyze use with blueberry farmers in Pitt Meadows, British Columbia, and a later Ukrainian deployment. These are project claims requiring independent and current verification.

## Why it matters

This is a valuable contested Canadian case. It can test whether “data commons” language translates into open technical infrastructure and producer control, or whether public funding primarily de-risks proprietary platforms.

## What it does not demonstrate

A project description, data-commons framework, or public funding does not establish open-source licensing, farmer governance, durable public infrastructure, or equitable benefit.
