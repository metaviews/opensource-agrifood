# UBC Farm Seed Catalogue / Seed Hub

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: British Columbia, Canada
- Project: https://ubcfarm.ubc.ca/seed-catalogue/
- Seed Hub: https://ubcfarm.ubc.ca/csfs-research/seed-hub/
- Field-guide context: Canadian discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

The UBC Farm Seed Catalogue supports regional seed production, seed saving, seed trials, and public access to ecologically grown seed adapted to British Columbia conditions.

## Open layer

Biological commons, public-domain seed, open-pollinated varieties, regional adaptation, seed research, and producer-cooperative infrastructure.

## What is actually open

The catalogue describes UBC Farm seeds as certified organic, open-pollinated, regionally adapted, held in the public domain, and GE-free. It reports annual production of 20–30 varieties from 15–20 crops. The Centre for Sustainable Food Systems at UBC Farm identifies itself as a founding member of the BC Eco Seed Co-op, a producer cooperative offering BC-grown seed. At least one UBC Farm tomatillo variety is identified as registered with the Open Source Seed Initiative.

## Governance and control

The case combines university research, farm production, public seed sales, and a producer cooperative. It is relevant to regional seed sovereignty, but the precise governance of the seed catalogue, research data, breeding decisions, and cooperative membership requires further verification.

This is not an Indigenous seed-sovereignty record. Indigenous seed sovereignty involves distinct cultural, legal, relational, and consent obligations that should not be reduced to public-domain or open-source licensing.

## Evidence of use

The catalogue provides public variety information, planting instructions, and purchase pathways. The seed programme links to seed trials research with FarmFolkCityFolk and to the BC Eco Seed Co-op.

## Maintenance and funding

The catalogue is maintained through the UBC Farm and associated seed and food-system institutions. Current cooperative governance, financial sustainability, and breeding continuity require further evidence.

## Why it matters

This is a strong Canadian biological-commons lead that complements OSSI and Bioleft while showing a regional university-farm and producer-cooperative model.

## What it does not demonstrate

Public-domain seed claims do not establish unrestricted access to all genetic materials, community control over all decisions, Indigenous sovereignty, or broad farmer adoption.
