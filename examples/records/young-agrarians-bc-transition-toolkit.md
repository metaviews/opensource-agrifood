# Young Agrarians BC Transition Toolkit for Non-Family Farm Transfer

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Canada (British Columbia; national interest)
- Project: https://youngagrarians.org/bc-transition-toolkit/
- Live docs: downloadable PDF + browsable "Tools" section on the page
- Field-guide context: the closest Canadian analog to "succession knowledge as a commons" found in the C3 pass — a farm-transition toolkit written for exactly the non-family transfer the succession program studies
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

Farm succession literature is overwhelmingly family-succession-shaped, but many retiring farmers have no family successor. This toolkit is "a resource kit for anyone curious about – or neck-deep in – alternative ways to transition land and farms from one generation to the next" (verified from the page). Young Agrarians has run land-access support since 2013 (Land Access Guide, Land Linking Workshops) and the BC Land Matching Program since 2016.

## Open layer

Free to download and browse; no open licence stated. The page carries a legal disclaimer and waiver only ("for general information only… assumes no liability… It is essential to seek professional advice"). Funder named: Real Estate Foundation of British Columbia.

## What is actually open

- The full toolkit as PDF plus browsable per-tool downloads (fully read 2026-11-18).
- **A six-stage transition framework** (Setting Your Vision → Assessing Feasibility → Making a Plan → Document → Implement → follow-through), with process tools per stage: vision-setting worksheets, feasibility assessment, model guides — **Transition to Co-Operative Ownership** and **Transition to Community Ownership** (land trust) — and three in-depth case stories (Horse Lake Farm Co-operative, Lohbrunner Community Farm, and a third farm) plus a legacy-letters download. **No legal instruments**: the toolkit contains process knowledge and model paths, not leases, bylaws, or agreement templates — the legal layer is deferred to professionals by design ("it's essential to have support from your transition team during Stages 4-6").
- Hard Canadian data points stated in the toolkit: "50% of farm operators 35 years of age and younger are leasing land" (verified as reported — the toolkit's own figure); "Across B.C. there are over 20 farms where land ownership is shared and there is more than one agricultural enterprise accessing it. We call these farms Community Farms"; and the Qualified Farm Property tax frame ("a $1 million individual lifetime capital gains exemption… may be possible to transfer Qualified Farm Property to family members on a tax-deferred basis").
- Its acknowledgements name the sector's own knowledge network: MNP LLP, North Valley Law, FCC, Farm Management Canada, Practical Farmers of Iowa, B.C. Co-operatives Association, B.C. Foodlands Co-operative (whose founder's foreword describes the Foodlands Co-operative of BC as a provincial "foodlands trust" and Fraser Common Farm Co-operative as a 35-year community-farm precedent), L'ARTERRE in Quebec, and Land for Good.
- A land acknowledgement naming unresolved Indigenous title, positioning the toolkit in response to "a colonial system of laws and policies" — the interface-with-not-around posture the succession program's frame requires, already practiced by the sector's own operator.

## Maintenance and funding

Grant-funded (Real Estate Foundation of BC); page live and downloadable at check.

## Why it belongs

Canada's succession-knowledge layer exists, at least in BC, in exactly the shape the program's L1/L3 layers imagine — practitioner-authored, non-family-transfer-focused, case-storied — but closed-licensed, so its knowledge cannot be legally forked to other provinces or jurisdictions. It is both the best Canadian evidence that the free-to-read layer exists and a case that the licensing gap extends to the most progressive operators in the sector.

## What it does not demonstrate

An open licence; adoption outcomes; legal instruments (verified absent — the toolkit is process knowledge and model paths, deliberately deferring legal drafting to the transition team).
