# Arctic Co-operatives Limited

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: northern Canada — 30+ independent member-owned multi-purpose co-ops across Nunavut, Northwest Territories, and Yukon
- Project: retail federation (grocery, hotel, fuel, construction, arts marketing via its West Baffin Eskimo Cooperative / Dorset Fine Arts ties); owned and controlled by the member community co-ops
- Field-guide context: opened via the A5 northern/remote food systems scan (G-OSA-39); verified 2026-09-09
- Record type: governance record — like IGFC and Terre de Liens, curated for what the ownership layer does and does not produce; here, the corpus's cleanest ownership-conduct evidence

## Problem addressed

Northern community grocery operates under the worst structural conditions in Canadian food retail: fly-in/ice-road supply chains, tiny markets, extreme climate costs — under private monopolists (the North West Company historically dominant) with weak competitive discipline. Food insecurity in Inuit Nunangat is the visible outcome; the Retail Food Environments literature and the Competition Bureau's southern findings both point at concentrated retail conduct.

## The open layer — and the honest finding

**Governance-open, systems-closed.** Verified points:

- **Ownership**: member co-ops own the federation; the SMU Co-operative Management Education case study (2021) documents 32 member co-ops with community accountability; many Inuit members "came to view them as their own innovation — reflecting a deep sense of collective" ownership (Springer, 2025, Retail store governance in remote Indigenous communities).
- **Conduct under identical conditions** (the corpus's key evidence): economist Nicholas Li's comparison, reported in Food Secure Canada's Hill Times op-ed (2024-09-16) — North West Company profits rose significantly 2019–2023, matching southern retail's inflation-era pattern, while **Arctic Co-op Ltd's net margins and patronage dividends were stable or declining**. Same geography, same customers, opposite extraction. This is the empirical answer to "does ownership structure change behaviour when regulation doesn't."
- **Publications**: annual reports and supply-chain statements (Fighting Against Forced Labour and Child Labour Act filings) are published as PDFs — reported, not open-licensed.
- **Systems**: no open software, hardware, or data layer located. Member stores run conventional proprietary retail stacks. Patronage dividends flow to members, but the transaction/inventory/ordering data layer is vendor-proprietary like any chain store.

## Why it belongs

1. It anchors the A5 scan's first shape (governance-open without systems-open) with the corpus's strongest natural experiment: the ownership layer demonstrably changed conduct in the hardest retail conditions in the country — without any open systems layer.
2. It gives the grocery concept its northern mirror: a real, century-old community grocery system whose governance works, and whose missing layer (open, self-hosted retail systems — see CORE-POS) names exactly where the open movement could contribute.
3. The Northern Review literature ("Continuing to Study and Learn from the Northern Co-operative Experience") situates it as a studied, ongoing co-operative development model — a living archive of what works.

## What it does not demonstrate

- That co-op ownership *caused* the margin stability (scale, mandate, and accounting differences with NWC also differ; the Li comparison is descriptive, not causal identification).
- That the federation produces open artifacts — it does not; the record is curated with absence as the finding, not presence.
- Food-price parity outcomes by store banner (the public data is financial conduct, not shelf-price series).
