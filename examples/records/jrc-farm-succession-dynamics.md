# JRC — Farm succession and land access dynamics (Belgian, Irish and Polish cases)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: European Union (Belgium, Ireland, Poland comparative study)
- Project: https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC145973/JRC145973_01.pdf
- Live docs: same (JRC repository, open access)
- Field-guide context: the C9/C10 pass's process framework — succession documented as a multi-year gradual process with post-installation effects, under an open licence
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

Succession is treated in policy as a transaction; the JRC study treats it as a
process — analysing succession, retirement, and successor effects across three EU
member states.

## Open layer

**CC BY 4.0, © European Union 2026** (verified in the document, 2026-11-18) — the
succession field's second open-licensed research artifact found by this program
(after the FAO land-banking study), and the only peer-reviewed institutional
analysis of succession dynamics found under an open licence.

## What is actually open

- The comparative institutional analysis of succession and land-access dynamics in
  Belgium, Ireland, and Poland, including the finding that succession is "not a
  single event but a gradual process that may start many years before formal
  transfer and continue after installation" (subagent pass quote) and a sharper,
  shorter-lived successor effect for women (10–15 years post-installation) in the
  studied cases.
- Full analysis text under CC BY 4.0 — legally adaptable for the adaptation
  commons' evidence base.

## Why it belongs

Two contributions to the program: it corroborates, from an institutional-analysis
direction, the transition-duration finding that C9 needs (succession is a years-
long process, so transfer vehicles must be designed for the seller's remaining
life, not the transaction); and it is one of the program's rare instances of the
open layer actually present in the succession field — a peer-reviewed succession
study that is legally forkable. It also carries a gender finding (women successors'
effects are sharper but shorter-lived) that C10 records as three-country evidence,
not generalizable.

## What it does not demonstrate

Canadian applicability; generalizability beyond the three cases; the
widow-control hypothesis.
