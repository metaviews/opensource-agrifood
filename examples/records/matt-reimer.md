## Why this person qualifies as a candidate

Matt Reimer is a named Manitoba farmer who built and documented an autonomous grain-cart system using open hardware and source code. His work is a strong historical example of a farmer participating in open technical communities, although current personal maintenance activity is not established.

## Farming role and Canadian connection

MIT News describes Reimer as a grain producer who grew up on his family farm in Manitoba, Canada. CBC describes his farm as a 2,500-acre rural Manitoba operation growing mostly canola and wheat. Farmers Weekly describes him as a Manitoba farmer operating a 220-horsepower John Deere tractor and a 24-tonne grain cart.

These sources establish a Canadian farming role. Acreage, crop mix, and equipment details are reported figures from different periods and should not be treated as a current audited farm profile.

## Open-source and maker participation

Reimer used an open hardware platform and source code from a drone-autopilot ecosystem as the starting point for his autonomous tractor work. MIT News reports that he learned programming through an MITx course and developed an application allowing a combine operator to call the grain cart, position it under the auger, unload while moving, and return it to the field edge.

His public Hackaday profile identifies him as a farmer in Manitoba and lists an Autonomous Tractor project as project owner/contributor. His public GitHub account contains the AutonomousGrainCart repository, described as software for interacting with a tractor equipped with a Pixhawk to make it autonomous. The account also contains public Arduino and ArduPilot-related repositories.

The work was not merely private farm tinkering. Reimer published a public project, participated in a maker community, and made software available for others to inspect and use. Farmers Weekly reports that he encouraged others to use the software and describes the work as built from open-source programming and accessible hardware.

## Evidence of on-farm use

MIT News reports that the driverless tractor unloaded grain from a combine more than 500 times during one harvest and estimated savings of at least CAD $5,000 for that season. This is useful evidence that the project operated on a real farm rather than remaining a prototype demonstration, but it is a historical project-reported account rather than an independently audited impact study.

## What this case demonstrates

Reimer’s case shows how open-source participation can emerge outside a formal agricultural software project. A farmer used open hardware, open code, online learning, public repositories, and a maker community to solve a labour and logistics problem on his own farm.
