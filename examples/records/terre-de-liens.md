# Terre de Liens

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: France (19 regional associations; the reference model cited in Canadian land-access proposals)
- Project: https://terredeliens.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the land sectoral scan (G-OSA-35, sub-cell 2); verified 2026-09-04

## Problem addressed

Farmland enters the market as a speculative asset, pricing out farmers and stripping land of its agricultural vocation. Terre de Liens removes land from that market permanently: citizens' savings buy farms, which are held in perpetuity — "Terre de Liens ne revend pas ses fermes" — and leased long-term to organic/peasant farmers under environmental rural leases (bail rural environnemental), of which Terre de Liens is France's leading prescriber.

## Open layer

**None.** This is the record's analytical point: the world's largest farmland commons, participatory to the jury level, operates closed stewardship. The mentions légales explicitly prohibit reproduction of site content beyond private use — an all-rights-reserved stance, the inverse of open licensing. The farm registry exists as a browsable web map with no downloadable dataset and no open licence. Per-farm economics, lease terms, and tenant-selection criteria are not published as data. Reporting is via PDFs.

## Governance and control

Tripartite, verified from primary pages: (1) **Fédération Terre de Liens** — national association created 2003 (via the RELIER network with La Nef ethical bank, the biodynamic movement, Confédération paysanne), a federation since 2018, governing board of 23 drawn from 19 regional associations plus farmer and employee representatives; (2) **Foncière Terre de Liens** — solidarity investment company (SCA, created 2006, first acquisitions 2007) that owns ~91% of network farms with capital from ~20,000 citizen shareholders (€106/share, 25% income-tax reduction up to 200 shares, 5-year minimum holding, no dividends; Finansol-labelled; ESUS-agreed); Caisse des Dépôts/Banque des Territoires invested €6M in 2014; (3) **Fondation Terre de Liens** — public-utility foundation (2007) receiving land donations and bequests.

## Evidence of use

Current scale (site, 2026-09-04): **449 farms, 14,200 ha, 63,524 members, 1,720 volunteers**. Foncière impact report (own PDF, 2025-12-31): 399 foncière farms, 1,407 ha acquired in 2025 for €12.5M, 37 new farms in 2025. Growth trajectory consistent across vintages (8,500 ha in 2022; ~10,000 ha/405 farms in 2024). Tension is documented too: a Reporterre investigation (2025-04-03) records farmer grievances about "bureaucratisation" and centralisation, including an eviction case — grievances that closed data makes hard to audit, which is itself part of the finding.

## Why it belongs

The strongest possible case for the land cell's closed-stewardship finding: maximal commoning (citizen capital, local juries, perpetual non-resale) with zero open-licensed artifacts. It generalizes the corpus's governance-without-open-assets pattern to its most mature instance and marks the design boundary for any Canadian farmland-commons vehicle (the 2025 BC Farmland Trust white paper explicitly proposes a Terre de Liens-style institution): participation does not produce openness — a separate decision, not yet made by any land trust anywhere, produces openness.

## What it does not demonstrate

That commons ownership requires closed data (no farmland trust anywhere has chosen otherwise, so the counterfactual is untested at scale). Per-farm transparency (absent). The "world's largest farmland commons" framing rests on the network's own counters; the 449-farm headline spans foncière and foundation scopes.
