# Community seed banks and CSBAN (Nepal)

Status: candidate

Region/reach: Nepal; community seed banks across Terai, hill, and mountain regions; national association reach through the Community Seed Banks Association of Nepal (CSBAN)

Project links:
- Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT: https://alliancebioversityciat.org/stories/nepali-community-seedbanks-join-forces-conserve-agrobiodiversity
- LI-BIRD: https://libird.org/
- CSB guideline account: https://seedsforresilience.org/document/community-seed-banks-in-nepal/
- Our Seed / local-variety catalogue reference: https://alliancebioversityciat.org/stories/nepali-community-seedbanks-join-forces-conserve-agrobiodiversity

Problem addressed: loss of local crop varieties, weak local seed supply, limited farmer access to quality seed, and erosion of associated knowledge and farmer rights.

Open layer: community-managed biological commons and seed-system infrastructure. This is not verified as an open-source seed-licensing system. The evidence supports collective management, conservation, exchange, documentation, local seed enterprise, and farmer-rights advocacy.

What is actually open:
- Community seed-bank methods, training materials, and governance knowledge are publicly documented.
- Local seed varieties are conserved and exchanged through community institutions.
- CSBAN represents community seed banks and supports local-variety production and marketing.
- An online local-variety catalogue and the “Our Seed” mobile app are publicly described.
- LI-BIRD and partners have published guidance and research on establishing and managing community seed banks.
- No OSSL/OSSI-style licence, app source repository, catalogue data licence, or formal data-governance terms were located in this pass.

Governance and control: local farming families, farmer groups, and community institutions manage seed banks. Women are documented as managers, entrepreneurs, and seed custodians. CSBAN provides association-level representation. LI-BIRD, Bioversity/CIAT, Oxfam, SeedChange, and development partners provide support and capacity. The balance between community control, NGO support, government recognition, and donor dependence requires further verification.

Evidence of use:
- LI-BIRD reports direct involvement in establishing 23 community seed banks.
- The national association is described as legally registered and representing community seed banks.
- Shivagunj Community Seed Bank reported production of approximately 75 tonnes of seed across varieties and conservation of 115 local varieties of six crop species in a 2024 LI-BIRD account.
- The Kalonuniya local aromatic rice variety was registered at the National Seed Board.
- A 2022 study documents community seed banks entering national seed markets in Nepal and other countries, while stressing managerial, technical, financial, social, and policy constraints.

Maintenance and funding: community seed banks combine seed sales, local participation, project support, and institutional partnerships. The Nepal evidence repeatedly identifies sustainability, local-government ownership, resource mobilization, and institutional development as unresolved challenges. A current national financing picture was not located.

What this case demonstrates:
- A substantive farmer-managed biological and organizational commons exists in Nepal.
- Community seed banks can combine conservation, seed access, farmer rights, agroecology, and local enterprise.
- Openness in biological systems may be expressed through collective custody and circulation rather than a software-style licence.

What it does NOT demonstrate:
- That community seed banks use an open-source seed licence.
- That all conserved varieties, knowledge, or catalogue data are openly reusable.
- That CSBAN has verified current scale, stable financing, or uniform governance across members.
- That the Our Seed app is open source or that farmers control its data.
