# ONDC / Beckn (Open Network for Digital Commerce)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: India (national); protocol usable elsewhere
- Project: https://ondc.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the DPI-for-agriculture cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) aims to move digital commerce away from walled-garden platforms toward an open, interoperable network, so that small sellers, cooperatives, and Farmer Producer Organizations can transact without platform lock-in. It is not an app or intermediary but a set of specifications built on the Beckn protocol, an open, interoperable, decentralised commerce protocol.

## Open layer

Open specifications and an open network protocol: discovery, order booking, payment, delivery, and fulfilment are standardised and unbundled so buyers, sellers, and logistics providers interoperate across applications. Agriculture was activated as a domain in June 2022 with NABARD, explicitly to establish market linkages for Farmer Producer Organizations.

## What is actually open

- ONDC describes itself as using open-source methodologies, open specifications, and an open network protocol; Beckn is the technology and specification layer (developed by Nandan Nilekani, Pramod Varma, and Sujith Nair with ISPIRT; Beckn brand owned by the Open Shared Mobility Foundation).
- Beckn is not e-commerce-specific: it is positioned as a third-layer digital public infrastructure (transaction layer) usable for mobility, healthcare, and other domains.
- The exact state and licences of the protocol specification repositories were not verified in this pass (repository names under ONDC/beckn orgs could not be confirmed via the GitHub API on 2026-08-02).
- Network policy, trust, grievance handling, and reputation are designed by ONDC on top of the Beckn layer — the governance is not fully decentralised.

## Governance and control

ONDC is a not-for-profit company incubated by the Quality Council of India; it sets network policies and charges sellers a fee for network maintenance and development. Control is shared between the network operator (policies) and the open protocol (interoperability). It is not farmer-governed; farmers and FPOs are participants.

## Evidence of use

- Wikipedia documents the launch timeline, NABARD agriculture activation (20 June 2022), rural e-commerce via Common Service Centres, and stated targets (25% of domestic digital commerce; by 2024, 3 crore sellers and 30 crore shoppers).
- IBM's explainer reports ~7 million orders processed, 370,000+ sellers/service providers across 588 cities, with adoption by Flipkart, Paytm, PhonePe, Amazon, SBI, and Meta.
- The 7 million order figure and the 2024 targets are inconsistent orders of magnitude, illustrating the gap between network framing and verified scale; both are third-party or target figures.
- A 2026 academic paper (Dittrich) analyses Beckn as a free and open-source universal resource discovery and transaction protocol.

## Maintenance and funding

Seller fees fund network maintenance and development; the Beckn Foundation supports the protocol. Long-term viability and the balance of power between big adopters and small sellers are contested.

## What this case demonstrates

ONDC/Beckn is the clearest located example of open-protocol commerce infrastructure with an agricultural domain: openness at the protocol and specification layer, with network governance held by a non-profit operator. It tests the project's distinction between open protocols and open ecosystems — interoperability without farmer ownership of the network.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish that the network's openness translates into farmer or small-seller benefit; scale claims are inconsistent.
- The protocol repositories' licences were not verified in this pass.
- It is commerce infrastructure, not farm-data or farm-machinery infrastructure.
- Big-platform adoption (Amazon, Flipkart, Meta) complicates the "against walled gardens" framing.
