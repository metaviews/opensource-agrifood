# Canadian government farm-succession and lease legal documents (federal/provincial state layer)

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: Canada (Ontario, Manitoba, British Columbia)
- Project: OMAFRA Publication 70; Manitoba Agriculture contracts-and-leases; BC Farmland Lease Workbook
- Live docs: see Sources
- Field-guide context: the C3 pass's Canadian state layer — government-authored succession and lease legal material that is free to read but not open
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

Canadian governments publish the country's main non-proprietary farm-succession and lease documents. Whether they grant reuse rights determines whether a Canadian open-legal layer can start from Crown material.

## Open layer

None verified — and one explicit negative. Asset by asset (all checked 2026-11-18 from the downloaded documents themselves, not just web pages):

- **OMAFRA Publication 70, Farm Succession Planning Guide** (120 pages, 2020, updated page 2024-01-16; covers farm business organizations, operating agreements, ownership transfer methods, taxation, wills): the PDF's imprint page states **"© Queen's Printer for Ontario, 2020"** — explicit Crown copyright, no open licence in the document. (ontario.ca site terms were not verified for this document; recorded as unresolved, but the document's own imprint carries plain copyright.)
- **Manitoba Agriculture sample cash lease agreement** (published as an editable `.docx` — the most adaptable format in the Canadian set): **no copyright or licence statement anywhere in the document's text layer** (verified by unzipping the .docx). Manitoba government general terms of use would govern — unverified.
- **BC Farmland Lease Workbook** (assisting landowners and land seekers in preparing a farm land lease): **no licence or copyright statement found in the PDF text layer**. BC publishes much material under the Open Government Licence – British Columbia, but this workbook's own text carries no licence statement — unresolved.

- **Saskatchewan Agriculture "Cash Lease Agreements — Full Version" booklet** (sample forms: consent of non-owning spouse, certificate of acknowledgement, consent to make major improvements): **no copyright or licence statement found in the PDF text layer** (verified by download and search, 2026-11-18).
- Inventoried but licence-unverified this pass: Quebec's MAPAQ multi-part series "Étapes pour réussir un transfert de ferme — Pour la relève" (quebec.ca); PEI's "Step Toward the Future: Farm Succession Series" (princeedwardisland.ca); Nova Scotia's "Taking Stock" workbook and the NSFA 2026 succession survey report.

## What is actually open

Nothing verified as open-licensed across four provinces checked. The `.docx` format of the Manitoba lease is the notable affordance: the province distributes its lease as an editable file, expecting adaptation in practice, while granting no written right to adapt. Saskatchewan's booklet of statutory-form samples is likewise free-to-read and unlicensed.

## Governance and control

Crown/provincial government publication. Control rests with the publishing ministries.

## Evidence of use

These are the standard documents their provinces' farmers use; no usage metrics this pass.

## Maintenance and funding

Government maintenance; Pub 70 page updated 2024-01-16 (published 2020-07-17).

## Why it belongs

It resolves the C3 pass's cheapest verification question — and the answer is negative: **the Canadian state layer is free-to-read, not open.** Even where the format invites adaptation (Manitoba's .docx), no licence grants it. This closes the most plausible "maybe the public sector already did it" escape route from the licensing-gap finding, and it means an open Canadian farm-tenure legal layer must license its own documents rather than reuse Crown ones.

## What it does not demonstrate

That no OGL applies (site-level terms remain unverified for all three documents — recorded as unresolved, not as verified absence); other provinces (Saskatchewan, Quebec, Atlantic) were not audited this pass.
