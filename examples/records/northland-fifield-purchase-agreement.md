# Northland / Fifield Cranberries — agricultural asset purchase agreement (2005)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (Wisconsin; SEC-filed contract exhibit)
- Project: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/818010/000089706905002245/dbk35a.htm

- Field-guide context: the C5 pass's only executed-contract evidence — real drafting showing a farm operation's records excluded from conveyance at sale
- Last checked: 2026-11-19

## Problem addressed

Whether a farm's books and records transfer with the farm by default. Publicly
available executed agricultural purchase agreements are rare; this one is filed.

## Open layer

Public SEC EDGAR exhibit — a real contract, freely readable. The contract itself is
proprietary between the parties; the filing is public record.

## What is actually open (verified verbatim from the exhibit, 2026-11-19)

The exclusion clause: "Seller shall not sell, convey, transfer or assign to Buyer,
and Buyer shall not purchase, acquire or accept, any other property of Seller,
including without limitation any cash, accounts receivable, inventories,
**corporate books and records**, contracts, investments, computers, software..."

## Governance and control

SEC filing; parties commercial.

## Evidence of use

One executed deal (2005, cranberry marsh operation) — documents that records *can*
be excluded from an agricultural asset sale; not a market-norm claim.

## Maintenance and funding

Static SEC record.

## Why it belongs

The succession program's data-continuity layer needed evidence of what actual
contract drafting does with farm records at sale, and this is the only executed
example the pass could locate: exclusion, expressed explicitly. It grounds the
pass's finding that data handover lives (if anywhere) in bespoke drafting, and the
adaptation-commons case for a model records-conveyance clause with continuity as
its default.

## What it does not demonstrate

That exclusion is the norm; any Canadian agreement; treatment of digital/agronomic
data (this is corporate books-and-records language from 2005).
