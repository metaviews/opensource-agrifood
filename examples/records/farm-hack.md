## Problem addressed

Farm Hack is a community-led approach to developing, modifying, and sharing designs for farm tools, machinery, and innovations, using a farmer-to-farmer learning model. It addresses the gap between industrial farm-equipment supply and the tool needs of small-scale and agroecological farms, and the loss of farmer-built tool knowledge.

## Open layer

Community platform and events for sharing open tool designs; not a single project but a commons infrastructure for design sharing.

## What is actually open

Farm Hack combines a web platform (repository of open designs and documentation) with in-person events ("hackathons") where tools are demonstrated, built, and then posted online. The FAO record describes it as emphasizing farmer-to-farmer learning and platforms for collective ingenuity in agroecology-appropriate technologies. Examples documented include FIDO, a farmer-built electronic tool, from the Cornell small-farms community. Design-licence conventions across the repository were not audited for this record.

## What this case demonstrates

Farm Hack represents peer community infrastructure for agricultural tool commons: the event-plus-platform pattern that lets farmer knowledge circulate without a cooperative or company structure. It is the looser, decentralised counterpart to L'Atelier Paysan's structured cooperative model — useful for comparing governance models for open farm hardware.
