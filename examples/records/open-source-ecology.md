# Open Source Ecology (OSE)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (Factor e Farm, Maysville, Missouri); independent OSE Germany chapter
- Project: https://www.opensourceecology.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the open-hardware/robotics/sensing cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

OSE's stated goal is the Global Village Construction Set (GVCS): open-source designs for 50 types of industrial machines that, in the project's framing, are needed to "build a small civilization with modern comforts". Within agriculture this includes the LifeTrac multipurpose open-source tractor, seeder, rototiller, hay rake, baler, and micro-combine, among others. The declared intent is to make machines fabricable at "a fraction of commercial costs" and to share designs freely.

## Open layer

Open machine designs and documentation (GPL / CC-BY-SA terms stated on the LifeTrac project page), with an explicit claim to a replicable, open, social production model.

## What is actually open

Designs and documentation are published on the OSE wiki and Dozuki documentation platform. The LifeTrac project page states "Terms of Use: GPL / CC-BY-SA". Machine designs are described as modular construction sets (bolt-together space frames, interchangeable power units and wheel units). Whether every component design and bill of materials meets a defined open-hardware standard was not audited for this record.

## Governance and control

OSE is a network/NGO led by founder Marcin Jakubowski (founded 2003), operating from Factor e Farm in rural Missouri. It is not farmer-governed: it describes itself as a network of farmers, engineers, architects, and supporters. Wikipedia carries a conflict-of-interest flag on the article (July 2020), which means promotional framing from project sources should be discounted accordingly.

## Evidence of use

- The 2011 TED talk brought the GVCS to wide attention; Jakubowski became a Shuttleworth Foundation Fellow (2012) and TED Senior Fellow (2012).
- Project-reported progress: in 2014, 12 of the 50 machines designed/blueprinted/prototyped, with four reaching documentation stage; in 2018 the project reported 33% completion. The LifeTrac page itself is internally inconsistent: the page text says the project is "currently on the 7th prototype" while the embedded dashboard records "Version v13.9 (Prototype 6), September 2013".
- Independent replication evidence is thin. Public discussion (e.g., Facebook groups) includes farmers and builders asking whether anyone has actually built GVCS machines — suggestive of low demonstrated replication outside the project's own site.

## Maintenance and funding

Funding has come from foundations (Shuttleworth), a 2011 Kickstarter (US$63,573), and donations. OSE Germany exists as an independent effort based on OSE principles. Maintenance of the design corpus and its currency (e.g., LifeTrac documentation dating from 2013-2014) appears partial; documentation freshness was not verified comprehensively.

## What this case demonstrates

OSE is the maximalist version of open-source agriculture hardware: a civilization-scale design commons rather than a farmer tool project. It makes legible the gap between publishing designs and demonstrating replication, and between vision-led claims and audited progress — both central to this project's evidence discipline.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish that GVCS machines are built and used at any scale beyond prototypes at Factor e Farm; independent replication evidence is anecdotal and weak.
- The "fraction of commercial costs" claims are not independently verified.
- The "33% completion" metric's definition and evidence base are unclear.
- It does not establish farmer control or farmer benefit; it is a founder-led NGO network.
- Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest flag (2020) means the project's own claims require independent confirmation.
