# Digger's Mirth Collective Farm

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Burlington, Vermont, USA (Intervale; local markets and wholesale)
- Project: https://diggersmirth.com/
- Live docs: https://diggersmirth.com/
- Field-guide context: worker-owned collective vegetable farm — the farmworker-cooperative ownership model, distinct from the producer cooperatives that dominate the corpus's commons-governance current
- Last checked: 2026-09-07

## Problem addressed

Farm workers in North America are overwhelmingly employees-at-will with no ownership of the enterprises they work in, in a sector with documented wage theft, hazard exposure, and (in Canada) tied visas that prevent changing employers. The farmworker cooperative is the structural alternative: the enterprise is jointly owned and democratically controlled by the people who do the farm work. Digger's Mirth is one of the longest-running US examples of that model in vegetable production.

## Open layer

None at the asset level — and that is the record's analytical point. Digger's Mirth is governance-open (collective ownership, democratic operation, equal hourly wage across roles regardless of task, per farmbilllaw.org 2022 and Civil Eats 2018 profiles) while publishing nothing under an open licence: no open tooling, no open data, no documented open practices. It belongs in this corpus as the anchor case for shape 2 of the labour scan: **ownership without tooling** — the worker-ownership answer exists, but the open-technology layer has not built for it, and the farm has not built with open technology either.

## What is actually open

- The farm's own description of its model (collectively owned and operated, 15 acres of organic vegetables in Burlington's Intervale, hiring for the 2026 season — active as of 2026-09-07).
- Third-party documentation of the equal-wage practice (farmbilllaw.org, 2022) and of the collective-farming model generally (Civil Eats, 2018; Farmland Access Legal Toolkit's collaborative-farming resources).

## Governance and control

Worker collective: the people who work the farm own and operate it. Distinct from a producer cooperative (where member farmers remain individual business owners) and from a CSA (member eaters). The corpus's Current 4 commons-governance records (OFN, CoopCycle, SAOS, JoinData) are producer-, courier-, or member-governed; Digger's Mirth represents the missing member class — labour itself as the governing body.

## Evidence of use

- Operating farm in continuous production since 1990s Burlington Intervale (verified live site, 2026 season hiring complete).
- Cited as the model case in Vermont employee-ownership coverage (Burlington Free Press, 2019) and farmworker-cooperative legal literature (Farm Bill Law Enterprise, 2022).
- Survived the 2023 Vermont floods with community fundraising ($68,000 GoFundMe, VTDigger 2023) — evidence of embeddedness, not of model durability under capital stress.

## Maintenance and funding

Market-and-wholesale farm revenue; conventional small-farm economics. No open-source funding question applies (no open assets). The relevant fragility is the collective's own succession and capital position — undocumented in open sources.

## Why it belongs

The labour scan's central claim is that open agrifood has been built around the farm as an enterprise, not the farm as a workplace. Digger's Mirth is the counter-anchoring case: the workplace owns the enterprise. Reading it against the corpus's tooling records (farmOS, OFN, PickApp as the closed contrast) makes visible what the open-technology layer has never produced — governance tooling for worker-owned farms — and gives the "who captures the value" question its most direct answer: the workers do.

## What it does not demonstrate

No open-source, open-data, or open-hardware practice. No evidence the collective model scales beyond small vegetable operations or survives ownership transition. It is a single US case; no Canadian farmworker cooperative was located in the labour scan (an honest absence for the corpus's home-jurisdiction convention).
