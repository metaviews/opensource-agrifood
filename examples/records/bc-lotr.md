# BC Land Owner Transparency Registry (LOTR)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: British Columbia, Canada (first and only registry of its kind in Canada)
- Project: https://www.gov.bc.ca/landownertransparency
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the land sectoral scan (G-OSA-35, `research/2026-09-land-sectoral-scan.md` sub-cell 1); verified 2026-09-04

## Problem addressed

Land held through corporations, trusts, and partnerships hides the actual owners behind legal intermediaries — the mechanism by which farmland and real estate pass into anonymous hands. LOTR requires those structures to disclose their beneficial owners to a public registry, making "who controls this land" answerable for BC land held through intermediaries.

## Open layer

Public registry transparency — individual public search, deliberately **not** bulk open data. This is a different open form from any dataset record in the corpus: the openness is a legal right of public access to named records, not a licence over a corpus.

## What is actually open — and what is not

- **Public search** (since 2021-04-30; free since 2024-04-01, previously $5.25/record): search by person name or parcel identifier (PID) only — s.35 LOTA bars bulk access.
- **Disclosed publicly**: "primary identification information" — names, and for individuals name plus birth month/year (not full birthdate, SIN, or home address).
- **Exempted from public view**: personal identifiers; individuals under 19 or incapable; holders registered ≤90 days; and anyone granted a s.40 safety/health omission (435 omission applications received Nov 2020-Jul 2025, with a 90-day notice window before information becomes public).
- **Scope limits**: some corporations/trusts excluded (Schedule 1; 2023 amendments added e.g. corporations wholly owned by Indigenous nations); LOTA does not apply to Indigenous land (s.6).

## Governance and control

Administered by the Land Title and Survey Authority of BC — a **publicly accountable statutory corporation** (not a Crown corporation) created 2005, self-funded through regulated fees under an Operating Agreement with the Province. Enforcement sits with the Province: penalties up to the greater of $25,000 (individual)/$50,000 (corporate) or 5% of assessed property value.

## Evidence of use and of opacity

Scale (dated benchmark, BC Gov News 2024-02-16): **553,068 transparency declarations** covering 553,234 properties, and 137,068 transparency reports, as of 2023-12-21. (LTSA's current page cites lower cumulative figures — 388,325/77,884 — an unresolved discrepancy between provincial and LTSA sources.) Use: 109,414 searches in FY2024/25. Enforcement is the registry's soft spot: the Ministry of Finance has declined to disclose whether any penalties were ever issued; critics state zero known penalties paid despite ~$11M/yr in filing fees. "World's first such registry" appears only in secondary media; official sources say "first-of-its-kind in Canada."

## Why it belongs

The Canadian anchor record of the land cell: the one place in Canada where beneficial farmland ownership through intermediaries is publicly visible — and a demonstration of both the power and the ceiling of registry transparency. It exists because the Province chose to create it (post-Cullen Commission), it is searchable rather than bulk-open, its exemptions and unverified enforcement carve out the margins, and no other province has followed. It is the counter-factual to the scan's finding that "who controls Canadian farmland" is unanswerable from public data — a partial answer, existing by legislative decision in exactly one jurisdiction.

## What it does not demonstrate

Bulk or machine-readable openness; nationwide coverage; effective enforcement (undisclosed); ownership visibility for individually held land (only intermediary-held land is captured).
