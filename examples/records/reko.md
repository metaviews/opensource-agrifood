# REKO

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Finland-origin (Pietarsaari, 2013) — "There are over 160 REKO rings in Finland at the moment" (Interreg Europe, undated); reported spread across Scandinavia and, by one 2021 snapshot, 14 countries
- Project: REKO rings — producer-consumer direct trade through closed Facebook groups: producers post offers, consumers comment to pre-order, payment arranged directly, goods collected at a shared local point weekly or biweekly. No legal entity, no organization, no software, no licence
- Field-guide context: opened via the grocery concept's Finnish co-op ecosystem survey (`grocery/research/finnish-co-op-survey.md`, 2026-11-25); load-bearing quotes re-verified at source same day
- Record type: commons-without-infrastructure record — curated with the documented tooling refusal as the finding, in the same "absence as finding" family as the Arctic Co-operatives governance record

## Problem addressed

Livelihoods for small-scale and organic Finnish farmers without middlemen or sales-channel infrastructure. "The REKO model was initially conceived by Thomas Snellman, an organic farmer, who was distressed at the livelihood prospects for small-scale, organic farmers in Finland" (Hagolani-Albov & Ehrnström-Fuentes, University of Helsinki / Helda). Launched 2013 in Swedish-speaking Ostrobothnia, inspired by the French AMAP, under the EkoNu development project.

## The open layer — and the honest finding

**A copyable social model on closed infrastructure — no artifact, by choice.**

- The mechanism is documented as infrastructure-free by design: "REKO is a situated example of how digital interaction can radically alter the producer/consumer interactions in a local food community without having to invest heavily in infrastructures" (Helda, CC BY-NC).
- The platform layer is closed and the dependence is acknowledged in the model's own literature: "because there is no formal cooperation between Facebook and REKO, there is always the possibility that Facebook will alter the features or set-up without warning" (Helda, CC BY-NC).
- The instructive absence is documented, not inferred: "the founder of REKO, Thomas Snellman, was contacted frequently by both large and small IT firms who wanted to design and develop a platform that would work according to REKO needs" — and declined. The same paper names the attempted alternatives (REKOrder; Sweden's Local Food Nodes) as marginal or non-open.
- Replication is purely social — no licence, no franchise, no organization to copy from. Scale snapshots (recorded unreconciled; no registry exists): first ring 2013 with 7 farmers and 39 consumers; "In 2021 there are 600 rings in Scandinavia and beyond in a total of 2 million members in 14 countries!" (COCOREADO roadmap, 2021); "over 160 REKO rings in Finland" (Interreg, undated).

## Why it belongs

1. It is the collection's complement to Foodsharing.de: where that record shows a food commons that *built and licensed* its own software, REKO shows a food commons that scaled internationally while *deliberately refusing* shared software — openness of the model, closedness of the infrastructure, documented as a founder's choice.
2. It prices the trade: zero infrastructure cost and zero governance overhead, paid for in platform risk on proprietary, unaccountable infrastructure. The corpus's software-stack finding (governance tools mature, data layer building, participation/culture layers unbuilt) gets here its natural experiment — the layers carried entirely by a closed platform.
3. It is the Finnish-origin ancestor of the direct-trade ring model now present across the Nordics and reported in 14 countries — a geography and mechanism the collection did not otherwise cover.

## What it does not demonstrate

- Any open artifact — there is none; the record is curated with the refusal as the finding, not a positive open layer.
- Durable scale — the ring counts are single-source snapshots from different years; the network is informal and no registry exists to verify against.
- Sustainability — the Facebook-dependence risk documented in the model's own academic literature is unresolved; nothing here shows the model surviving a platform change.
