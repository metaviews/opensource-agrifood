# Common European Agricultural Data Space (CEADS / AgriDataSpace / FSDN)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: European Union (supranational)
- Project: https://ceads.eu/ ; preparatory action: https://agridataspace-csa.eu/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the DPI-for-agriculture cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

The EU is building a Common European Agricultural Data Space (CEADS) to facilitate secure, trusted, transparent data sharing, processing, and analysis in agriculture, under the European Strategy for Data. The stated goals: accelerate the twin digital and green transitions, reduce administrative burdens, and provide a foundation for agricultural AI. A companion instrument, the Farm Sustainability Data Network (FSDN), replaces the FADN as the EU's harmonised source of farm microeconomic data for CAP impact assessment.

## Open layer

Federated data-space infrastructure: an interoperability and governance layer (FAIR principles, common agricultural ontologies, alignment with INSPIRE and Copernicus standards) rather than a single platform. The openness is architectural and regulatory — data spaces, standards, and access agreements — under GDPR-compliant governance.

## What is actually open

- The AgriDataSpace preparatory action (Digital Europe Programme, €2 million, 15 partners in 10 countries: research institutes, agricultural data intermediaries, industry associations) concluded and delivered a September 2024 policy brief proposing governance, business models, and a technical reference architecture; a follow-up deployment action was launched in early 2025.
- A governance analysis (MDPI, 2026) reports: federated architecture; multi-actor governance with public authorities, research, industry, and farmers; GDPR-compliant data access agreements; farmers' organisations included in governance bodies "primarily in advisory capacity", with institutional actors (public authorities, research) holding a structural majority; no direct farmer-level revenue model; sustainability via data-quality and interoperability services.
- FSDN is an EC statistical infrastructure replacing FADN; its data obligations on farmers are regulatory in nature.

## Governance and control

Supranational, multi-actor: the Commission and member states provide public oversight; the structural majority sits with institutional actors, with farmer organisations advisory. The data space is designed to connect CAP administrative data, public registers, earth observation, meteorological, and private platform data.

## Evidence of use

- EC policy brief (23 September 2024) documents the preparatory action's conclusions and the deployment follow-up.
- The MDPI governance analysis (2026) provides a structured, cited assessment of governance and funding.
- A farm-centred critique exists ("Agri-food data spaces: highlighting the need for a farm-centered strategy", ScienceDirect perspective), contesting the institutional orientation.
- Actual data-space operations and farmer-level participation post-deployment are unverified in this pass.

## Maintenance and funding

Digital Europe and Horizon Europe funded; long-term sustainability is planned via data-quality and interoperability services rather than a farmer-level revenue model — a recognised open question.

## What this case demonstrates

CEADS is the clearest located example of supranational agricultural data infrastructure in the making, and a direct instance of the project's regulatory-layer question: data spaces where the openness is architectural (standards, federated governance) while farmer organisations hold advisory rather than structural power. It complements AgriStack (state stack) and WAGRI (state API platform) as the third governance form: federated data space.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish operational data sharing or farmer-level participation post-deployment.
- Farmer organisations are advisory, not structural — this is the contested governance finding.
- The sustainability model beyond programme funding is not established.
- It is not open-source software per se; it is a data-space governance and interoperability programme.
