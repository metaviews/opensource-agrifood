# Open Data Kit (ODK)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: global; documented use in resource-limited and African field research
- Project: https://getodk.org/
- Research case: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5786425/
- Field-guide context: `units/open-data-kit-africa-agritech.md`

## Problem addressed

ODK supports data collection where paper workflows, weak connectivity, field logistics, and multilingual or complex forms make conventional systems difficult.

## Open layer

Open-source software, mobile and web forms, offline field infrastructure, and integrations.

## What is actually open

ODK provides open-source tools for forms, photos, GPS, skip logic, multiple languages, offline use, synchronization, and data export. ODK Cloud is a paid hosted service; self-hosting and self-support are available through the open-source stack.

## Governance and control

ODK gives implementing organisations technical control over collection and hosting. The governance of collected data depends on the deploying organisation and is not guaranteed by the software licence.

## Evidence of use

The official site reports more than 2 million users and more than 250 million submissions annually. A peer-reviewed 2017 study documents ODK use in a household survey in Nigeria’s Niger Delta. The field guide identifies additional agrifood leads that still require individual source checks.

## Maintenance and funding

The project combines open-source software with a commercial hosted service and community infrastructure. This makes it a useful case for examining how open projects fund maintenance without making the core software proprietary.

## Why it belongs

ODK is durable enabling infrastructure: it shows how open field tools can support agricultural research, monitoring, extension, and data collection without being an agrifood application themselves.

## What it does not demonstrate

ODK’s open software does not guarantee farmer control, responsible data governance, agrifood impact, or equivalent outcomes across deployments.
