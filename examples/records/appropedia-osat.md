# Appropedia / Open Source Appropriate Technology (OSAT)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: global; designed for the developing world / Global South
- Project: https://www.appropedia.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the Global South farm-level open hardware cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

Appropedia is a sustainability wiki and the de facto open-design commons for appropriate technology (AT): technologies designed with explicit attention to the environmental, ethical, cultural, social, political, and economic context of the communities they serve — including agricultural tools, water pumps, solar dryers, and processing equipment for smallholder farmers. The open source appropriate technology (OSAT) current argues these designs should be shared as free and open designs so that communities can build, adapt, and repair them without patent lock-in.

## Open layer

Open design commons: wiki content under a share-alike licence (CC BY-SA), hosting designs, build instructions, deployment logs, and literature for appropriate technologies oriented to the developing world.

## What is actually open

- Appropedia hosts the OSAT concept page (created 2007, edited through 2025), the agricultural-tools category (including the FAO "Farm Implements for Arid and Tropical Regions" design principles), and a large body of tool designs and how-tos.
- The OSAT academic current is substantial: Pearce, "The case for open source appropriate technology" (2012, cited 308 times); "3-D Printing of Open Source Appropriate Technologies" (2010, cited 393); Buitenhuis et al., "Open Design-Based Strategies" (2010, cited 77). The OSAT paradigm is documented as enabling production as well as consumption, localization, and gratis-and-libre access.
- The wiki's licence is share-alike; individual pages and design files should be checked per asset. No verification standard exists for whether a posted design has been built or works.

## Governance and control

Community-governed wiki, with academic anchoring (Queens Applied Sustainability Group / MOST group and the broader OSAT literature). It is not farmer-governed, but it is explicitly oriented to developing-world communities.

## Evidence of use

- The agricultural-tools page (2007-2025) documents appropriate-tool principles and the caveats of the approach: land concentration determines whether tool design or land reform matters more; in small-farm Asia and Latin America, mechanization may reduce labour rather than increase yield.
- The literature claims open designs enable adaptation and lower costs; deployment logs on the wiki exist but are uneven.
- No audit of how many Global South communities have actually built Appropedia designs was located; the wiki is a commons of designs, not a tracker of builds.

## Maintenance and funding

Wiki infrastructure maintained by the Appropedia community; content is volunteer- and bot-maintained (e.g., StandardWikitext bot edits). No commercial funding model documented in the sources read.

## What this case demonstrates

Appropedia/OSAT is the platform layer of Global South appropriate-technology openness: a share-alike commons where smallholder tool designs are supposed to circulate freely. It is the design-commons counterpart to Farm Hack and L'Atelier Paysan, but with wiki-level (not cooperative-level) governance and no build-verification standard.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish that posted designs are built or deployed at scale; the wiki is not a build tracker.
- Individual pages' licences and design-file quality vary; no per-asset audit was performed.
- It is not farmer-governed, and it does not resolve the land-concentration caveat its own agricultural-tools page names.
- The OSAT "end to IP lock-in" claim is a paradigm claim, not a demonstrated outcome.
