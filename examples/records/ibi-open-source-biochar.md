# IBI open-source biochar technologies (aggregator)

Status: curated
Region/reach: Global aggregator; hosted by International Biochar Initiative; individual designs span US, Australia, and other regions
Project links:
- IBI "Open Source Biochar Technologies" page: https://biochar-international.org/about-biochar/how-to-make-biochar/open-source-biochar-technologies/
- IBI open-source pyrolysis plant guidelines (PDF, 2024): https://biochar-international.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/IBI_Pyrolysis_Plant_Guidelines.pdf

Problem addressed: aggregate and make accessible open-source and open-access biochar production designs, guidelines, and methods — from backyard kilns to small pyrolysis plants — to support the development and testing of small-scale biochar production equipment.

Open layer: open-access aggregation of multiple designs and guidelines — the IBI page is a curated collection of downloadable design documents, papers, and guides. The page itself is an aggregator, not a single licenced hardware project. Individual designs carry different provenance and licensing.

What is actually open (asset by asset, licences verified):
- IBI "Open Source Biochar Technologies" page (2024 refresh): curated list of open-source and open-access biochar designs and resources, including:
  - Wilson Biochar: Top-feed Open Draft (TFOD) kilns, with diagrams and photos (Kelpie Wilson)
  - 1G Toucan TLUD for Biochar Production (Dr. Hugh McLaughlin, PDF download)
  - "Jolly Roger Ovens" family of biochar-making devices (Dr. Hugh McLaughlin, PDF download)
  - Small Scale Biochar Production (Dr. David Domermuth, Appalachian State, PDF paper)
  - How to make high and low adsorption biochars for small research studies (Dr. Hugh McLaughlin, PDF)
  - Biochar Oven developed at Star Seed Gardens Nursery Bootcamp 2012 (Stephen Joseph, Paul Taylor, et al., PDF)
  - Liberty Biofuel Products biochar processor (Ed Cahoj, switchgrass gasifier designed for biochar, PDF)
  - Backyard biochar kiln instructions (Kelpie Wilson adaptation from Sai Bhaskar Reddy Nakka + Twin Oaks Forge, PDF)
- IBI "Guidelines for Development and Testing of Pyrolysis Plants" (32-page PDF, 2024): open-source guide for engineers and system designers, produced to assist development and testing of small pyrolysis plants; authors: Prof. GX Pan, Dr Q Ding, Prof. S Joseph, Prof. LQ Li, Prof. F C Christo. Also published as "Commissioning of an Open Source Twin Trough Pyrolyzer."
- BioEnergy Discussion Lists (stove designers community, open-source environment) — linked as a community resource.
- CC licensing guidance: the page notes that inventors who do not pursue patents may consider Creative Commons licences as an alternative to copyright law for open design.

Licensing: mixed and informal. The page aggregates designs that are "open source" or "open access" by various means — some are PDF downloads with implied open use, some carry CC or public-domain framing, some are designer-published. There is no single licenced commons here. The "open source" characterisation is real at the level of individual designs being openly accessible and reusable, but the aggregator page itself is not a licenced commons — it is a curated open-access collection. This matches the corpus discipline "collection ≠ commons" and "openly published ≠ open-licensed."

Governance and control: IBI is the host/aggregator (industry/standards body). Individual designs have their own originators (Wilson, McLaughlin, Domermuth, Joseph/Taylor, Cahoj, Wilson/Nakka). No single governance structure for the aggregated layer — each design remains with its originator; the page provides visibility and access.

Evidence of use:
- The individual designs have varied build evidence — Wilson TFOD kilns are widely used in backyard biochar circles; McLaughlin TLUDs and JROs have build communities; the Starseed oven and Liberty processor have documented build photos.
- The IBI guidelines doc (2024) is recent and intended for active use by engineers and system designers developing small pyrolysis plants.
- The Kon-Tiki case (separate record) provides the strongest documented spread evidence in the broader biochar open layer; this aggregator page is the breadth layer, not the deep-evidence layer.

Maintenance and funding: IBI hosts and maintains the page; individual design documents are maintained by their originators (some are dated — McLaughlin/JRO 2012, Starseed 2012 — others are recent — IBI guidelines 2024). No single maintenance stream for the aggregate.

What this case demonstrates:
- A curated open-access aggregator for biochar hardware designs exists and is actively maintained (IBI page, 2024 refresh) — the breadth of the open layer is real and documented.
- Multiple open-source/open-access biochar kiln designs exist beyond Kon-Tiki — the open layer is not a single design but a family (TFOD, TLUD, retort, JRO, oven, gasifier).

What it does NOT demonstrate:
- That the aggregated designs form a single licenced open-source hardware commons — they are an open-access collection with mixed provenance and licensing; the "open source" is real per design but not unified.
- That every design on the page has verified build evidence — some are dated (2012) with build photos but no systematic replication record.
- That the aggregator provides quality control or interoperability — it is a listing, not a standards body.
