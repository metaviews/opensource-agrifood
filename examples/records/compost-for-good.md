# Compost for Good rotating drum composter

Status: curated
Region/reach: United States (New York, Adirondack region); community-scale, local reach
Project links:
- Project page: https://www.adkaction.org/project/compost-for-good/ (now under Adirondack North Country Association / ANCA, 2024)
- Design guide (PDF, 2022): https://www.adkaction.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/design_guide_2021.pdf
- Operating manual (PDF, 2021): https://www.adkaction.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/manual_2021_web.pdf
- YouTube channel: https://www.youtube.com/@CompostForGood

Problem addressed: community and institutional-scale food-waste composting at a cost below commercial equipment. The project designed and built a rotating drum composter to process unsorted food waste for a ~250-person community (roughly 35,000 lb/year), at a material cost of approximately $15,000 — comparable commercial units range from $40,000 to $150,000.

Open layer: free, open-source hardware design (stated) — design guide and operating manual published as free PDFs; the project explicitly states the design is "available to everyone" and "provides enough detail so that others may build their own composter."

What is actually open (asset by asset, licences verified):
- Design guide (2022 PDF): free download; project describes it as "free, open-source community-scale composter design." No OSHWA certification located. No specific open-source hardware licence (CERN OHL, Solderpad, etc.) located on the project page — the "open source" is declared in project language, not formalised in a hardware licence file.
- Operating manual (2021 PDF): free download; includes general composting guidance plus specifics from operating the drum composter since early 2017. Intended to be supplemented by in-person consultation.
- Educational materials: town-hall presentations, marketing templates, grant-writing support — knowledge/advocacy, not hardware design.
- YouTube videos: operational documentation.

Governance and control: non-profit project (AdkAction, now supported by ANCA since 2024). Not a commons governance structure — it is an advocacy/technical-assistance project publishing designs. Data-ownership claims not relevant (no data platform).

Evidence of use:
- The original drum composter has operated since early 2017 in the Adirondack region, processing community food waste.
- Compost for Good opened a Human Urine Research and Demonstration (HURD) facility in September 2024 — urine-compost integration as a new frontier.
- Replication outside the original project: NOT VERIFIED. No located build report of an independent Compost for Good drum build from the design guide. The design guide's language ("further modifications can be made to reduce material and labor costs") implies an expectation of iteration, but no systematic build tracking exists in the public record. This is the single biggest residual for this record.

Maintenance and funding: non-profit / advocacy funding (AdkAction, ANCA, regional partnerships, Clarkson University collaboration announced 2024). Not a commercial equipment vendor.

What this case demonstrates:
- A community-scale, designed, documented composter can be built for roughly one-third to one-tenth the cost of commercial equivalents — the affordability claim is evidenced by the project's own cost accounting and the commercial price range it cites.
- Free open-source design documentation (design guide + operating manual) is a real and usable artefact — the document is downloadable and detailed.
- Community-scale in-vessel rotating drum composting is technically feasible and operable over multi-year periods (operating since 2017).
- A non-profit advocacy project can carry an open-source hardware design without a formal OSHWA/licence framework — the design is openly accessible and reusable in practice, but the licence-formality gap is real.

What it does NOT demonstrate:
- That the design has been replicated by independent builders — no verified build reports located.
- That the design has an open-source hardware licence in the OSHWA/CERN OHL sense — the "open source" claim is project-declared, not licence-verified.
- That the design scales beyond community scale (250-person, 35K lb/yr) — the sizing is specific to that scale.
- That the design is the only or best open composter design — this is one case in a thin layer, not a comprehensive solution.
- That on-farm (rather than community/institutional) composting is covered — the design is food-waste-focused, not farm-production-focused.
