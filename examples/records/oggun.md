# Think Oggún (Oggún tractor)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (designed in Alabama) / designed for Cuba; distributors reported in four countries
- Project: https://ronnietractors.com/ (Think Oggún); history: https://ronnietractors.com/history
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the Global South open-hardware cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

Smallholder farmers — initially Cuba's — need tractors that are affordable, simple, and repairable in the field. The project's stated Cuba rationale: the country imports roughly 80% of its food, has returned land in roughly 40-acre tracts to 300,000 family farms, and has only about 60,000 tractors, most over 30 years old. The design brief required trust (open design, provided to the Cuban government), off-the-shelf parts, field repairability, affordability, and the ability to do 100% of fabrication and assembly in country.

## Open layer

Claimed "Open System Design / Open System Manufacturing" model: a tractor built from off-the-shelf components, designed to be manufactured in-country rather than imported.

## What is actually open

- No public design repository was located (no Oggún/ThinkOggún GitHub presence; design files were not found publicly).
- The project's own primary source qualifies the openness: once a country reaches 100% local manufacture, it pays CleBer a technology licence fee for five years. The model is "not one of selling a tractor, but enabling them to manufacture a tractor", with distributors in four countries.
- This is the record's contested core: "open system" functions as a trust and transfer mechanism (designs shared with governments and licensees), not as open licensing in the OSHWA/Open Source Initiative sense. It is closer to a paid manufacturing-transfer licence than to a free-to-redistribute design commons.

## Governance and control

Private LLC (CleBer = Clemmons & Berenthal): Horace Clemmons (software-industry standards pioneer) and Saul Berenthal (Cuban-born software entrepreneur). Manufacturing is by Ronnie Baugh Tractors (Alabama). Control sits with the company; the Cuban farmer framing ("This is not a tractor, this is a way of thinking") is invoked as the project's philosophy.

## Evidence of use

- Primary source (Think Oggún history page) documents the design rationale, the Cuba origin story (US-Cuba opening, December 2014), the four-country distributor claim, and the licence-fee model.
- Trade and press coverage: growingproduce.com ("New Tractor For The Small Grower On The Horizon"); Greenhorns ("incredible American-made, open source, radically accessible"); Granma (Cuban state newspaper, 2016) on CleBer as an engine of change between Cuba and the US.
- Civil Eats (2022) documents actual US customers: Stone Barns Center (Jack Algiere) and Hudson Valley Seed Company (Steven Crist), who value the adaptable cultivar toolbar.
- The US$13,500 price is a project claim. Cuban deployment is not independently verified.

## Maintenance and funding

Small-company model; current operational status, distributor network health, and ongoing maintenance support are unverified in this pass. The 5-year licence-fee model is the stated sustainability mechanism.

## What this case demonstrates

Oggún is the strongest located Global South-oriented open-manufacturing tractor case, and the clearest instance of "open-washing" ambiguity in this cycle: an "open system" that is a paid licensing and transfer model rather than a free design commons. It tests the project's asset-by-asset openness discipline directly.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish that designs are openly licensed or freely redistributable; no public design files were located.
- It does not establish Cuban manufacture or deployment; embargo and logistics dynamics are unverified.
- The four-country distributor claim and the price are project-reported.
- Current activity and maintenance are unverified.
