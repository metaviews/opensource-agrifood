# SMART (Spatial Monitoring and Reporting Tool)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: global (60+ countries; built and maintained by a partnership of WWF, WCS, ZSL, Frankfurt Zoological Society, North Carolina Zoo, Panthera, Re:wild and others)
- Project: open-source software suite for conservation monitoring — patrol management, wildlife observation, threat assessment
- Field-guide context: opened via the A5 northern/remote food systems scan (G-OSA-39) as the software layer under Nunavut's CBMN; verified 2026-09-09
- Record type: software record — enabling infrastructure outside agrifood, curated for its documented role in a Canadian Indigenous food-governance system

## Problem addressed

Community monitors and enforcement patrols need standardized, analyzable records of what is seen, harvested, and threatened in the field — collected offline, synced intermittently, and usable by non-specialists. Commercial conservation-data platforms exist; most communities cannot buy them.

## Open layer

SMART's own materials (verified 2026-09-09): **"SMART Tools are open-source, non-proprietary, and freely available."** Desktop application plus mobile apps and plugins (R statistical plugin; CyberTracker integration for field capture); designed for connected and *disconnected* sites. Honest caveat, recorded: the project distributes builds from its own site and no public source-code repository was located at verification time — the "open-source" claim is the partnership's, the licence text was not independently read. Treated as open by claim and long third-party documentation (opensource.com 2013 profile onward), flagged unverified at the licence-text level, consistent with house verification practice.

## The northern agrifood connection (why it earns a record in this corpus)

Nunavut's Community-Based Monitoring Network — the data infrastructure of the country-food system's wildlife co-management — runs on SMART (SMART Community case study, NWMB program). A suite built for African anti-poaching patrols became the data layer for Inuit harvesting governance in Canada. That direction of transfer — Global South open source serving Canadian Indigenous food systems — is the finding the corpus had not previously documented.

## What it does not demonstrate

- Licence-text verification (no public repo found; claim taken from project materials).
- Any direct food-production use: SMART's agrifood relevance is entirely via the northern monitoring/governance chain, not farm software.
- Sustainability model: the SMART Partnership is institutional (big-NGO funded); its maintenance funding profile is closer to the "institutional stewardship" archetype than a community commons.
