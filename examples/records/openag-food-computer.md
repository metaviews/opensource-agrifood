# MIT Open Agriculture Initiative (OpenAg) — Food Computer

- Status: `candidate` (contested — see contested-claims note)
- Region / reach: United States (MIT Media Lab, Cambridge MA); global maker/education reach claimed
- Project (archived): https://github.com/OpenAgricultureFoundation/
- Field-guide context: the field guide's autonomous-greenhouse research layer (Tencent × WUR Autonomous Greenhouse Challenge, `units/chinese-hyperscaler-agritech-substrate.md`) is the constructive successor; OpenAg is the cautionary counterpart
- Last checked: 2026-08-06

## Problem addressed

OpenAg (founded 2015 by Caleb Harper at the MIT Media Lab) aimed to build open-source controlled-environment agriculture platforms ("Food Computers" — tabletop Personal Food Computer, container-sized Food Server, warehouse Food Data Center) for experimental, educational, and personal growing, with openly shared "climate recipes" (phenome data linking climate conditions to plant traits).

## Open layer

By design, all hardware, software, and data were to be open source — a standardized open platform for agricultural research. This is the corpus's most ambitious claimed open-CEA stack.

## What is actually open

- `OpenAgricultureFoundation/openag_brain` — GPL-3.0, but **archived** (last push 2018-09-19).
- `OpenAgricultureFoundation/gro-hardware` — **no clear licence detected** (license field empty), **archived** (last push 2019-01-04). The hardware designs — the physical "Food Computer" — carry no verifiable open licence.
- The `openag` org holds only unrelated infrastructure repos (MIT, Apache-2.0, archived/low-activity); no maintained CEA codebase.
- "Climate recipe" database: announced, not confirmed as a durable open resource.

## Governance and control

MIT Media Lab project; closed April 2020 with Harper's departure (officially confirmed May 2020). No foundation, cooperative, or successor maintains the assets.

## Evidence of use

- As of 2016: six prototype Personal Food Computers in Boston-area schools; three Food Servers at MIT, Michigan State, and Cinvestav (Mexico). Never commercially available.
- **Contested / negative evidence:** OpenAg drew sustained criticism that early results were exaggerated or fabricated — staff described buying potted plants and presenting them as Food-Computer-grown; Gizmodo called it a "Theranos for plants"; IEEE Spectrum reported use of Syrian refugees to tout non-functional units. The closure followed the MIT Media Lab / Epstein funding scandal (Joichi Ito, a key funder, resigned).

## Maintenance and funding

Defunct since 2020. Funded via MIT Media Lab (Ito-era). No maintenance, no successor, no open governance.

## Why it belongs

OpenAg is the corpus's reference case for the **gap between open-source aspiration and open-source reality** in CEA: a flagship "everything open" claim that ended archived, licence-unresolved on hardware, and publicly discredited on results. It is essential honest context for G-OSA-22 — it shows why "would have been open source" is not an open layer.

## What it does not demonstrate

OpenAg does not demonstrate a maintained, licensed, verifiable open CEA stack. Its open claims are aspirational; the durable artifacts are archived code (one repo GPL-3.0, hardware licence-unstated) and a discredited results record.

## Contested claims

- **Asserter:** OpenAg stated all hardware, software, and data would be open source / a standardized open platform.
- **Contester:** NYT (2019), Business Insider, Chronicle of Higher Education, IEEE Spectrum, ProPublica (2019-2020) — reported exaggerated/fabricated results and closed amid scandal.
- **Status:** contested; unresolved in OpenAg's favor. Recorded as candidate with the contested finding carried explicitly.
