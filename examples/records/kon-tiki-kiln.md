# Kon-Tiki flame-curtain biochar kiln

Status: curated
Region/reach: Designed in Switzerland (2014); open technology transfer to Nepal and other regions; global farmer reach via open transfer model
Project links:
- Open Source Ecology wiki: https://wiki.opensourceecology.org/wiki/Kon-Tiki_Kiln
- Biochar Journal article (Schmidt & Taylor, 2020): https://www.biochar-journal.org/en/ct/39
- PLOS ONE paper (Trials et al., 2015): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4871524/
- Ithaka Institut (anchoring organisation): http://www.ithaka-institut.org/en/

Problem addressed: affordable, clean, farmer-scale biochar production. Industrial biochar is expensive; the goal was to enable small farmers and communities to produce high-quality biochar from their own farm and garden residues using simple, low-cost equipment — closing the agricultural resource loop on-farm.

Open layer: open-source biochar hardware by publication and practice — the design was published open source in the Biochar Journal (2020), documented in a PLOS ONE peer-reviewed paper (2015), and hosted on the Open Source Ecology machine-design-commons wiki. Ithaka Institut promotes open technology transfer to farmers. The design is an open-topped conical steel kiln ("flame curtain" principle).

What is actually open (asset by asset, licences verified):
- Design description and specifications: published in the Biochar Journal article (open-access, downloadable PDF), on the OSE wiki (community-edited page with specs, images, design drawings), and in the PLOS ONE paper (open-access research). The "flame curtain" operating principle (light from above, char below, oxygen excluded by the flame) is fully described.
- Specifications: production capacity ~700 L biochar in 4-5 hours; yield 25-30% dry matter; flame-surface temperature ~620-660°C; 30-50 cm into the flame zone ~750°C. Flat-pack 1000-L version documented.
- PLOS ONE paper: open-access peer-reviewed evidence on emissions (35% CO reduction, 36% methane reduction vs open burning on average) and char quality (low volatiles/tars, clean char).
- OSE wiki page: community-hosted design commons page, with images, specifications, related pages (Earth Pit Kiln, Biochar Crusher, Biochar Economy).
- Ithaka Institut open technology transfer: design was developed for a Terra Preta project in Nepal and spread to farmers via open transfer — documented in the Biochar Journal article and PLOS ONE paper.
- Formal hardware licence: NOT LOCATED. The OSE wiki page does not carry a CERN OHL or other hardware licence statement on the Kon-Tiki design itself; the Biochar Journal article is open-access but is an article, not a hardware licence. The "open source" character is real and well-documented in publication + practice + transferability, but a formal hardware licence file on the design was not located in this pass. This is a licence-formality gap, not an openness absence.

Governance and control: design originates with Hans-Peter Schmidt and Paul Taylor (Ithaka Institut, Switzerland). Ithaka Institut is the anchor for development and transfer. OSE wiki hosts the design as part of its machine design commons (GPL / CC-BY-SA community). The open transfer model is Ithaka-driven. Not a farmer-governed commons — the design is open-access and transferable, but governance remains with the originators / hosting platforms.

Evidence of use:
- Original development for Nepal Terra Preta project (mountain farmers, low-tech context) — the design was driven by a real Global South deployment need.
- Documented spread: PLOS ONE paper and Biochar Journal article document open technology transfer to farmers in Nepal and other regions; OSE wiki documents a 2017 inaugural burn at Whole Village ecovillage (Ontario, Canada); further builds documented in Australia (Terra Preta Developments, Tasmania) and broader spread via the biochar community.
- Independent builds are documented in the biochar community (Australia, parts of Africa and Asia via open transfer) — but systematic replication tracking does not exist; the record is "spread via open transfer" not "N builds verified."
- PLOS ONE paper is independent peer-reviewed evidence (not project self-report) on emissions and char quality.

Maintenance and funding: Ithaka Institut (non-profit research/advocacy) anchors development; OSE hosts the wiki page; the design is low-maintenance hardware (steel cone) — no software maintenance. No commercial vendor; the design is free to build.

What this case demonstrates:
- Open-source technology transfer of a simple, low-cost hardware design to small farmers in a Global South context (Nepal) is documented and real — the design was driven by an on-the-ground deployment need, not an abstract open-source aspiration.
- Peer-reviewed open-access evidence (PLOS ONE 2015) exists on emissions and char quality for this open design — a stronger evidence base than most open hardware cases.
- Flame-curtain pyrolysis can produce high-quality, low-tar biochar at farmer scale with lower emissions than open burning — the technical claim is evidenced.
- A design can be "open source" in publication + practice + transferability without carrying a formal OSHWA/CERN OHL licence file — the licence-formality gap is real but does not negate the openness in practice.
- The biochar sub-domain has a richer open-hardware story than the compost sub-domain — multiple kiln designs, an aggregator (IBI), an open technology transfer model, and peer-reviewed evidence.

What it does NOT demonstrate:
- That every farmer can reliably build and operate the kiln without training — requires the charmaker's craft.
- That the design has been systematically replicated and tracked — documented spread exists but not systematic replication counting.
- That the design is the only or best open biochar kiln — the IBI ecosystem includes multiple designs (Wilson TFOD, McLaughlin TLUD/JRO, Starseed oven, Liberty processor); Kon-Tiki is the standout but not the full layer.
- That on-farm compost production is covered — this is biochar production, a separate sub-domain.
- That a formal hardware licence exists on the design — licence-formality gap, see above.
