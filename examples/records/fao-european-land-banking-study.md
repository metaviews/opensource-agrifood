## Problem addressed

State land banks and land agencies are Europe's institutional answer to farm-transfer
frictions — fragmented holdings, cash-flow distress, retiring farmers without
successors — but the mechanisms are scattered across national law. This FAO study
compares them in one place.

## What is actually open (verified from the PDF)

- **Latvia (ALTUM state land fund), purchase-and-lease-back with buy-back option**
  (launched 2017): "It performs acquisitions of agricultural land with the aim of
  subsequently leasing it back to the seller, also providing for a buy-back option.
  Land sale, leaseback and buy-back option agreements are concluded at the same time…
  The farmer leases back the land and also has the option to exercise the buyback right
  after five years. The lease prices are market-based" — public patient capital as a
  cash-flow bridge (cited to European Commission 2021).
- **France (SAFER), lease intermediation (intermédiation locative):** the statutory
  land agency collects lease promises from all of a retiring farmer's landlords so the
  farm can transfer whole — "It is used to facilitate the transfer of farms in cases
  where the existing farmer wishes to terminate their activities or retire."
- Comparative coverage of Denmark, Germany, Netherlands, Spain, Lithuania models.

## Why it belongs

Two firsts for the corpus in one document: it is the **only open-licensed (CC-licensed)
financing-side artifact the program has found** — proving the licensing gap is a
practice, not a law of nature — and it documents the two state mechanisms that answer
problems private capital structurally cannot: cash-flow bridges (purchase-lease-back
with buy-back) and farm fragmentation (whole-farm lease assembly). Both feed design
document P5's evidence base: a Canadian non-family transfer shelter has European state
precedents to copy, not just proposals to invent.

## What it does not demonstrate

Succession-specific design in the models (they are land-management instruments used for
transfer among other purposes); Canadian applicability; usage-scale figures for the
succession use case.
