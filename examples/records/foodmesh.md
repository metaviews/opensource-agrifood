# FoodMesh / Mesh Exchange

- Status: `curated` (counter-example with a governance nuance — B Corp infrastructure on public contracts)
- Region / reach: Vancouver, British Columbia; western and central Canada; Metro Vancouver regional contract
- Project: https://foodmesh.ca
- Last checked: 2026-09-07

## Problem addressed

A for-profit, B-Corp-certified marketplace and logistics layer for surplus food: Mesh Exchange (dba FoodMesh) lets suppliers (retailers, manufacturers, distributors, farms) sell, donate, or claim unsold surplus — including to farms as animal feed — with matching, liability handling, and impact measurement (self-reported: 53M+ kg diverted, 80M+ meals, $16M+ savings unlocked).

## Open layer

None. The platform is proprietary, built on Salesforce by Traction on Demand (Digital Technology Supercluster project, 2020–2023, "Emergency Food Distribution Network" consortium with United Way, Overwaitea/Save-On-Foods, Daiya, Buy-Low, Vancity enviroFund, Metro Vancouver, health authorities). No public source, no open data, no published API.

The governance nuance: FoodMesh's growth model is **public-sector contracting** — Metro Vancouver has a contract with FoodMesh to run the regional food recovery network; municipal and health-authority partners route their surplus-diversion obligations through a closed platform. Public waste-reduction policy is being operationalized on private, closed infrastructure, the same enclosure shape the corpus found in ag data (G-OSA-01) and land registries (G-OSA-35).

## Why it belongs

- It is the Canadian redistribution counter-example with a different shape than Second Harvest: not a charity licensing its app, but a B Corp winning government contracts with closed software. Together with Second Harvest and FoodHero it completes the Canadian landscape — every significant surplus-diversion pathway runs on closed platforms, each monetized differently (licensed product / contracted service / retailer discount app).
- Its Sobeys/Save-On-Foods relationship (Sobeys' zero-surplus commitment names Mesh Exchange and Loop Resources as delivery partners) makes it a plausible live node for the event's retail-processing discussion: the diversion layer that connects retailers to farms and charities already exists, closed, in the event's own backyard.

## What it does not demonstrate

Any open component; impact figures are self-reported (the site carries an "our statistics explained" page but no audited data). The record documents enclosure, not failure.
