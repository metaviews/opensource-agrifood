# Agricultural Commodity Exchange for Africa (ACE)

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: Malawi / Southern and Eastern Africa links
- Project: https://www.aceafrica.org/
- Field-guide context: structured grain trade, market information, warehouse receipts, and farmer access to commodity markets
- Last checked: 2026-08-15

## Problem addressed

Smallholder grain producers often sell immediately after harvest because they lack storage, market information, finance, and bargaining power. ACE combines market information, certified warehousing, warehouse receipts, and electronic trading to let producers and traders defer sales and use stored commodities as collateral.

## Open layer

ACE describes a market infrastructure and information service available to participants. Its public materials describe price information through SMS, email, rural marketing advisers, and online trading platforms, alongside a warehouse-receipt system and electronic exchange.

## What is actually open

- Public market information and trading-system descriptions.
- Public documentation of the warehouse-receipt model, certified warehouses, grading, storage, and electronic trading.
- Public ACE materials report 53 warehouses and 225,884 metric tonnes of storage capacity, including 29 rural warehouses; these are organisation-stated figures and require periodic re-verification.
- No open-source software repository or open licence for the trading platform was located.

## Governance and control

ACE is a licensed commodity-exchange and structured-trade institution with historical roots in NASFAM's attempt to improve market access for farmers. It is not an open-source or farmer-controlled software commons. Farmer associations participate in the physical and market infrastructure, but control of the digital platform and exchange rules is institutional.

## Evidence of use

IFPRI reported that ACE had operated a Market Information System and Warehouse Receipt System since 2011. Its analysis covered 710 warehouse receipts from 2011–2018 for maize, pigeon peas, and soybeans. The Common Fund for Commodities documented warehouse receipts accepted for delivery on ACE's electronic platform and the use of stored maize as collateral.

## Maintenance and funding

The system is maintained through ACE's exchange and structured-trade operations, with historical development support from the Common Fund for Commodities, European Union, and development partners. The sources reviewed do not establish the current financial sustainability or farmer-level distribution of value.

## What this case demonstrates

ACE is an important boundary case: a public-interest, farmer-access-oriented market infrastructure that addresses grain marketing directly, but is not open source. It shows why the grain ecosystem scan must distinguish open information and institutional access from open software, open data, and farmer governance.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not demonstrate open-source software or open protocol governance.
- It does not guarantee profitable storage; IFPRI found outcomes varied by commodity, financing, storage duration, and policy shocks.
- It does not establish current farmer control over transaction data or exchange rules.
- It is not evidence that a digital market automatically removes structural commodity-market risks.
