# CSA T201 — Cybersecurity Guidance for the Food Value Chain

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Canada — National Standard of Canada (domestic); scoped to the whole agri-food value chain
- Instrument: national standard under development by CSA Group (SDO accredited by the Standards Council of Canada), designation **CSA T201**, ICS 35.030 / 67.040
- Field-guide context: opened via the A4 cybersecurity/resilience scan (G-OSA-38, `research/2026-09-cybersecurity-resilience-scan.md`); verified 2026-09-09
- Record type: standards/governance record — the live Canadian policy surface for food-system cybersecurity

## Problem addressed

Canada's food system is undergoing rapid digitalization (precision ag, cloud-connected machinery, automated processing, online retail) while CSA Group's research programme with the Community Safety Knowledge Alliance (CSKA) found the food value chain **largely not implementing cybersecurity standards** — a critical-infrastructure sector with consumer-scale cyber maturity. The Canadian incidents in this collection (JBS Brooks AB 2021, Sobeys 2022, Maple Leaf 2022) are the consequence set.

## The instrument

CSA T201, "Cybersecurity Guidance for the Food Value Chain" (per the SCC notice of intent, posted 2026-08-10):

- Defines operating context for agri-food subsectors and **tailors established industrial cybersecurity requirements (IEC 62443 series)** to them — including security profiles on the IEC TS 62443-1-5 scheme, scaled to the range of cyber maturity actually found across the sector.
- Covers baseline cyber hygiene, organizational governance, workforce practices, roles and responsibilities, and alignment with Canadian critical-infrastructure and regulatory context.
- Users explicitly include food producers, processors, distributors, retailers, **agricultural cooperatives**, equipment and technology providers, industry associations, and governments.

## Status timeline (verified)

- CSA Group/CSKA national survey of food-value-chain businesses ran from February 2025.
- CSA Group research report "Cybersecurity in the Canadian Food Value Chain" published February 2026.
- Notice of intent posted by SCC 2026-08-10; **SDO comment period 2026-08-18 to 2026-09-09** (the comment deadline fell on the date this record was verified).
- Status at last check: open for SDO comment — not yet published as a final standard.

## Open layer

None yet — and that is the record's analytic point. Standards documents from CSA Group are copyrighted, paid products, not openly licensed; T201 is a closed instrument shaping an infrastructure question in which the corpus argues openness (auditability, federation, local operability) is itself a resilience property. The standard's machinery (IEC 62443 tailoring) is compatible with open deployment but silent on it. Whether "open" appears anywhere in T201 — e.g., in maturity profiles for self-hosted or open-source-dependent operators — is a checkable question once the final text publishes, and a concrete advocacy surface for the project.

## Why it belongs

1. It is the **single live Canadian policy surface** where the A4 argument (concentration is the vulnerability; local operability is resilience) could be written into binding-ish guidance.
2. It treats **cooperatives as a named user class** — the sector NEW Cooperative's 2021 attack showed to be exposed.
3. Its existence confirms the sector-level gap the scan documents: a national standard is being written *because* practice is absent, the same regulatory-gravity pattern G-OSA-33 found around CCP records.

## What it does not demonstrate

- Any content position — the comment-period draft text was not readable in full at verification time (CSA Group site behind bot-wall; SCC notice carries scope only). Claims here are about scope, status, and framing, not clause content.
- That T201 will require or even mention open-source practices — unknown until publication.
