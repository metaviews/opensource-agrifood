# USDA Certified State Agricultural Mediation Programs

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (federally certified state programs)
- Project: https://www.fsa.usda.gov/programs-and-services/certified-mediation-program
- Live docs: certification criteria at 7 CFR 785 (eCFR)
- Field-guide context: the C7 pass's institutional anchor — the only government mediation layer found where farm family transition is an explicitly eligible topic and the quality criteria are published law
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

Farm succession is a family-conflict process, but mediation services are typically
insolvency- or lender-dispute-shaped. The USDA-certified state mediation program
explicitly extends mediation to family transition.

## Open layer

- The program page (verified 2026-11-18) lists "**Family farm transition**" and
  "Farmer-neighbor disputes" among eligible mediation topics.
- The state-program certification criteria are published federal regulation
  (7 CFR 785.3: mediator training, confidentiality, notification duties) — the
  process standard is openly published law.
- US federal government work; no copyright restrictions on the program materials.

## What is actually open

Program rules and certification criteria as public law; state program pages
documenting eligibility and fees (e.g. Oregon OCAMP: first four hours free). The
mediators' competency curricula are NOT open — the private credentialing layer
(IFTN's $900–$999 Certified Farm Succession Coordinator training) sits on top.

## Governance and control

USDA FSA certifies; individual states operate programs. Hybrid funding observed
(Oregon: subsidized first hours, then paid).

## Evidence of use

Ohio Farm Resolution Services reports ~70% of its mediation docket is farm
transition and family communication (verified as reported — program's own blog
statement, not audited). Succession conflict is the core caseload where the service
exists.

## Maintenance and funding

Federal program; long-standing.

## Why it belongs

The C7 pass's anchor: it proves a public mediation layer can name family farm
transition as an eligible topic with published quality criteria — the design
reference for any Canadian succession-mediation proposal (Canada's Farm Debt
Mediation Service is insolvency-only). Its documented caseload (70%) is the
strongest evidence that succession conflict is a mainstream institutional demand,
not a niche.

## What it does not demonstrate

Uptake statistics beyond the Ohio statement; that certification criteria transfer to
Canadian jurisdictions; mediator competency openness (explicitly closed).
