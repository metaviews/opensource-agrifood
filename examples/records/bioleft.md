# Bioleft

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Argentina / Latin America
- Project: https://www.bioleft.org/es/
- Platform: https://plataforma.bioleft.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

Bioleft addresses enclosure and concentration in seed systems through open seed circulation, participatory breeding, community evaluation, and shared agricultural knowledge.

## Open layer

Biological commons, seed exchange, participatory breeding, open protocols, and community-generated agricultural data.

## What is actually open

Bioleft’s platform documents a process for uploading and exchanging seeds under its Open Multiplication Licence. In July 2026, the project released a 2022–2026 collaborative creole-tomato evaluation dataset through Zenodo. The release contains more than 10,000 agronomic and tasting observations covering more than 50 varieties and states that the dataset is available under a Creative Commons licence. A participatory evaluation protocol is also publicly linked.

## Governance and control

The project presents the work as community-generated through farmers, growers, researchers, and other evaluators working across Argentine sites. The dataset preserves participant privacy and anonymity. The precise governance relationship between the platform, seed contributors, evaluators, and institutional partners requires further study.

## Evidence of use

The dataset documents four seasons of collaborative evaluation from 2022–2026, including work in farms, gardens, and community spaces. The platform provides active procedures for registering, requesting, and offering seeds.

## Maintenance and funding

Bioleft is associated with the Universidad Nacional de San Martín and maintains an active public platform and publication stream. Long-term financial support, decision-making structures, and geographic expansion remain uncertain.

## Why it belongs

Bioleft is a particularly valuable Latin American biological-commons case. Unlike OSSI’s primarily pledge-based example, Bioleft combines seed circulation, participatory breeding, open data, open protocols, and community practice.

## What it does not demonstrate

Public seed and data releases do not by themselves establish unrestricted rights to all biological derivatives, broad farmer adoption, equitable benefit sharing, or a governance model transferable to every seed system.
