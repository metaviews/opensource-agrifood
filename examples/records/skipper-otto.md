# Skipper Otto Community Supported Fishery

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Canada (Vancouver origin; members and pickups across BC, AB, ON, QC)
- Project: https://skipperotto.com/
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the fisheries/aquaculture sectoral scan (G-OSA-34, sub-cell 3); verified 2026-09-04

## Problem addressed

Small-scale independent fishers face a structural squeeze: Canadian seafood is ~90% exported and ~80% of seafood consumed in Canada is imported, while independent harvesters depend on volatile dock prices and export-auction channels. The Community Supported Fishery model — consumers prepay for shares of the season's catch, sharing risk with fishing families — shortens the chain and stabilizes harvester income.

## Open layer

None. This record's analytical value is precisely the honest absence: Canada's canonical, largest, most market-successful CSF operates with **no open data, open source, or open operational practices** — the fisheries repetition of the grocery cell's retail finding.

## What is actually open

Nothing technical. Ordering runs on "the Dock" (dock.skipperotto.ca), a proprietary platform. "100% traceability and transparency" is a marketing claim delivered as member-facing fisher stories and package information — not open data, an API, or an open traceability standard contribution. No public dataset, repository, or open publication was found anywhere on their properties.

## Governance and control — the caveat that matters

**Skipper Otto is not a cooperative.** It is a private, family-owned, woman-led business: co-founders Sonia Strobel (CEO) and Shaun Strobel (lead fisherman), third generation now fishing. Members are customers (refundable prepaid share credit from $100, 60-day guarantee), not owners; fishing families are suppliers, not members. The co-op-like element is CSA-style risk-sharing only. Mission-lock exists through **B Corp certification** (Certified since November 2023, verified on the B Lab directory; B Impact Score 109.6 vs median 50.9; the "highest-scoring seafood B Corp in North America" claim is self-reported but consistent with the score). Certified Living Wage Employer (2023); Ocean Wise partner.

## Evidence of use

Founded 2008 (the site claims "Canada's First Community Supported Fishery" — self-claimed; Off the Hook, NS, 2010, claims "first Atlantic Canadian CSF," and no independent source adjudicates absolute first status). Scale per their own pages: **45+ small-scale independent fishing families**; "thousands" of member households (no precise count published); **80+ pickup locations** (35+ Metro Vancouver, 45+ elsewhere — some of their own pages say 100+; 80+ is the conservative figure) plus home delivery in select regions of BC, AB, ON, QC. UBC residence-dining partnership launched September 2025 (confirmed by UBC Food Services) — described as UBC's first direct-from-small-scale-local-fishers partnership.

## Why it belongs

Skipper Otto is the fisheries cell's proof that market success and community embeddedness do not produce open assets — completing the corpus's governance-without-open-assets pattern across grocery retail (CSN, Landare), processing (IGFC), and now fisheries. The record documents the strongest CSF in Canada as a counterweight to the assumption that community-oriented food enterprises are naturally open. Its B Corp mission-lock is the closest thing to a verifiable, audited openness-adjacent commitment in the sector — an audited private standard substituting for open infrastructure.

## What it does not demonstrate

Openness (the finding). Cooperative governance (despite the sector's co-op rhetoric). Whether CSFs at scale change fishery data practices — no evidence they do. "Canada's first CSF" and "Canada's largest CSF" are self-claims; scale figures are self-reported.
