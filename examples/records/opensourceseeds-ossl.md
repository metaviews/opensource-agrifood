# OpenSourceSeeds / Open Source Seed Licence (OSSL)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Germany / Europe (licence usable internationally)
- Project: https://www.opensourceseeds.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the biological-commons cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

Seed privatisation and market concentration: plant-variety protection and patents restrict saving, exchanging, and breeding from seeds. OpenSourceSeeds (a service of the German NGO Agrecol, Association for AgriCulture and Ecology) provides new varieties under an Open Source Seed Licence (OSSL) to re-establish seed as a commons.

## Open layer

Licence-based biological commons: the OSSL is a legal tool that protects licensed germplasm and extends its reach to derivatives, compatible with existing seed laws. It is a contractual licence rather than a pledge — the key distinction from the US Open Source Seed Initiative (OSSI), whose pledge is a moral commitment.

## What is actually open

- The OSSL text is published on the project site with breeder information and a variety registry: licensed varieties include the Sunviva tomato (a well-known OSSL case), Vivagrande/Vivaroma tomatoes, spring/winter wheats (Convento C, Nudelwunder, Bäckerglück), sweetcorn Lisanco, chilli "Black Heart", and potatoes.
- The licence's design is documented in the academic literature (Kotschi, "The Open Source Seed Licence: A novel approach to safeguarding access to plant germplasm", 2018, cited 57 times): it works because seeds are licensed, not merely pledged, and the obligation travels with derivatives.
- Commons-based seed-system research extends the framing (Moeller/Pedersen "Open Source Seed Networking"; Fredriksson 2021 on open source seeds and local development).

## Governance and control

NGO-governed (Agrecol). The licence is the control mechanism: anyone using licensed seed accepts the obligation to keep derivatives open. Enforcement history is the open question — no public record of a licence being tested in court was located.

## Evidence of use

- The variety registry is live and specific (named varieties available for purchase).
- The 2018 academic treatment establishes the licence's legal design and compatibility with seed law.
- "Open-source bread" and public campaigns document the project's community engagement.
- The number of licensees, varieties under licence at scale, and enforcement activity were not verified in this pass.

## Maintenance and funding

NGO-run with donations/support ("Support us" programme). Long-term sustainability of the registry and legal maintenance is not documented.

## What this case demonstrates

OpenSourceSeeds/OSSL is the strongest located alternative to pledge-based seed openness: a contractual licence whose obligations travel with derivatives — the enforceable counterpart to OSSI's pledge. It completes the collection's biological-commons layer with the licence mechanics that OSSI and Bioleft lack.

## What it does not demonstrate

- It does not establish enforcement in practice; no court-tested cases were located.
- Licensee and variety scale beyond the registry is unverified.
- It operates mainly in Europe; Global South uptake is unverified (G-BC-02).
- The licence's effectiveness against large commercial breeders is untested.
