# Understanding the Conversation about Farming — FrameWorks media-framing analysis

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (national news and field communications)
- Project: https://www.frameworksinstitute.org/app/uploads/2020/10/Farming-MCFFA-Report.pdf

- Field-guide context: the C11 pass's framing evidence — the measured crisis narrative any opportunity-framed succession content must run against
- Last checked: 2026-11-19

## Problem addressed

How news media frames farming shapes how the public and the sector understand
problems like succession. This report analyzes that framing directly.

## Open layer

Free-to-read report PDF (FrameWorks Institute, 2020, with the Farming and Food
Narrative Project); no licence line recorded.

## What is actually open (verified from the PDF, 2026-11-19)

- "The media often portray farmers as victims struggling under the weight of
  macroeconomic forces outside their control."
- "The tone of these stories is overwhelmingly negative and fatalistic."

## Governance and control

FrameWorks Institute (nonprofit communications research); commissioning partners
per report.

## Evidence of use

Established media-framing methodology applied to farming coverage; cited across
agricultural communications work.

## Maintenance and funding

Published 2020; static report.

## Why it belongs

C11's media-framing cell needed measured evidence rather than impressions, and this
is the strongest available: farming coverage is crisis-framed and fatalistic in the
market any succession content competes in. Canadian succession trade press
(Glacier FarmMedia, verified in the pass) leads with the same plan-gap crisis
frame. The transfer from general farming coverage to succession framing specifically
is recorded as an inference the pass did not test.

## What it does not examine

Succession themes specifically; Canadian trade press; social media ('farmtok').
