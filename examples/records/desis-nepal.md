# DESIS 2.0 — Digitally Enabled Seed Information System (Nepal)

Status: candidate

Region/reach: Nepal; national seed-sector infrastructure across federal, provincial, and local institutions

Project links:
- Current platform: https://seed.sqcc.gov.np/
- CIMMYT 2025 account: https://www.cimmyt.org/news/delivering-seed-from-breeder-to-farmer-inclusive-digital-innovations-transforming-nepals-seed-systems/
- CIMMYT 2020 account: https://www.cimmyt.org/news/seed-systems-in-nepal-are-going-digital/

Problem addressed: slow, paper-based aggregation of seed demand and supply; weak coordination among public research, certification, seed businesses, cooperatives, and farmers; limited information on registered varieties and seed quality.

Open layer: public-interest digital seed infrastructure. An earlier CIMMYT account described DESIS as based on open-source software. The current platform provides public service information and access pathways, but a current source repository, licence, API documentation, and open-data terms were not located. The open-source claim is therefore unresolved.

What is actually open or public:
- Public-facing seed catalogue and information service.
- Described catalogue of more than 80 crops and 800 released or registered varieties.
- Digital seed demand/supply balance-sheet functions.
- Digital inspection and certification processes.
- Seed tracking and QR/blockchain-backed authenticity functions are described by CIMMYT.
- Earlier project documentation described the software as open source and planned a mobile web/Android service.
- Current source code, licence, API, data export rights, and farmer data policy were not located. The live platform displays a “Developers Credit” element, but the public material reviewed does not identify the credited developer, source repository, or procurement/maintenance relationship.

Governance and control: the platform is managed and institutionalized through Nepal’s Seed Quality Control Center, with links to NARC, CCDABC, PQPMC, the Financial Comptroller General’s Office, and the Department of Information Technology. CIMMYT, USAID/Feed the Future, and local technology partners supported development. Governance is public-sector and institutional rather than demonstrated farmer ownership.

Evidence of use:
- CIMMYT reports hundreds of active users during the earlier rollout and national institutional adoption of DESIS 2.0.
- The current platform is live and identifies the Government of Nepal and Seed Quality Control Center as anchors.
- CIMMYT reports use across federal, provincial, and local government tiers and participation by seed-sector stakeholders.
- Current operational volume and farmer-level outcomes were not independently verified.

Maintenance and funding: institutionalized government ownership and integration into the national seed-control system provide a stronger maintenance anchor than a short-lived pilot. Development funding and technical support came through CIMMYT/USAID and local partners. Current vendor, support, code ownership, and procurement terms remain unverified.

What this case demonstrates:
- Nepal has a nationally oriented digital seed infrastructure with real institutional scope.
- Public-interest digital infrastructure can be important without being open source.
- A historical open-source description must be checked against current licence and source-release evidence.

What it does NOT demonstrate:
- That DESIS 2.0 currently releases source code under an open-source licence.
- That seed data is openly reusable or portable by farmers.
- That blockchain or QR tracking itself creates farmer control.
- That current user numbers, certification volumes, or farmer outcomes are independently verified.
