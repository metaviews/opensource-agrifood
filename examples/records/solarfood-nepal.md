# SolarFood solar-drying project (Nepal)

Status: lead

Region/reach: Nepal and Bhutan; Kathmandu University and local women-led cooperatives are part of the project ecosystem

Project links:
- Ruralis 2025 project update: https://ruralis.no/en/2025/06/10/solarfood-kicks-off-in-nepal-aiming-to-empower-women-in-the-himalayan-region/
- Ruralis/SolarFood needs assessment: https://ruralis.no/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/r-8_22-a-need-assessment-for-designing-sustainable-solar-drying-technology-in-nepal-and-bhutan-p-otte-et-al.pdf

Problem addressed: post-harvest loss and inadequate, inefficient, or unhygienic food preservation technologies for small-scale farmers.

Open layer: emerging local-manufacturing and open-transfer pathway. SolarFood aims to develop solar dryers that are locally manufactured, locally owned, socially accepted, and embedded in women-led cooperative business models. No public open hardware licence or complete design-file release was located in this pass.

What is currently public:
- A 2022 needs assessment based on farmer interviews in Nepal and Bhutan.
- Public descriptions of a solar dryer developed through the earlier project phase.
- A 2025 project phase focused on inclusive business models and women-led agricultural cooperatives.
- Explicit project goals around local ownership and local manufacturing.
- No verified public licence, CAD repository, bill of materials, independent build, or current cooperative deployment record located.

Governance and control: the project is research-led, with Ruralis, Lund University, Royal University of Bhutan, and Kathmandu University among the partners. Women-led agricultural cooperatives are intended to be central users and business actors. The degree of cooperative ownership, design control, and downstream value capture remains to be verified.

Evidence of use: the 2022 work includes 15 Nepal farmer interviews within an 80-interview Nepal/Bhutan needs assessment. The 2025 update states that fieldwork and case-area selection were underway. A current deployment outcome was not located.

Maintenance and funding: the project is funded through research support, including the Swedish Research Council for the earlier phase and the Research Council of Norway for the newer phase. A post-project maintenance and manufacturing model is still being developed.

What this case demonstrates:
- Local manufacturing and women-led cooperative ownership are being treated as design requirements rather than afterthoughts.
- Appropriate technology may become open through transferability and local production, even before a formal open-source release.

What it does NOT demonstrate:
- That SolarFood is currently open source or open hardware.
- That women-led cooperatives own the design, manufacturing, or resulting businesses.
- That the dryer has been independently replicated or adopted at scale.
