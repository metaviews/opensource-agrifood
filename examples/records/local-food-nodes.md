# Local Food Nodes

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: Sweden-origin (Röstånga ek Förening) — reported deployment in ~14 countries (Finland, Norway, Germany, Belgium, UK, USA, Japan, South Africa, Australia, and others)
- Project: pre-order-only local farmers-market platform run by a donation-based, not-for-sale economic association; self-described as a "fully financial transparent and not for sale, digital open source solution"
- Field-guide context: opened via the Finnish co-op survey's follow-up pass (`grocery/research/finnish-co-op-survey.md`, addendum 2026-11-25) as the structured alternative to Facebook-based REKO rings
- Record type: open-platform lifecycle record — curated as candidate with defunction as the finding

## Problem addressed

The platform-exit problem of informal local-food trade: REKO rings run on closed Facebook groups, and their founder declined replacement software. Local Food Nodes was the nearest attempt to provide what REKO refused — an independent, association-run, open-code ordering platform where "participants own the sales channel."

## The open layer — and the honest finding

- **Claimed open**: the platform was historically public at `gitlab.com/localfoodnodes/localfoodnodes` and catalogued as MIT-licensed PHP in the awesome-selfhosted list — **third-party catalogue claim only**.
- **Scale (verified as reported)**: "As of July 2023, the app boasts 1050 producers and 280 nodes, attracting approximately 20 new users daily" (FAO AgTech Observatory page, aggregating LFN's own figures).
- **Now apparently defunct (verified 2026-11-25)**: localfoodnodes.org serves a NameBright domain-parking page, and the GitLab project API returns 404. The MIT licence can no longer be verified against any primary source. Last good Wayback snapshot seen: 2025-08-04. No press release or successor statement located.

## Why it belongs (as candidate)

1. It is the corpus's control experiment for REKO's no-tooling choice: the structured open alternative existed, reached real multi-country scale, and died. The lifecycle lesson — open-source claims without durable infrastructure vanish silently, and a defunct open platform is indistinguishable from a closed one once the repo disappears — belongs beside the collection's active commons records (Foodsoft, FoodCoopShop).
2. Its legal form (donation-based economic association, "not for sale") is a governance-design data point for the platform-cooperative layer.
3. The defunction is documented, not assumed: domain parked, repo 404, licence unverifiable — each checked at source.

## What it does not demonstrate

- Current operation — the platform appears dead; no formal closure statement was found.
- The MIT licence — unverifiable from any primary source; recorded as third-party claim only.
- That REKO rings migrated to it — no evidence of displacement found in any country.
- Openness of anything today — there is no reachable artifact.
