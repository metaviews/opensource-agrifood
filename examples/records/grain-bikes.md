# Grain Bikes (bicycle-powered grain processing machines)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: US Northeast (Massachusetts); plans freely buildable anywhere
- Project: https://farmhack.org/tools/bicycle-powered-thresherfan-mill-and-dehuller
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the processing sectoral scan (G-OSA-33, `research/2026-08-processing-sectoral-scan.md` sub-cell 2); verified 2026-09-02

## Problem addressed

Small farms and micro-mills need threshing, winnowing, dehulling, and coarse milling without grid power or four-figure equipment. Grain Bikes provides human-powered machines (~30-100 W) for exactly that scale — processing grain a few pounds at a time, off-grid, for a few hundred dollars in materials.

## Open layer

Published hardware: complete step-by-step build plans (PDFs) and a video build series, hosted on the FarmHack tool library under an explicit licence. Machine suite: bicycle-powered thresher (~0.5-1 lb/min), fanning mill/winnower (up to 10 lb/min), dehuller/flour mill (~0.5 lb/min); the SARE project also covered a seed fractionator and a bicycle PTO power unit (five machines total, three core).

## What is actually open

Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0), shown on each FarmHack tool page. Important boundary: the plans are deliberately human-oriented workshop instructions — the SARE final report states they are "not blueprints or cad files," with no bill of materials or cut list. This is workshop-level documentation, not engineering design files.

## Governance and control

Community-hosted on FarmHack (the peer design-sharing commons already in the corpus). Designed and built by Jan Yoder (SARE project leader, grant recipient Woodmetalcanvas), initiated 2014 at farmer Bill Braun's request, with cooperator Emily Vogler (plans), filmmaker Olaf Bertram-Nothnagel (video), and three farms.

## Evidence of use

Funded by Northeast SARE Partnership grant ONE16-277 ($13,734, 2016-2017), public final report. Materials per machine "well under $200" excluding scavenged bikes (thresher $148.75, fanning mill $145); build 21-39 h per machine. Replication: at least one independently documented third-party build (thresher, with video, "works well in beans but harder on wheat"), plus further builders running first samples and a mill build ($80 parts, 15.5 h, CS Bell burr modifications). The final report called adoption "too early to tell." No systematic replication record.

## Maintenance and funding

One-time SARE grant funded the documentation; FarmHack hosts the pages (tool pages last updated 2024-08-22). No ongoing maintenance model — typical of the grant archetype, but the plans' completeness reduces the maintenance burden.

## Why it belongs

The only CC-licensed open processing-hardware design found in the entire processing scan, at any scale — and its scale (bicycle-powered, 2-3 orders of magnitude below plant capacity) is itself the finding. It marks the boundary where the open layer currently ends in grain processing: real machines, real plans, real licence, micro-scale. Any future open mill-hardware design would start from this precedent and its gap.

## What it does not demonstrate

Plant-scale viability, throughput, or any regulatory pathway (grain processing for sale). Replication beyond a handful of documented builds is unverified. The plans lack BOMs and CAD, which aids accessibility but limits engineering-grade replication.
