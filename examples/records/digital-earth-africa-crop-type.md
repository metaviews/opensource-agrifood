# Digital Earth Africa crop-type mapping framework

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Africa; Zambia, Namibia, and later scaling references to Niger, Rwanda, and Mozambique
- Project page: https://digitalearthafrica.org/en_za/creating-an-open-source-framework-for-crop-type-mapping-in-africa/
- Repository: https://github.com/digitalearthafrica/crop-type
- Field-guide context: new discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

The framework supports crop-type mapping through field-data collection, model development, and map production using Earth-observation data and the Digital Earth Africa platform.

## Open layer

Open analytical workflows, documented notebooks, repository code, and access to analysis-ready satellite datasets and continental services.

## What is actually open

The `digitalearthafrica/crop-type` repository is public and identifies an Apache-2.0 licence. It documents a repeatable notebook workflow for sampling, preparing field samples, and predicting crop types through the Digital Earth Africa platform.

## Governance and control

The framework was developed with the Regional Centre for Mapping of Resources for Development and FrontierSI, with country-level and institutional partners. The public sources do not yet establish who controls field labels, derived maps, or local agricultural data.

## Evidence of use

Digital Earth Africa describes development for Zambia and scaling partnerships involving Niger, Rwanda, and Mozambique. The repository’s latest visible commit is from December 2022, creating an important distinction between institutional programme activity and visible repository maintenance.

## Maintenance and funding

The framework was supported through the Enabling Crop Analytics at Scale initiative. Current maintenance, country-level capacity, and operational ownership require further evidence.

## Why it belongs

This is a strong African open geospatial case. It adds remote sensing and crop analytics without reducing the collection to another agricultural AI model, and it raises the question of whether open workflows produce durable local technical control.

## What it does not demonstrate

An open notebook repository and named country partnerships do not establish current operational use, farmer benefit, local ownership of data, or continued maintenance.
