# Upcycled Certified Standard (Upcycled Food Association / Where Food Comes From)

- Status: `curated` (counter-example — certification capture of the category's definitional layer)
- Region / reach: US-origin, global programme; WFCF is a NASDAQ-listed verification company
- Project: https://www.upcycledfood.org/upcycled-certification / https://www.wherefoodcomesfrom.com/upcycled
- Last checked: 2026-09-07

## Problem addressed

A young product category needs a definition: what counts as "upcycled"? The Upcycled Food Association (trade association) developed the Upcycled Certified Standard — the category's definitional vocabulary, covering which ingredients qualify (surplus or byproduct that would otherwise be wasted), supply-chain audit requirements, and on-pack claims. The standard was then acquired: Where Food Comes From, Inc. (WFCF) bought the Upcycled Certified program and is "the exclusive source for Upcycled Certified" verification.

## Open layer

None — and the fee schedule makes the capture precise (Upcycled-Certified-Fee-Schedule-2024.pdf, read 2026-09-07):

- New client application: $250
- Product review: $1,250 for 1–5 products, $100/product thereafter (annual)
- Licensing fee: $175/product/year for use of the mark
- Chain-of-custody desk audit: $500/location
- Bundleable with WFCF's other programmes (Non-GMO Project, Certified Organic, etc.)

A company wanting to truthfully label its upcycled product must pay a recurring per-product toll to a single for-profit verifier for the category's only recognized definition. The standard document itself is not published as an open specification; access comes through engagement with the certification regime.

## Why it belongs

The sub-cell scan's shape 2: upcycling's standards layer was captured from birth. Contrast the A1 processing layer, where the standards that exist (GS1 EPCIS 2.0) are royalty-free even when their identifiers cost money — in upcycling, the *definition itself* is the revenue stream. This matches the maintenance-funding taxonomy's prediction that certification becomes the value-capture point where practices scale, and it sharpens the corpus's "who controls" question: control of a food category's meaning, priced per product per year.

## What it does not demonstrate

That certification is bad governance per se — third-party verification has real value and WFCF administers credible audit processes. The record documents exclusivity and pricing, not bad faith. The open-question residue: could a free, open-licensed definition of upcycled content coexist with paid verification? Nothing in the regime allows it today.
