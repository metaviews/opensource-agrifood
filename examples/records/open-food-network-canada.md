# Open Food Network Canada

- Status: `candidate`
- Region / reach: Canada; connected to the global Open Food Network community
- Project: https://about.openfoodnetwork.ca/
- Open agricultural technology: https://about.openfoodnetwork.ca/project/open-agricultural-technology/
- Field-guide context: Canadian discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

Open Food Network Canada supports local-food commerce and community food infrastructure through online marketplace software, resources, and partnerships for farmers, food producers, and community hubs.

## Open layer

Software commons, local-food marketplace infrastructure, open standards, data-governance frameworks, and community partnerships.

## What is actually open

The Canadian organisation describes the Open Food Network platform as marketplace software and identifies a global community using and contributing to a software commons. Its open-agricultural-technology page explicitly distinguishes open source, open standards, and data sovereignty, and points to OpenTEAM, GOAT, and the Data Food Consortium Open Standard.

## Governance and control

Open Food Network Canada is an incorporated not-for-profit. Its public framing places farmer and community control, data sovereignty, and protection from lock-in at the centre. The exact governance relationship between the Canadian organisation, global software, local operators, and data holders remains to be verified.

## Evidence of use

The organisation reports a decade of supporting farms, artisans, and community organisations, and lists current Canadian projects including local-food directories, open agricultural technology, and sustainable cut-flower initiatives. These claims require more deployment-specific verification.

## Maintenance and funding

The Canadian organisation is supported through partners and funders and maintains active public programmes. Repository maintenance, hosting economics, and software governance require direct checking.

## Why it matters

This is the clearest Canadian lead for the distribution and coordination layer of agrifood. It would extend the catalogue from production and research infrastructure into local-food marketplaces and community-controlled food networks.

## What it does not demonstrate

A not-for-profit structure and data-sovereignty framing do not establish open licensing of every component, farmer control in every deployment, or equitable outcomes across local food networks.
