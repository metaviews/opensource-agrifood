# Agrarian Commons legal guide (Farmland Access Legal Toolkit / CAFS)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (Vermont Law and Graduate School's Center for Agriculture and Food Systems, with Agrarian Trust); model documents usable as drafts anywhere US tax/legal concepts apply, untested outside
- Project: https://farmlandaccess.org/guide-to-creating-an-agrarian-commons/
- Live docs: https://farmlandaccess.org/land-trusts/agrarian-commons-guide/equitable-lease/ (sample lease downloadable as .docx)
- Field-guide context: the only complete, free-to-read legal document set for community-based farmland stewardship found in the succession program's C3 pass
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

Community-based farm stewardship vehicles (the Agrarian Commons model: a national 501(c)(3) supporting local 501(c)(2)/(c)(25)-family land-holding commons) require custom legal structures that no group can cheaply reinvent. This guide publishes the structure — including the hard-won finding about which US tax structures fail.

## Open layer

Free to read and download; no open licence. Every page carries an educational-use disclaimer only ("this toolkit provides general legal information for educational purposes only"). The sample equitable lease downloads as an editable `.docx` — the format is adaptable even though the licence is not.

## What is actually open

- **Narrative legal learning** (verified from the guide, 2026-11-18): how 501(c)(2) and (c)(25) structures were expected to work and why they narrowed — (c)(2) was designed by Congress to hold property for a single nonprofit parent; educational programming or on-farm events can risk tax-exempt status; the parent's required 51% board control is "at odds with AT's goal of supporting a local community to have local control."
- **Sample 501(c)(3) bylaws**, an **Equitable Lease sample** (17 articles, 2-year initial term with mutual intention to renew to 99 years; preamble states Agrarian's 501(c)(3) purposes incl. protecting farmland from speculation; exhibits include a Memorandum of Understanding between tenants and leased farm equipment description), a **sample land donation agreement**, and ecological stewardship/farm-ranch standards.
- Lease-term limits note: state law caps some farm lease terms (some states max 10 years); "Agricultural Lease Terms by State" marked "Coming soon!"

## Governance and control

Produced by CAFS (a law-school initiative) in partnership with Agrarian Trust; the guide's content is jointly authored legal work. No community governance over the documents themselves; CAFS controls the toolkit site.

## Evidence of use

The Agrarian Commons model it documents is operating (10 commons, 8 states, 400+ acres after ~12 years — per the land scan, G-OSA-35). No third-party replication using these sample documents is documented.

## Maintenance and funding

Law-school/programme-funded (Vermont Law and Graduate School). No stated update schedule; the lease sample upload path shows 2024/09.

## Why it belongs

This is the closest thing found to an open farm-tenure legal template set — and its licence status is the C3 finding in miniature: the sector's most complete model documents are free to read but carry no right to adapt and redistribute, which reproduces the lawyer-in-the-loop bottleneck for every would-be replicator. It also carries the program's strongest evidence that the legal layer is custom-built and expensive (the (c)(2)/(c)(25) saga).

## What it does not demonstrate

No documented adoption of the sample documents by any commons; no Canadian-law version; the equitable lease is a sample with placeholders ("[pdfs to be inserted]" exhibits) and has no stated licence permitting adaptation; "Coming soon!" sections may never arrive.
