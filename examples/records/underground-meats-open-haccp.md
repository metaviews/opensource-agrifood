# Underground Meats open-source HACCP plan

- Status: `archived` (historical; open infrastructure failed — see Why it belongs)
- Region / reach: United States (Madison, WI origin; claimed use by ~20 US processors)
- Project: Kickstarter "Underground Meats: Open Source Food Safety Model" (2013)
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the processing sectoral scan (G-OSA-33, `research/2026-08-processing-sectoral-scan.md` sub-cell 4); verified 2026-09-02

## Problem addressed

HACCP plan development is the fixed cost that keeps small meat processors out of compliance: each plant must write and validate its own plan against the same hazard logic. Underground Meats (Madison, WI, part of Underground Food Collective) proposed to break that by validating one USDA dry-cured salami HACCP plan and giving it away as a template.

## Open layer

A shared food-safety compliance record — a validated HACCP plan released under the Creative Commons CC0 Public Domain Dedication, with a stated intent that other processors adapt it. Templates, not software.

## What is actually open

The first draft plan ("UFC_HACCP_NotCookedShelfStable_March2014.pdf", ~208 KB) was published in March/April 2014 under the CC0 Public Domain Dedication as committed on the Kickstarter page. Correction to the earlier scan note: no wiki ever existed — the plan was hosted as a static PDF on the company's own sites (undergroundbutcher.com, then a Squarespace URL). The March 2014 draft remains retrievable via the Wayback Machine.

## Governance and history

Grassroots initiative by Underground Food Collective; no institutional steward. Kickstarter "Open Source Food Safety Model": goal $40,000, raised $49,141 from 714 backers (123%), closed 2013-10-12; 11 updates, the last on 2017-05-31. Documented by NPR (2013-10-02), Food Safety News (2013-09-20), Eater (2014-07-11), and The Counter (2016-01).

## Evidence of use

Jonny Hunter stated in The Counter (2016) that "at least 20 U.S. processors have used the Underground plan to navigate the compliance process" — self-reported, no independent corroboration located. Even taken at face value, usage never produced a maintained commons.

## Status of the open infrastructure: failed

- The Squarespace PDF host cited by The Counter returns 404 in all captures from 2024 onward (never successfully archived live).
- undergroundmeats.com no longer has any HACCP page; undergroundfoodcollective.org does not resolve; no "openhaccp" domain or wiki was ever found live or archived.
- No successor project or cooperative successor infrastructure was located.
- The company itself still operates (undergroundmeats.com live, products in retail as of 2026) but has published nothing about the project since ~2017.
- The plan survives only in Wayback Machine captures: https://web.archive.org/web/20140512115605id_/http://undergroundbutcher.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/UFC_HACCP_NotCookedShelfStable_March2014.pdf

## Why it belongs

This is the processing cell's single codified-and-licensed open artifact — and its failure is the finding. It proves demand existed (714 backers, ~$49k, self-reported adoption by ~20 processors) and that durability did not follow. The processing scan attributes the failure to regulatory gravity: HACCP plans require plant-specific validation, which structurally limits template reuse; a CC0 document with no steward, no update mechanism, and no institution behind it decayed within a few years of release. The record anchors G-OSA-33's fourth shape — the regulatory-validation wall around CCP records that no open project has crossed.

## What it does not demonstrate

That open food-safety records are impossible — only that this attempt, at one plant, with one plan, without stewardship infrastructure, did not persist. The ≥20-processor figure is self-reported. The archived PDF is a 2014 draft; its validation status and any later revisions are unknown.
