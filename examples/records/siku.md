# SIKU — the Indigenous Knowledge Social Network

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Arctic-wide (Canada, Alaska, Greenland); 100+ active Indigenous-led projects (University of Waterloo VIP page)
- Project: free mobile app and web platform "by and for Indigenous Communities and Harvesters" — wildlife, sea ice, weather, and food observations; operated by the Arctic Eider Society (Sanikiluaq-origin Canadian charity)
- Field-guide context: opened via the A5 northern/remote food systems scan (G-OSA-39, `research/2026-09-northern-remote-food-systems-scan.md`); verified 2026-09-09 against the platform's Terms of Use (2026-04-13 version)

## Problem addressed

Indigenous harvesting knowledge — the operational data of the country-food system — has historically been extracted by outside researchers or held in inaccessible government databases. Harvesters need tools to document ice safety, wildlife, and food sources on their own terms, and communities need infrastructure that keeps stewardship authority local.

## Open layer (read carefully — this record's point is the governance model)

SIKU is **free but not open-licensed**; its radical openness is in the data-governance inversion, verified from its Terms of Use:

- **"You own your data. Nobody else does – not even SIKU."** Sharing a post grants *no* licence to the platform — the Arctic Eider Society must seek permission to use content like any other third party. This is the exact opposite of every mainstream social platform's ToS.
- Post-by-post privacy/stewardship/ownership controls; user-facing dashboards; data downloadable by its owners at any time.
- The platform explicitly rejects the citizen-science frame: "not a citizen science tool (citizens providing data to scientists) but rather a tool to empower Indigenous self-determination (Indigenous peoples collecting data for Indigenous peoples and maintaining control over data stewardship)."
- Existing Indigenous governance structures (permits, research licensing, ethics protocols) must be respected by all users, including researchers.

This is the corpus's clearest operating example of **sovereignty-first platform design**: sharing is a community decision, opening is not the default, and the platform operator has contractually no rights over user data.

## Function in the food system

Country-food harvesting documentation (hunting stories, wildlife observations, ice conditions for safe travel to harvesting areas), food-sharing knowledge, species observations feeding Indigenous-led research and co-management; SmartICE sea-ice thickness data (SmartBUOY/SmartQAMUTIK) is delivered *through* SIKU rather than an open-data portal. Canadian Space Agency partnership (2025) integrates satellite data. Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami's Inuit Nunangat Food Security Strategy context: harvesting data under Inuit authority.

## What it does not demonstrate

- Source-code openness — the app's code licence is not published/verified (site under maintenance at check; no public repo located). The openness claim rests on governance terms, not software freedom.
- That community-controlled platforms guarantee community *capacity* — stewardship still requires local data clerks, connectivity, and training.
- Open-data interoperability: by design, nothing on SIKU is openly licensed, so integration with external research requires permission flows rather than API access to open data.
