# L'Atelier Paysan

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: France; originated in Rhône-Alpes, later Brittany; national reach through training tours and local "essaims" (local chapters)
- Project: https://www.latelierpaysan.org/
- Commons Transition case study: https://primer.commonstransition.org/4-more/5-elements/case-studies/case-study-l-ateliet-paysan/
- Field-guide context: new discovery from the open-hardware/robotics/sensing cycle, 2026-08-02

## Problem addressed

Small-scale and organic farmers in France need agricultural machines suited to their practices, but mainstream manufacturers rarely produce them. When such machines exist, maintenance is costly and farmers must adapt their farming techniques to the machine's logic rather than the reverse.

## Open layer

Open designs for agricultural machinery and buildings, with farmer self-build training, openly published plans, and cooperative governance.

## What is actually open

L'Atelier Paysan publishes tool plans and design files openly through its "Outils et plans" section and technical collections. The Commons Transition case study (2017) describes the design files as openly available on the project platform, forming a global digital commons. Specific licence terms for individual design assets are stated per asset and were not independently audited for this record. The hardware itself is almost entirely metal; the cooperative runs metalworking workshops (cutting, drilling, welding) so farmers can build and repair their own tools, including via mobile training units.

## Governance and control

L'Atelier Paysan emerged in 2009 as a subgroup of ADAbio, an organic-agriculture development association in Rhône-Alpes, and later became a cooperative (SCIC) of farmers, employees, and farming organisations. The cooperative's internal decision-making and control structures were not audited for this record. Design and training activity is documented as co-developed with farmers: tools developed by farmers (initially by the farmer Joseph and others) were standardised, documented, and disseminated by the founders.

## Evidence of use

- The project reports sustained training and self-build activity across France, including wine, fruit, cattle, and horse-power farming, and market gardening.
- FAO's STI portal records L'Atelier Paysan publications and describes the cooperative and its mobile machinery units.
- Independent coverage exists from Commons Transition, Shareable, Local Futures, Low-tech Lab, and the GIA (Agroecology in Action) network.
- The project's own site shows active news through July 2026 (tool updates, training sessions, local chapters).
- No independent audit of the number of tools built or farms equipped was located.

## Maintenance and funding

On 21 April 2026 the Tribunal de Commerce de Grenoble pronounced the judicial liquidation (liquidation judiciaire) of the SCIC L'Atelier Paysan. This is a first-order maintenance finding: even a farmer-governed cooperative with a decade and a half of activity was not financially sustainable in its corporate form.

The movement's continuation is being organised through two successor structures: the association Communs Paysans (farmers, agricultural employees, citizen supporters, and agroecology organisations) and Soudons, fermes !, the network of local essaims. The public call from these structures states that the liquidation will not end the political and technical project, and commits to preserving the resources and knowledge gathered by L'Atelier Paysan and protecting them from commercial appropriation. Whether the design assets survive the liquidation intact, and under what terms, is unresolved.

## What this case demonstrates

L'Atelier Paysan is the strongest located example of farmer-governed open hardware in agriculture: a cooperative that integrates design, documentation, training, and support so that the openness of the plans is backed by the capability to build and repair. Its 2026 liquidation demonstrates that governance-model strength does not by itself solve the funding and maintenance problem — the same fragility the project's research question is built around.

## What it does not demonstrate

- The specific licence of every design asset is not verified.
- The number of tools built, farms equipped, or farmers trained is not independently audited.
- The cooperative's internal governance and decision-making are not independently documented.
- The survival of the design commons through the liquidation, and the licence terms under which it continues, are unresolved as of the research date.
