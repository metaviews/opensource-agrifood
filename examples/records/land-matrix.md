# Land Matrix

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: global (low- and middle-income-country focus; ~100 countries covered)
- Project: https://landmatrix.org/
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the land sectoral scan (G-OSA-35, sub-cell 1); verified 2026-09-04

## Problem addressed

Large-scale land acquisitions — the "land rush" — proceed through deals that are rarely public. The Land Matrix is the independent monitoring initiative that collects, verifies, and publishes deal-level data on them: investors, size, purpose, location, outcomes, and narratives.

## Open layer

Open data with a verified open licence, plus multi-stakeholder coalition governance. Bulk CSV/XLSX exports via a documented API endpoint (deals, locations, contracts, data sources, involvements, investors tabs), with a required citation format.

## What is actually open

**CC BY 4.0 International**, verified from the primary data policy page (landmatrix.org/data-policy) — attribution, indication of modifications, link-back, no endorsement implication; third-party photos/documents may carry other licences. The dataset is deliberately bounded: **systematic near-census collection covers transnational deals in agriculture and forestry only**; "green deals" (wind, solar, carbon) collection began 2024 and is incomplete; other sectors appear on the site but are not systematically collected.

## Governance and control

Steering committee of five partners — CDE (University of Bern), CIRAD, GIGA Hamburg, GIZ, and the International Land Coalition — plus four Regional Focal Points (AFA/Asia, Ecoaction/Eastern Europe, FUNDAPAZ/Latin America, RECONCILE/Africa with ISRA-BAME). GIGA maintains the global database; CDE the geo-referenced data. Funded by BMZ, SDC, the European Commission and partners. Established 2009; first database version launched April 2012.

## Evidence of use

4,398 deals recorded (site counter, 2026-09-04, including intended/failed deals); a concluded-deals-only figure of 1,785 deals / >50M ha circulates via partner ILC — per-definition figures, not a single headline. The dataset is the standard reference in land-grab scholarship and journalism, with documented limitations: its own FAQ concedes verification difficulty, biases, and constant revision; academic work (e.g., *Journal of Peasant Studies*, 2013) documents media-source reliance and coverage bias while treating it as the best available dataset on international land acquisitions.

## Why it belongs

The land cell's one case of a genuinely open-licensed, bulk-downloadable dataset about land control — and it exists because a donor-funded coalition built it outside any state. It demonstrates both the value of deal-level transparency and its epistemic limits (verification gaps that registries like BC's LOTR avoid by legal force, but at the price of being search-not-bulk and jurisdiction-bound). Pairs with OpenLandContracts (contract transparency) and Cadasta (community documentation) as the donor-governed transparency stack that substitutes for absent state openness.

## What it does not demonstrate

A complete global census (systematic only for transnational ag/forestry); Canada relevance (no Canadian deals systematically collected — domestic Canadian land deals fall entirely outside its scope and outside any Canadian equivalent).
