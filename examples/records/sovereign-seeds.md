# Sovereign Seeds

- Status: `lead`
- Region / reach: Canada; national Indigenous-led initiative
- Project: https://www.sovereignseeds.org/
- External organisational description: https://www.tamtrust.org/grant-partner/sovereign-seeds/
- Field-guide context: Canadian high-sensitivity discovery lead; not transferred from the Agrifood AI Field Guide

## Problem addressed

Sovereign Seeds works on the revitalisation of Indigenous seed and agricultural food sovereignty across Canada. The available descriptions connect this work to cultural agriculture, seed stewardship, food systems, learning, leadership, production, and entrepreneurship.

## Open layer

This is not currently classified as an open-source project. Its relevance to the collection is through community-controlled biological and knowledge commons, Indigenous governance, seed access, and anti-extractive food-system practice.

## What is actually evidenced

Sovereign Seeds’ own public site presents the work through the headings “seeds,” “cultures,” “learners,” “leaders,” “producers,” and “entrepreneurs.” A funder profile describes it as a by-and-for Indigenous initiative working across Canada since 2019 through knowledge transmission, training, mentorship, seed access, leadership amplification, cultural agriculture education, cultural seed revitalisation and distribution, and cultural farm and food entrepreneurship.

## Governance and control

The by-and-for Indigenous framing is central evidence, not a synonym for open licensing. Any future record must follow the organisation’s own language and protocols, and must not infer that cultural seed materials, teachings, data, or imagery are available for unrestricted reuse.

## Evidence of use

The external organisational description reports programming for Indigenous communities, food learners, leaders, practitioners, and producers. More direct evidence from Sovereign Seeds about current programmes, communities, protocols, and outcomes is still needed.

## Maintenance and funding

The organisation has a public website and appears in funder and partner profiles. Its organisational status, current funding model, and long-term programme infrastructure require direct verification.

## Why it matters

Sovereign Seeds is potentially the most important Canadian lead for testing the limits of the collection’s “open” vocabulary. It may represent a sovereignty-oriented commons whose legitimacy depends on control, consent, cultural protocol, and reciprocity rather than public release.

## What it does not demonstrate

A public website, seed access, or a by-and-for description does not establish that seed materials, knowledge, or data are open. Nor should the project be evaluated by imposing open-source criteria that conflict with its own governance obligations.

## Verification boundary

Do not promote this lead to a curated record until direct, community-controlled sources establish what the organisation wants publicly represented, which materials or practices are shareable, and what consent and governance conditions apply.
