# Island Grown Farmers Cooperative (IGFC) mobile slaughter unit

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Washington State, US (San Juan Islands / Puget Sound / Skagit Valley); the reference model cited in Canadian mobile-abattoir work
- Project: https://www.igfcmeats.com/
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the processing sectoral scan (G-OSA-33, `research/2026-08-processing-sectoral-scan.md` sub-cell 1); verified 2026-09-02

## Problem addressed

Small producers in island and remote regions without a local kill floor. IGFC's mobile slaughter unit (MSU) brought USDA-inspected slaughter to the farm gate: the first FSIS-inspected mobile red-meat slaughter unit in the United States, operating since 2002, paired with a cut-and-wrap facility and now a fixed plant.

## Open layer

Open governance (cooperative ownership) and a widely replicated operating model. This is deliberately recorded as a governance case, not an open-hardware case: the unit's engineering drawings were never published under an open licence.

## What is actually open

Nothing technical is open-licensed. Bruce Dunlop of IGFC designed the unit (working commercially with trailer maker Featherlite); the design is held privately, and no repository, drawings, or HACCP plan documents were released under any open licence. What travels is the *model* — replicated through USDA extension documents, the Niche Meat Processors Assistance Network case studies (which publish costs, capacity, and lessons as descriptive text), and direct consultancy. Replication happened by copying, not by open licensing.

## Governance and control

Farmer-owned service cooperative formed in 2002 specifically to purchase and operate the unit from the Lopez Community Land Trust. ~30 founding members ($600 initial capital charge each); 65 members by 2011; volunteer member board meets monthly; expansions self-financed via member loans and equity retains rather than external debt; 95% of members use services at least yearly; non-members served at higher rates.

## Evidence of use

Operating since 2002; broke even in its first year. New 4,500 sq ft processing facility at the Port of Skagit (Burlington) completed 2023. The mobile service was suspended for 8+ months in 2024 due to skilled-labour shortage, with USDA-inspected island service resuming from Q1 2025 (Salish Current, 2024-12-05). Current island-service frequency not directly confirmed by a primary source at verification.

## Maintenance and funding

Initial build (~$150k, 2000) via USDA grants obtained by the Lopez Community Land Trust plus ~$80-90k private donations — no initial debt. Self-sustaining on fee-for-service revenue since 2002. The main ongoing constraint is labour, not capital or governance.

## Why it belongs

IGFC is the anchor open-governance case of the processing cell: it demonstrates that cooperative ownership can hold capital-intensive, licence-gated slaughter infrastructure for two decades where proprietary and state models fail — while also demonstrating, negatively, that even a celebrated replicable model produced no open artifacts. Its replication across the US mobile-slaughter movement and its citation in Canadian debates travelled through case studies and word of mouth, not licences. It pairs with the Underground Meats record: two processing-openness attempts, one organizational (durable, no open assets), one documentary (CC0-licensed, host dead).

## What it does not demonstrate

That openness, in any technical sense, had a role: the open layer here is ownership structure. The MSU design is proprietary; the HACCP plan is plant-specific; the operating knowledge is tacit. It also does not demonstrate scalability beyond its region, or that the co-op form solves the labour constraint.
