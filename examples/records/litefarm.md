## Problem addressed

LiteFarm is a free and open-source farm-management tool for current and aspiring sustainable farmers. It supports farm operations, production records, finances, and related management work for diversified, agroecological, and organic farms.

## Open layer

Open-source farm-management software, public source code, community contribution, farmer/researcher co-design, and a documented API.

## What is actually open

The public GitHub repository identifies a GNU GPLv3 licence. LiteFarm’s contributor page links to the repository, technical guidance, product guidance, a contributor wiki, and API documentation. The official site says the project is developed in cooperation with researchers and software developers at the University of British Columbia and contributors.

## Canadian connection

LiteFarm is coordinated by the University of British Columbia and is a Canadian-connected project with international reach. The Canadian connection should not be treated as evidence that its users, governance, or deployments are primarily Canadian.

## Governance and control

The project describes itself as community-led, not-for-profit, and built by farmers and researchers. Its public materials support a co-design and participation claim, but do not by themselves establish farmer control over project governance, hosted data, or product priorities.

## Evidence of use

The official site reports use in more than 155 countries, more than 10,000 users, and more than 7,500 farms. These are project-reported figures rather than independently audited adoption statistics. The public repository showed active development, including a visible commit on 2026-07-31.

## Maintenance and funding

The project has a public repository, contributor documentation, support channels, a product suggestion portal, and a UBC research and development anchor. Long-term funding, hosting economics, and the relationship between research funding and operational maintenance require further verification.

## Why it matters

LiteFarm is the strongest bridge between the existing international open-farm-software coverage and the Canadian ecosystem. It adds a farmer-facing, non-AI example with a Canadian university anchor and explicit participatory design language.

## What it does not demonstrate

A GPL licence and community-led description do not establish farmer ownership, unrestricted hosted-data access, equitable global deployment, or durable financial sustainability.
