# DFO aquaculture and sea-lice open reporting (BC datasets)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Canada (federal; BC site-level, national aggregate)
- Project: https://open.canada.ca/ (DFO datasets; see Sources)
- Field-guide context: new discovery via the fisheries/aquaculture sectoral scan (G-OSA-34, sub-cell 4); verified 2026-09-04

## Problem addressed

Open-net pen salmon farming is Canada's most contested food-production controversy, and its environmental performance (sea lice, fish health, transfers, antimicrobial use, mortality) is the evidence base of that debate. DFO publishes licence-condition reporting as open data — the regulatory environment's transparency layer.

## Open layer

Open government data: five datasets, all verified on open.canada.ca under the **Open Government Licence – Canada** (ca-ogl-lgo), CSV-based (national reporting also XLSX).

## What is actually open — asset by asset

1. **DFO sea lice audits of BC marine finfish aquaculture sites** (5cfd93bd-b3ee-4b0b-8816-33d388f6811d): quarterly random DFO audits; farm staff count lice on 50% of sampled fish, DFO staff the other half; data 2011 onward; farm/site-level.
2. **Industry sea lice counts at BC marine finfish sites** (3cafbe89-c98b-4b44-88f1-594e8d28838d): monthly industry abundance counts showing active farms; 2011 onward; farm/site-level.
3. **Sea lice mitigation events at BC salmon farms** (fdba4d10-…): in-feed treatments, mechanical removal, medicinal/non-medicinal baths, harvest; 2011 onward; farm-level.
4. **Managing transfers and fish health at BC salmon farms** (700fe290-7653-49e1-b961-741dc1ead924, quarterly): Fish Health Management Plans, salmon transfers, monthly sea-lice abundance, antimicrobial use, mortality; 2015 onward; links to related datasets (fish health events, DFO fish health audits, mortality).
5. **National Aquaculture Public Reporting Data** (288b6dc4-16dc-43cc-80a4-2a45b1f93383, annual): industry submissions under the Aquaculture Activities Regulations; marine finfish + land-based/freshwater sector files (2022 and 2024 released) covering Atlantic and Pacific Canada — coarser than the BC licence-condition datasets.

Correction to the scan note: granularity is **farm/site-level throughout** — the "farm→pen" progression in earlier reporting was not found in the open datasets. Coverage starts **2011** (transfers/fish health 2015), not ~2010. Portal frequency metadata says "as_needed" even where reporting is monthly/quarterly — cite the notes' cadence.

## Governance and control

Federal (DFO). Data is generated by industry operators under licence conditions, quality-checked and republished by DFO. Licence conditions are enforceable: 48-hour reporting is required when motile *L. salmonis* exceeds 3 per fish during the March-June out-migration window (DFO BC marine finfish monitoring report).

## Evidence of use

The datasets are the official farmed sea-lice source cited by the research community: Revie et al. (2025), *Scientific Data* 12:1331, harmonized 2001-2023 wild + farmed sea-lice observations (~10,000 monthly farm estimates across ~100 farms in seven DFO fish-health zones; >365,000 wild hosts from ~17,000 sampling events) into a **CC BY 4.0** dataset on figshare (doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.28078100) — because the official data, while open, is heterogeneous enough in format and granularity that researchers must rebuild it before it is usable. That harmonization layer is the record's analytical point: the transparency is real; the interoperability is not.

## Why it belongs

Canada's strongest aquaculture open-data layer — and the contrast case within one government: sea-lice and fish-health reporting is open under OGL-Canada, while the same department keeps quota holdings, catch records, and licence-holder registries confidential (Marine Policy 2022). The cell's Canadian verdict in one department: open *watching*, closed *operations*. It pairs with the CGC grading-data record (open documents, no interoperability) and the processing cell's absent CFIA abattoir dataset (no open registry at all).

## What it does not demonstrate

Machine-readability at scale (CSV files, no API or documented schema; portal frequency metadata even mislabels the cadence). Pen-level granularity. Atlantic-Canada site-level sea-lice depth (that comes only via the coarser national AAR reporting). Licence-decision openness — the Discovery Islands phase-out and 2029 open-net-pen phase-out exist as announcements and court records, not as structured open data about the sector's most consequential licensing events.
