# Nebraska LB525 — Agricultural Data Act (2026)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (Nebraska; first-of-kind state statute)
- Project: statute via National Agricultural Law Center-hosted analysis
- Live docs: https://nationalaglawcenter.org/wp-content/uploads//assets/Webinars/Ag%20Data%20Rights%20NALC%2010.21.2026.pdf
- Field-guide context: the C5 pass's statutory hook — the first law making agricultural data the producer's protected property
- Last checked: 2026-11-19

## Problem addressed

Before 2026, agricultural data ownership existed only in vendor terms of service —
the contract layer was the only layer. Nebraska made farm data a property right.

## Open layer

Public statute (verified through the NALC/ArentFox Schiff CLE slides; statute text
not directly fetched — recorded as second-hand): "an agricultural producer owns and
controls agricultural data originating from the producer's farm, land, device, or
equipment," with sale of that data requiring separate express written consent.

## What is actually open (verified from the CLE analysis, 2026-11-19)

- The pre-2026 baseline, verbatim: "Until Nebraska enacted LB525, no state or federal
  law specifically addressed ownership of or rights in agricultural data."
- The statute's consent architecture follows the *producer*, not the land — what
  that means at death or farm sale is an untested question the succession program
  records.

## Governance and control

Nebraska Legislature; analysis hosted by the National Agricultural Law Center.
Missouri and Iowa noted as pending in the same analysis.

## Evidence of use

First enacted state ag-data property statute; the reference point for the emerging
state statutory layer.

## Maintenance and funding

State law; CLE analysis by ArentFox Schiff.

## Why it belongs

C5 needed a legal-structural anchor for farm-data continuity at transfer, and this
is the first one anywhere: if data follows the producer by statute, the succession
question changes from "what do the terms say" to "what happens to a producer-owned
data right at transfer" — a question no vendor, contract, or statute yet answers.

## What it does not demonstrate

Section-level consent mechanics (statute text not fetched); application at death or
sale; transferability outside Nebraska; any effect on the documented absence of
handover processes.
