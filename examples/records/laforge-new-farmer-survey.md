# Laforge et al. — Beginning farmers' entry barriers (Canadian Food Studies)

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: Canada (national survey, 1,326 respondents)
- Project: https://canadianfoodstudies.uwaterloo.ca/index.php/cfs/article/download/288/296/1653
- Live docs: same (open-access journal article)
- Field-guide context: the C4 pass's Canadian demand-side evidence — the largest surveyed account of what actually blocks new-entrant farming in Canada
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

The entry-cost debate is usually conducted on land-value aggregates. This survey asks
new, aspiring, and exited farmers directly what blocks them.

## Open layer

Open-access journal article (Canadian Food Studies / La Revue canadienne des études
sur l'alimentation); explicit licence not stated on the article page.

## What is actually open (verified as reported from the article, subagent pass)

- Survey of **1,326 new, aspiring, and exited farmers**.
- The two dominant barriers: "**The most significant barriers concerned affordable
  land and financing their early farm businesses**" — land and early-stage financing,
  not technology, education, or equipment access.

## Governance and control

Academic authors (Laforge et al.); open-access journal.

## Evidence of use

The national demand-side complement to the land scan's supply-side data (land values,
operator ages) — cited in the succession program's C4 pass as the verified evidence
that the entry problem is capital-shaped, which relocates the program's centre of
gravity to tenure, financing, and visibility.

## Maintenance and funding

Journal publication; survey date per the article.

## Why it belongs

It is the Canadian evidence that the entry floor is land-and-finance-shaped — the
finding that keeps the succession program honest about what open technology can and
cannot do (C4's resolution), and the survey-method precedent for the measurement gaps
C10 recorded (roles described, not measured).

## What it does not demonstrate

Itemized startup costs (the survey measures barriers, not a cost table); the
open-technology share of entry cost; year-fresh data.
