# Of Women and Land: How Gender Affects Successions and Transfers of Iowa Farms

- Status: `curated`
- Region / reach: United States (Iowa survey; US generalizing)
- Project: https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/4b02b1e1-942b-47ba-82cc-4025f299a72b/content
- Live docs: CARD working paper 22-WP 631 (published in *Applied Economic Perspectives and Policy*)
- Field-guide context: the strongest quantitative evidence found on gender in farm succession — son-preference measured directly, and the transferor's gender shown to shape successor choice
- Last checked: 2026-11-18

## Problem addressed

Farm succession's patriarch-to-son default is widely asserted and rarely measured.
This study measures it: successor choice by the transferor's gender, using Iowa
survey data.

## Open layer

Open-access working paper with stated reproduction-with-attribution permission
(verified in the PDF).

## What is actually open (verified from the PDF, 2026-11-18)

- **Son-preference, quantified:** "Among those with identified successors,
  **57% chose sons and 8% chose daughters**. We find a 12.4% probability a female
  farmer will choose a daughter, but only 5.9% for a male farmer." Female farmers
  are twice as likely to pass to a daughter — succession power follows the
  transferor's gender.
- Via the USDA TOTAL survey (reported within the paper): **37% of US non-operator
  principal landowners are women**, and land owned by women non-operators skews
  old (76% controlled by someone over 65) — a large, aging, female-controlled
  farmland layer entering the transfer wave.

## Why it belongs

It converts the program's gender question from anecdote to measurement: the
default is real (7:1 sons-over-daughters among male-identified successors' choices)
but not fixed — the transferor's gender doubles the daughter-choice probability.
For the succession program, it is the empirical warrant for designing the
adaptation commons and transfer vehicles for female-controlled succession rather
than encoding the patriarch-to-son default. It also documents the US women-
landowner layer (37% of non-operator principal landowners) that the Canadian data
cannot yet describe.

## What it does not demonstrate

Canadian applicability (Iowa survey); causal mechanisms behind son-preference;
non-operator decision-making power; the widow-control hypothesis.
