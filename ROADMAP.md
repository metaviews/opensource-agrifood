# Roadmap

Last reviewed: 2026-10-09. This is the single place that says where the project is, what is finished, what is open, and what comes next. It replaces the "current phase" prose that used to live in the README. Update it whenever a workstream changes state; the README only points here.

## The project in one paragraph

Open Source Agrifood is a research and knowledge-building project asking who controls the systems — hardware, data, standards, models, seeds, governance — that make up open agrifood, and who benefits. It has three workstreams that share a repository but have different tempos: the **research corpus** (records, scans, syntheses, register), the **grocery concept** (a concept-development space that stress-tests an open grocery operation against real cases), and the **event** (*Building the Open-Source Future of Canadian Agrifood*, a Future Herd spectacular event, Tuesday 24 November 2026, Discord). The event is a beneficiary of the research, not its organizing purpose.

Ten proposals, each with precedent, failure mode and test; five require statutory change, five can be done administratively.
- Support layer mapped (2026-09-11, at the user's direction): `upcycling/research/cfin-and-support-programmes.md`. CFIN is a national member network (26.3K+ members and followers; $23.7M awarded across 130+ projects; five Regional Innovation Directors) whose YODL platform is a searchable directory of co-packers, suppliers, consultants and lab spaces — three of the concept's outstanding questions in one place, free to join. None of its three funding programmes funds infrastructure; Foodtech Next funds demonstrations in operational environments, which makes a community pilot a plausible funded *host site* rather than an applicant. The Foodtech in Canada ecosystem report is gated to a paid tier, consistent with the project's recurring finding that the sector's own information is sold. Food 4.0 turns out to be CFIN's *proposed* action plan rather than enacted policy — but it carries the industry-side statement that food is "largely absent from Canada's core innovation agenda," which the policy document now cites instead of asserting the premise itself.
- Dairy case study written (2026-09-19, at the user's direction): `dairy/open-milking-robot-case-study.md` — a viability case for an open milking robot against a quota-capped ~80-cow Ontario farm, with a provenance-labelled cost spine (commercial robot pricing is aggregator-sourced and unverified; the maintenance figure contradicts across sources and the contradiction is recorded), five savings mechanisms grounded in the supply-management frame, a staged-openness ladder, an honesty clause naming Stage 3 hardware reciprocity as the droppable rung, and a "who to ask" list (Ontario Dairy Research Centre/Guelph, OMAFRA, dealer principals, ICAR, dairy co-ops). G-OSA-26's finding that open dairy automation is completely absent from open repos is the novelty premise; two adjacent repos licence-checked via GitHub API (MilkIt GPL-2.0; a no-licence dormant DeLaval analysis repo recorded as not open).

- CEA case study premise written (2026-09-21, at the user's direction): `cea/open-climate-control-case-study.md` — a viability case, not a scan (no G-ID), citing G-OSA-22, G-OSA-39, G-OSA-36, G-OSA-38, the land scan and the definition document as prior evidence. - Data-center heat case study written (2026-10-07, at the user's direction): `cea/data-center-heat-case-study.md` — a framed case on data-center waste heat as a CEA energy input, opened by a source pass rather than a G-OSA scan (no G-ID; absence claims recorded as unscanned). Three tiers of evidence: two formal feasibility studies (Virginia/RII June 2025 — the load-bearing source: one DC + one greenhouse does not work on heat-load mismatch, the Farm Park / district-exchange model is the workable form, grade decides feasibility at 30–40°C air-cooled vs 45–55°C water-cooled, and Virginia's full DC waste heat models to 6,000–8,500 acres of greenhouse; Legendary Harvest/RII–NDSU–Applied Digital in North Dakota), operating installations (Green Mountain → trout farm, Luleå and Notre Dame greenhouses, Nordic district heating, the Uithoorn/Greenport Aalsmeer/Switch/Liander multi-party grower heat network, Tencent Tianjin, the Huailai demonstration with unverified one-year break-even), and two Canadian announcements held as plan-stage (SPUR Innovation's Fergus/Woolwich farm-over-server campus, already moved 25→14 acres; Q-Scale Lévis with company-claim tonnages) plus two design concepts explicitly not counted as evidence (Helsinki Hybrid; Thermalize/Everflow self-labelled pre-pilot illustration). The physics finding that matters for the corpus: **a greenhouse is a heat sink, an indoor vertical farm is a net heat source** — the DC-heat serving-CEA story is greenhouse and aquaculture, not racking. Relation to the existing CEA case: an energy-side companion at the commercial/district rung only — it explicitly does not rescue rung B (northern communities have no data centers) and does not touch the rung-B cost spine or honesty clause; what transfers is the §4 design thesis, since heat-hosted climate control is the clearest economic justification for a forkable control profile that trades heat/light/power. Five openness candidates named for a future scan (heat/host matching registry, licence-clear paired measurement, heat-host control profiles, a waste-heat extension on the CGO standard, and the risk that the pairing forms closed by default through SDE++ contracts, EED reporting and proprietary skids), each explicitly hypothesis-grade. Six viability targets recorded, of which the first two are the load-bearing pair: one published MW-thermal-in/kilograms-out measurement, and one open-licensed heat-host control profile. A source-quality note is recorded: the SEO market-research pages on this topic contradict internally, so the ~1 MW ≈ 2–3 ha rule of thumb is carried as unverified.

- Wiki Agri Tech open-tool-catalogue record added (2026-10-08, at the user's direction, after a ducksize.com coverage audit of both projects): `examples/records/wiki-agri-tech.md` — the Aspexit-origin catalogue of agricultural digital tools, surfaced from ducksize's "Open-source catalog for agriculture" post, whose cited URL (`lesoutilsnumeriquesdesagriculteurs.com/en/`) now returns 404. Openness is recorded asset by asset — data open, editorial share-alike non-commercial, source code not located — which is why it enters as `candidate` rather than `curated`. Its own counters disagree across pages (1,916 meta title / 2,573 open-data pages / 2,731 tools directory / "+2500" on aspexit.com) and are recorded as disagreement, not adopted; no repository, bulk dump or adoption figures found. Indexed in `examples/catalog.md` and `examples/README.md`; collection now 129 records on disk.
