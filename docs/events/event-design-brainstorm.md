# Open Source Agrifood event — design brainstorm

Status: brainstorm, working draft. Not a brief. Not a commitment.

Date: 2026-08-05
Hosts: future-herd/ salon programme
Project: opensource-agrifood/ (research clearing house / observatory to be built)
Format: online, Discord, half-day, four sessions, salon-style, situationist
Outputs: Future Herd podcast episodes + open agrifood clearing house / observatory

## What is already settled (do not relitigate)

- **Platform: Discord**, designed as curated participatory theatre (Aug 1 venue scan). Use that document's spatial grammar: foyer, main stage, backstage/green room, audience discussion, breakout spaces, roles, text-as-marginalia, bots/webhooks as cues, scheduled events as time framing, persistent commons afterward.
- **Jurisdiction: Canada.** Institutional partners, participants, policy framing, and regulatory references are Canadian. This is a Canada-focused event, not a global one. Future events may broaden scope. The Global South currents in the research base remain reference material, not the event's centre of gravity.
- **Timeline: half-day, mid-fall 2026** (late October or early November). Exact day-of-week and time-of-day still open.
- **Session order (locked): agents → research → ethics/policy → open source.** Reasoning: agentic AI is the live transformative surface and the audience's entry point; research grounds it with credible Canadian researchers already part of the Future Herd network; ethics/policy follows because it engages what the room has just seen; open source closes because that is where the action is and the closer should earn the after-hours commons.
- **Three mandatory sessions:** agents, research, open source. Ethics/policy is conditional — see "The ethics/policy question" below.
- **Format: salons, not panels.** A salon is a facilitated spontaneous conversation. The format lives or dies on facilitation, not on a pre-set agenda. Pre-circulated materials are fine; pre-circulated talking points are not. The event design must avoid building a panel with extra steps.
- **Role-play / constructed situation is on the table** as a real option for one session, not as a metaphor. It can either replace ethics/policy or sit inside it.
- **Agents session is oriented around a current from the landscape** — the contested terrain in Canadian agrifood, not the abstract topic.
- **Farmers are invitees, not subjects.** Accessibility is the design principle; fetishization is the failure mode. Detailed discussion of farmer participation deferred until format and framing are firmer.
- **The research base exists:** 58 verified records, 13 scan docs, 21 gaps, 4 contested claims. The headline finding — openness is layered, the layers decouple, and farmer agency is the scarcest layer — is load-bearing.
- **Salon precedent:** the 2026-08-25 "Feed the People!" salon is a usable rehearsal. It establishes: 90-min rhythm, two-presence model (salon participants on audio/video, public viewers on stream), RSVP-by-email workflow, manual moderation, publisher link not Future Herd position, no fixed agenda promised.
- **Situationist tendencies:** SAM manifesto frames the broader practice. The salon is one mode — there is no obligation to import the whole SAM tactical repertoire.

## The ethics/policy question

Three readings of what ethics/policy could become:

1. **A salon like the others.** A facilitated conversation among ethicists, regulators, civil-society groups, and practitioners. Risk: attracts the people who already talk about ethics/policy. Audience gets smaller and more predictable. The conversation ends in abstractions the room already shares.

2. **A situation / role-play session.** The salon is a constructed scene — a hearing, an inquiry, a tribunal, a futures negotiation, a public consultation. The situation *performs* the ethics and policy. Outputs are transcripts, draft documents, testimony, or a public letter. This is where role-play earns its place — not as theatre, but as a research instrument that surfaces what a panel would smooth over.

3. **Distributed across the other three.** Ethics embedded in the agents session (who decides what an agent is allowed to recommend to a farmer? who is liable when it's wrong?), policy embedded in the open source session (what policy would we champion? what would we actively resist?), method embedded in the research session (CARE, Indigenous data sovereignty, consent in practice). The "fourth session" slot is then freed for something only a salon can do.

My instinct: **option 3 distributes, but option 2 is the memorable thing.** A hybrid is plausible — the agents session opens with the ethics frame ("what is an agent allowed to do?"), the open source session closes with the policy frame ("what would we ask the federal government to do?"), and the role-play session sits in slot 3 as the situation. This honours your "three mandatory, ethics/policy conditional" framing by *embedding* ethics/policy inside the mandatory sessions and giving the situation a stage. The role-play then functions less as a panel replacement and more as the connective tissue between agents (what is happening) and open source (what we should be doing).

Whether ethics/policy is *replaced* by the situation, or whether the situation *contains* ethics/policy, is itself a design choice we don't need to make yet. Worth noting: a role-play without an ethical or political frame risks being clever for its own sake. A role-play *with* one risks becoming a lecture.

## What's missing from the four-session frame (re-ranked for Canada, half-day, this order)

### 1. Farmers and field practitioners as co-equal participants
Your research headline — *farmer agency is the scarcest layer* — is the structural argument. A Canada-focused open-agrifood salon where farmers are not in the room, on mic, on equal terms, reproduces the problem the event is meant to surface. Means: stipends or honoraria for non-institutional participants; scheduling that respects harvest and farm-work rhythms (which in late October is *tight* — late harvest, fall fieldwork, equipment prep); low-bandwidth and audio-only paths; no assumption of institutional email or video-camera norms; support for first-time-Discord users. Mid-November is safer than late October for farmer availability — worth flagging.

### 2. The clearing house built *during* the event
The clearing house is more credible if it is born in the salons, not assembled after. Every dossier entry cited in a session becomes a record on the spot; the contested-claims ledger is updated live; the gap register gains entries the salon surfaces; the open source session produces a structured artifact (a draft memorandum, an open letter, a forked taxonomy, a candidates list) the post-event commons continues to work on. The salon is a research instrument; the podcast is one of several honest records.

### 3. Canada's own current — not just a Canadian reading of global currents
The landscape synthesis v2 names seven currents. Which one is most distinctively *Canadian*? Candidates: the open-core + paid-services hybrid (LiteFarm at UBC, Ekylibre-style FMIS, AgExpert); the commons-governance-without-licences current (Canadian cooperative infrastructure, CUMIS-style federations, the Saskatchewan Wheat Pool lineage); the regulation-as-access current (CFIA, Canadian Grain Commission, AAFC programs, the July 2026 FTC/Deere precedent's Canadian echo). The Canadian-ness of the salon depends on which current the agents session orients around, and which the open source session grounds itself in.

### 4. Language and access as first-class design constraint
Canada is officially bilingual. French-language participation is a baseline, not a feature. At minimum: transcripts as canonical artifacts (every session produces one); interpretation for at least one session; text chat as a fully featured participation path, not a back-channel; low-bandwidth and voice-only testing. This also lets the situation be honest about which voices it actually centers.

### 5. The Discord server itself as a curated environment
The server is the artwork, not the platform. Decisions to make early:
- Channel structure: small, legible, plain-language labels.
- Roles: audience, salon participant, speaker, facilitator, observer-from-the-field, moderator, archivist. Each role has a visible purpose.
- Onboarding: a single short text-channel walkthrough that takes a first-time participant from "I just clicked the link" to "I understand where the salon happens and where I can talk."
- Marginalia: a parallel text channel functioning as live notes and citations. Cite as you speak. The transcripts become primary documents.
- A #commons channel that survives the salons. The observatory is born here.
- Avoid: bots, gamification, badges, default notification noise, gaming-era jargon.

### 6. Production infrastructure for a half-day
Half-day intensifies the load: four sessions, fewer transitions, higher per-session energy. Realistic minimum: 2–3 production humans per session (stage runner, green-room host, marginalia/writer, chat moderator). The triad: transcripts → podcast, live notes → research base, chat logs → observatory. Without this triad, the event collapses into a stream and a podcast.

### 7. The podcast as one output, not the event
Future Herd podcast episodes are one artifact. Others: published transcript per session; curated dossier per session; research-question update; a post-event public letter or situation report; an updated landscape synthesis v3 (the salon surfaces new gaps). If podcast is the only output, the salon becomes a content farm. If the salon is treated as a research instrument, the podcast becomes one of several honest records.

### 8. Ethics, safety, and consent architecture
Especially with farmer participants and Indigenous data-sovereignty discussions: recording consent, attribution norms, the right to be quoted vs. the right to be heard, the right to speak off-record in the green room, the difference between public text and salon speech. This is a structural design decision, not a footnote.

### 9. Money
Stipends or honoraria for non-institutional participants, transcription and translation, podcast production, Discord boost (if needed for stage-video capacity), accessibility contingency. The event's openness claim depends on whether people who are not already paid to be in these conversations can show up.

### 10. Naming and invitations
Salon culture lives or dies on its title and its first-page invitation. "Open Source Agrifood Salon #1: Agents" is a programme. "Feeding the Algorithm," "The Sealed Source," "Who Counts the Yield," "Commoning the Commons" are situations. The title should signal that something is going to be examined, not merely discussed.

## The "agents as transformative change" framing

Your framing — agentic AI is where transformative change is currently happening — is the conventional one. Worth examining because the salon will inherit it.

Agrifood has been told transformative change is "currently happening" about every technology since precision agriculture in the 1990s. The salon gains something if the framing is *less certain* — not "agents will transform agrifood" but "agents are being deployed in Canadian agrifood; here is what is real, what is hype, and what the room thinks we should be paying attention to." That posture is more honest and more situationist — it opens the conversation rather than confirms the premise. It also leaves room for the room to disagree with the framing itself, which is a salonier outcome than agreement.

This is worth deciding early because the agents session's title and invitation will follow from it.

## Open framing questions (not format decisions)

These are the questions worth pressing on before we move to format and operations:

1. **What is the Canadian current?** Which of the seven currents from the landscape synthesis is most distinctively Canadian, and should anchor the salon? (See #3 above.)
2. **What is the role-play session trying to *do*?** Generate an artifact? Surface disagreement? Produce testimony? Negotiate a draft document? The goal determines the form. A role-play without a goal is theatre; a role-play with one is a research instrument.
3. **What is the clearing house in concrete terms by the end of the four salons?** A dossier library? An updated landscape synthesis v3? A federated observatory? A podcast-and-corpus bundle? An open letter to AAFC? The artifact determines the production requirements.
4. **Who is in the room, in what proportions?** Researchers, technologists, farmers, co-op staff, public-interest journalists, policy people, civil-society groups, Indigenous seed keepers, vendor counsel, public servants. The proportions are the politics. Until we name the target proportions, every other choice is provisional.
5. **What is the one thing we want a participant to remember in 12 months?** Not "agents will transform agrifood" or "open source is good." A sharper proposition: e.g., "the layers decouple" / "the salon is where the contested terrain gets named" / "the clearing house is a thing I helped build." The answer shapes every other choice.
6. **What does the salon owe the participants?** A sense of having been in a room worth being in; a relationship that continues; an artifact they can use; compensation for their time; a record they helped produce. The answer changes who shows up and how.

## What we don't need to decide yet

- Exact day-of-week or time-of-day within the half-day.
- Format option (A/B/C/D blend from earlier brainstorm) — depends on framing.
- Production role chart — depends on framing and format.
- Public invitation copy — depends on title and framing.
- Budget — depends on participation model.

These can wait until the framing questions above have sharper answers. Rushing them is how salons turn into webinars.

## What to do next (in this order)

1. **Converge on the Canadian current** that anchors the salon. Read landscape synthesis v2 with this question in mind: which current is most distinctively Canadian, most generative for a salon, and most likely to produce material worth a clearing house?
2. **Decide the role-play goal.** What artifact or experience is the constructed situation trying to produce? The goal drives the form.
3. **Sketch the clearing house as an output of the salons**, not as a separate workstream. What specifically should exist on the other side of the half-day?
4. **Sketch the participant mix.** Target proportions for the room. This determines invitations, compensation, scheduling, language.
5. **Draft the title and one-paragraph invitation.** Once the Canadian current and the role-play goal are clear, the title will suggest itself.
6. **Then** move to format, cadence, server topology, production, and budget.
