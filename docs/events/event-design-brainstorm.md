# Open Source Agrifood event — design brainstorm

Status: brainstorm, working draft. Not a brief. Not a commitment.

Date: 2026-08-05
Hosts: future-herd/ salon programme
Project: opensource-agrifood/ (research clearing house / observatory to be built — concept itself deferred, not a deliverable to design toward at this stage)
Format: online, Discord, half-day, multi-session, salon-style, situationist
Outputs: Future Herd podcast episodes + whatever else emerges

## Sources of truth and how to use them

Three corpora are available; each plays a different role.

- **future-herd/ — authoritative.** First-person interviews, episode transcripts, knowledge pieces, intelligence briefs, the operational platform itself. This is what the room actually sounds like, what guests and topics have already landed, and what the salon inherits by being hosted here.
- **agrifood-ai-field-guide/ — structural reference for content framing.** 219 units across the full food value chain (inputs, on-farm, post-harvest, processing, distribution/retail, consumption, waste recovery), three-axis taxonomy, eight archetypes, 42 quotes, 35 scans, gap and lead registries. Useful for understanding where topics sit in the agrifood domain and what framings already exist. Explicitly not on-farm-only — covers the whole chain.
- **opensource-agrifood/ — context for the open-source ecosystem and marketplace.** 58 records, 13 scan docs, landscape synthesis v2, contested-claims ledger. Useful as one specific lens, not as the spine.

The event design should lean primarily on future-herd/ material (interview voices, what the room already responds to), use the field guide as a structural map of the agrifood domain (especially the food-side coverage I had been missing), and reach into opensource-agrifood/ only when the open-source question itself becomes a session.

## What is settled

- Platform: Discord, designed as curated participatory theatre (Aug 1 venue scan).
- Jurisdiction: Canada. Institutional partners, participants, policy framing, regulatory references are Canadian.
- Timeline: half-day, mid-fall 2026 (late October or early November). Exact day and time open.
- Salons, not panels. Facilitated spontaneous conversation; the format lives or dies on facilitation, not on a pre-set agenda.
- Role-play / constructed situation is in play for one session; the goal is edutainment and a memorable experience. The role-play does not have to produce an artifact. Drama and gravity are part of the brief.
- Farmers are invitees, not subjects. Accessibility is the design principle; fetishization is the failure mode. Detailed discussion deferred until format and participants take shape.

## What is open and worth keeping open

- **Session count.** Three or four. Both are credible.
- **Session order.** Not locked. The order should be a design choice, not a default.
- **Session topics.** The four you've been working with (agents/applications, research, open source, ethics/policy) are one starting set. The food side of agrifood (post-harvest, processing, distribution, retail, consumption, waste) is currently underweighted and worth weighing against the four. The field guide's archetype set (vendor-sweep / data-sovereignty / adoption-diagnosis / cooperative-alternative / critical-lens-Indigenous / regional-cluster-comparison / open-source + smallholder + multilateral / AI and labour) is another way to slice the territory.
- **Whether ethics/policy is a session, a frame, or both.** Your instinct — ethics/policy embedded in the role-play — adds drama and gravity. That's a legitimate design move on its own terms, separate from whether "ethics/policy" also appears as a topical salon or as a lens distributed across other sessions.
- **Who is in the room.** No room, no invitees, no list. Designing the participant mix before the participants exist is how we end up designing for an imagined audience instead of the actual one.
- **Clearing house / observatory concept.** It emerges when it emerges. Not a deliverable.

## Things worth noticing without resolving

A few things I'd flag for later but won't try to design now:

- **The food side of agrifood.** I'd been implicitly treating agrifood as agriculture. It isn't. Processing, distribution, retail, consumption, waste, kitchens, eaters — the food half of the compound. The field guide's three-axis taxonomy includes all of it; my framing had been ignoring it. Worth weighing whether the salon's centre of gravity leans agricultural or whether it spans the whole chain.
- **What the role-play is doing.** Edutainment and memorability. Not artifact production. The form follows from that: a constructed scene that teaches and is remembered, not a research instrument that yields a document. The role-play can also *contain* the ethics/policy framing — drama plus gravity — without ethics/policy needing to be a separate session.
- **What a successful salon looks like.** Recollection itself is the bar. Not "they walked away with the proposition." Not "they remember the slogan." They remember *being there*. That's a different design problem than most conference-design framings, and it puts weight on the dramaturgy (the situation, the room, the pacing, the people, the moments) rather than the content delivery.
- **Why Discord.** The bet from the Aug 1 venue scan still holds: Discord offers spatial and theatrical grammar (foyer, main stage, backstage/green room, audience discussion, breakout spaces, roles, text-as-marginalia, scheduled events, persistent commons). The platform's default culture isn't inclusive; that gets countered by design, not by hope. The server itself becomes part of the artwork.
- **Recurring vs. one-off.** Not yet decided. The salon programme can be a single event, a recurring series, or the beginning of something that runs alongside the podcast. That choice changes what the post-event commons looks like and how much production infrastructure needs to persist.

## What we don't need to figure out at this stage

Anything that requires knowing who is in the room, what they bring, what they've already said, or what the agrifood domain actually looks like from inside. Those are downstream of reading the authoritative material in future-herd/ and the structural map in agrifood-ai-field-guide/. The next useful step is reading those, not designing the format around them.

## Suggested next move

Read the future-herd/ episode and knowledge corpus for what the room already responds to — recurring themes, guest archetypes, framings that landed, framings that didn't. Read the agrifood-ai-field-guide/ taxonomy and archetype set as a structural map of the territory. Then come back and design from inside the material rather than around an imagined one.

When we reconvene with that grounding, the productive questions will be different — and probably fewer — than the ones in this doc.
