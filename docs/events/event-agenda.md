# Building the Open-Source Future of Canadian Agrifood

*A Future Herd spectacular event on AI and agrifood*

**Thursday October 29, 2026 · From 12 noon Eastern Time (ET) · Online · Discord · Half-day · Four sessions**

## What is happening

Canadian agrifood is changing quickly. New forms of intelligence are moving through the food system, from the farm and the field to processing, distribution, retail, and the places where food is shared and eaten. Choices being made now about knowledge, technology, and who gets to shape them will stay with us for a long time.

This event brings people into the middle of that change. Farmers, food-chain workers, researchers, technologists, policy people, and others who care about Canadian agrifood are invited to meet, listen, question, and imagine together. We want to make a room where knowledge can travel, where experience is welcomed, and where a good question can be as valuable as a confident answer.

The day moves through four questions: **who → what → how → when** — who is here, what is happening, how might we respond, and what happens next?

## Why this event

The research behind the event has been following the people, ideas, tools, and systems shaping this moment: open-source hardware, machine data, digital public infrastructure, AI, biological commons, and the full food value chain. This gathering is a chance to put that knowledge into motion—to share it, challenge it, add to it, and connect it with the people whose lives and work give it meaning.

Open source is part of the invitation, not a conclusion we have already reached. It may open new possibilities for access, sovereignty, adaptation, and shared capacity. It may also bring difficult questions about responsibility, security, governance, and participation. We are interested in what people discover when they bring those questions into the same room.

The day is an opportunity to encounter one another differently: to recognize what is already present, to make connections that were not there before, and to leave with a wider sense of what might be possible. Anything else that emerges will be welcome, but the gathering itself is the work.

## The agenda

### 1 — *Who:* **Power goes to those who show up ready to do something** *(12:00–1:00pm)*

We begin with the people who have come together. What do you know? What are you curious about? What are you trying to understand or make possible? There is no need to arrive with a finished position. Curiosity, attention, and lived experience all have a place here.

### Break *(1:00–1:15pm)*

### 2 — *What:* **Intelligence begins on the farm and in the food** *(1:15–2:15pm)*

What is happening with open-source AI in Canadian agrifood—and what are people seeing that others may not yet see? We will look across the food system, listen for what is real and what is still emerging, and make room for the questions that become visible when different kinds of knowledge meet.

### Break *(2:15–2:30pm)*

### 3 — *How:* **The Future Is Something We Practise** *(2:30–3:30pm)*

Then the day takes a turn. We will enter a simple theatrical situation together. First, a significant cyberattack disrupts systems on which Canadian agrifood depends. Then, a second development changes how the incident is understood: it appears to have been caused by a rogue AI.

You do not need to play a character or arrive with technical expertise. Respond as yourself, bring what you know, and see what the room makes of the situation. The exercise does not presume an open-source agrifood system or prescribe open source as a solution. The meaning belongs to the participants.

### Break *(3:30–3:45pm)*

### 4 — *When:* **The Plot Thickens** *(3:45–4:45pm)*

We close by looking back at what happened among us. What became newly visible? Which questions grew more interesting? What did we hear in one another that we could not have heard alone? We will not force the day into a conclusion. We will leave room for the conversation to keep unfolding.

### 5 — *Wind down:* **Socialize and hang out** *(from 4:45pm)*

The formal part of the day gives way to a more relaxed time together. Stay with us, settle into conversation, and spend time with the people you have met across the sessions.

There is no further agenda to follow and no conclusion we need to reach. This is time to compare notes, share what stayed with you, follow an interesting thread, or simply enjoy being in the same online space.

The conversation can continue at its own pace, with room for new connections and whatever feels worth carrying forward from the day.

## How to participate

Come as you are. Speak, listen, ask questions, share experience, follow the conversation, or simply stay with it. You do not need to be an expert in AI, agriculture, or open source. If you have a question, an experience, a concern, a possibility, or a willingness to pay attention, you have something to bring.

We have shaped the day, but we have not decided in advance what it must become. That will depend on who shows up, what they bring, and what becomes possible when we spend time thinking and making sense of things together.
