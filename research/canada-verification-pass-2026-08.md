# Canada verification pass

Research date: 2026-08-01

Status: second Canada-focused verification pass. This pass strengthens candidate records, identifies a high-value Canadian seed-commons lead, and keeps the open-source/open-data/commons distinctions explicit.

## Findings

### Holos is an open-source core, not an entirely open GUI stack

The Holos README states that Holos is a whole-farm GHG model available at no cost, welcomes feedback from researchers, farmers, and industry, and provides training guides for different farm types. It also states that the source code is GPLv3 unless otherwise noted.

The same README explicitly says that the Holos 4 GUI is not open source at this time. The open status therefore applies to the repository’s core source code and algorithms, not necessarily to the entire distributed user-facing application.

This distinction materially improves the record: Holos is a candidate for an open Canadian public model and software core, but not a simple full-stack open-source case.

Sources:

- https://github.com/holos-aafc/holos/blob/main/README.md
- https://github.com/holos-aafc/holos/blob/main/LICENSE

### LiteFarm has stronger Canadian and operational evidence than previously recorded

The current official site says LiteFarm is coordinated by the University of British Columbia, built by farmers and researchers, and used in more than 155 countries. It reports more than 10,000 users and more than 7,500 farms, while the GitHub repository identifies a GPL-3.0 licence and an active public development history.

The repository showed a visible commit on 2026-07-31, with 18,276 commits on the integration branch at the time checked. The project is therefore a strong candidate for promotion from the existing international record into a Canadian-connected featured case, provided the counters are treated as project-reported claims rather than independently verified adoption statistics.

Sources:

- https://www.litefarm.org/
- https://github.com/LiteFarmOrg/LiteFarm

### Open Food Network has direct technical and Canadian organisational evidence

The global Open Food Network site describes an open-source ecommerce platform used by more than 2,500 enterprises in 15+ countries. The public repository identifies an AGPL licence and active development, including a visible commit from 2026-07-30.

The Canadian organisation is an incorporated not-for-profit with a named team and an active programme. The team page names David Thomas as Executive Director, Karla Falk as User Support Manager, Theresa Schumilas as Open Flower Network coordinating partner, Alex Garos as research associate and board member, and Jodi Koberinski as board member. These named people connect software, local food, farming, food sovereignty, research, and community development.

The 2025 annual report documents projects including a map of Quebec food-logistics hubs and local flower-farming initiatives. This supports a candidate record with a direct Canadian institutional and operational anchor, while the global repository supplies the open-source evidence.

Sources:

- https://openfoodnetwork.org/
- https://github.com/openfoodfoundation/openfoodnetwork
- https://about.openfoodnetwork.ca/2025-annual-report/
- https://about.openfoodnetwork.ca/our-team/

### Agri-food Data Canada is better described as semantic and governance infrastructure than as an open-data repository

Agri-food Data Canada’s FAIR page describes a semantic engine, federation of data silos, access/authentication/data-transfer standards, provenance and traceability, and tools for governance and access control.

The Semantic Engine page establishes that the system uses the open, international, machine-accessible Overlays Capture Architecture (OCA) standard in partnership with the Human Colossus Foundation. It supports schemas, agreements, machine-readable records, data requests, and data documentation.

The research-centre page also establishes that some data portals require login, while other portals provide public visualisation or access. The project should therefore be represented as FAIR and semantic research-data infrastructure with mixed access conditions, not as an unrestricted open data commons.

Sources:

- https://agrifooddatacanada.ca/fair-data/
- https://agrifooddatacanada.ca/semantic-engine/
- https://www.semanticengine.org/
- https://agrifooddatacanada.ca/research-centre-data-access/

### UBC Farm Seed Catalogue is a strong Canadian biological-commons lead, but not an Indigenous project

The UBC Farm Seed Catalogue states that the farm’s seeds are certified organic, open-pollinated, regionally adapted, held in the public domain, and GE-free. It identifies the Centre for Sustainable Food Systems at UBC Farm as a founding member of the BC Eco Seed Co-op, described as a producer cooperative providing BC-grown seed.

The catalogue reports production of 20–30 varieties from 15–20 crops annually and links this work to seed trials research with FarmFolkCityFolk. It also identifies a UBC Farm tomatillo variety registered with the Open Source Seed Initiative.

This is a strong candidate for Canadian open seed and regional seed infrastructure. It must not be represented as Indigenous seed sovereignty merely because it uses public-domain, open-pollinated, or open-source seed language. The Indigenous seed-sovereignty sources establish a different governance and cultural frame, including responsibilities to seeds, community control, and protection from commercial exploitation.

Sources:

- https://ubcfarm.ubc.ca/seed-catalogue/
- https://indigenousclimatehub.ca/2021/07/saving-seeds-protecting-the-planet-and-seed-sovereignty/
- https://online.ucpress.edu/elementa/article/9/1/00089/116505/Can-public-universities-play-a-role-in-fostering

### Indigenous seed sovereignty is a governance and consent question, not an open-licensing category

The Indigenous Climate Hub describes seed saving as connected to Indigenous food sovereignty, cultural and spiritual relationships, seed rematriation, and protection of Indigenous seed rights. It notes that the international seed treaty does not by itself protect Indigenous knowledge or prevent commercial exploitation, and that clear documentation and agreements are needed.

The Canadian Climate Institute’s related work identifies Sovereign Seeds as a national Indigenous by-and-for seed and agricultural food sovereignty organisation. This is an important lead, but the current evidence gathered here does not yet justify a catalogue record without direct, community-controlled sources and clearer documentation of the organisation’s own protocols, work, and permissions.

Sources:

- https://indigenousclimatehub.ca/2021/07/saving-seeds-protecting-the-planet-and-seed-sovereignty/
- https://climateinstitute.ca/publications/indigenous-food-climate-sovereignty/

## Candidate status changes

### Promote from candidate to curated

#### LiteFarm

Reason: direct GPL-3.0 repository evidence, active development, Canadian UBC coordination, farmer/researcher co-design, project-reported global use, and a clear agrifood role.

Caveat: the 10,000-user, 155-country, and 7,500-farm figures are project-reported and should not be treated as independently verified adoption statistics.

#### Open Food Network Canada

Reason: direct AGPL repository evidence from the global project, Canadian not-for-profit operational evidence, named Canadian people and partners, and current Canadian food-system projects.

Caveat: the Canadian record should distinguish the Canadian organisation and deployments from the global software project.

### Retain as candidate

#### Holos

Reason: strong open-core and GPLv3 evidence, but the official README explicitly says the Holos 4 GUI is not open source.

#### Agri-food Data Canada

Reason: strong evidence for FAIR, semantic, provenance, and governance infrastructure, but mixed access conditions and unresolved licensing boundaries.

### Add as candidate

#### UBC Farm Seed Catalogue / Seed Hub

Reason: public-domain, open-pollinated, regionally adapted seed production; BC Eco Seed Co-op; UBC Farm research; and an OSSI-registered variety.

Caveat: this is a university/community seed infrastructure case, not an Indigenous seed-sovereignty case.

### Retain as lead / contested case

#### Standard Data Platform for Autonomous Agriculture

No new evidence in this pass establishes open-source licensing or producer governance. Retain as a useful Canadian contested case rather than promoting it.

#### Sovereign Seeds and Indigenous seed-sovereignty initiatives

Retain as high-sensitivity research leads. Do not create a record until direct community-controlled sources and appropriate governance context are available.

## Canadian ecosystem layers

The Canadian landscape is beginning to resolve into four layers:

### Collaborators and practitioners

- LiteFarm contributors and UBC-connected farmers and researchers.
- Open Food Network Canada staff, board members, farmers, researchers, and local-food organisers.
- UBC Farm, FarmFolkCityFolk, and BC Eco Seed Co-op participants.
- Agri-food Data Canada researchers and Semantic Engine collaborators.

### Institutional anchors

- University of British Columbia / Centre for Sustainable Food Systems.
- Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada.
- University of Guelph / Ontario Agri-Food Innovation Alliance.
- Canadian public-sector and cooperative research institutions.

### Adjacent voices and infrastructures

- Human Colossus Foundation and OCA.
- OpenTEAM, GOAT, and Data Food Consortium.
- Indigenous Climate Hub and Sovereign Seeds.
- Canadian Centre for the Study of Co-operatives and agricultural data-governance researchers.

### Framework positions

- Open software commons: Open Food Network, LiteFarm.
- Public open model core with closed GUI boundary: Holos.
- FAIR and semantic infrastructure with controlled access: Agri-food Data Canada.
- Biological commons and regional seed production: UBC Farm / BC Eco Seed Co-op.
- Indigenous sovereignty and consent beyond open licensing: Sovereign Seeds and related community work.
- Data commons as a contested commercial-public claim: Standard Data Platform for Autonomous Agriculture.

## Next Canadian batch

The next work should not broaden into a generic global search. It should continue inside Canada with three tightly related tasks:

1. verify the Canadian governance, deployment, and partner layers around LiteFarm and Open Food Network;
2. inspect the technical and licensing boundaries of Holos and Agri-food Data Canada;
3. develop a careful, community-sensitive source set for UBC Farm, BC Eco Seed Co-op, Sovereign Seeds, and other Canadian seed-sovereignty actors.

## What remains unresolved

- Open Food Network Canada’s exact relationship to the global platform governance and code repositories.
- The licences of Agri-food Data Canada’s Semantic Engine components and OCA schema packages.
- Canadian deployment evidence for LiteFarm beyond institutional coordination.
- Current public participation and governance of BC Eco Seed Co-op.
- Whether UBC Farm’s OSSI-registered variety has a distinct record or should remain part of the UBC Farm case.
- Direct, community-controlled evidence for Sovereign Seeds and Indigenous seed initiatives.
- Whether the Standard Data Platform released any reusable code, schemas, or data under open terms.
