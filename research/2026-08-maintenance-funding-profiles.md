# Maintenance and funding profiles: comparative verification (G-OSA-08)

Research date: 2026-08-02
Method: this cycle addresses G-OSA-08 ("who pays for maintenance after initial development") as a comparative verification pass across the collection's 49 records, not a discovery pass. All 49 maintenance/funding sections were extracted from the records; four anchor verifications were run the same day (OADA, GODAN, farmOS, Open Data Kit — all confirmed live/active as of 2026-08-02). No record was modified; this document is the comparative layer.

## The archetype taxonomy

Six funding/maintenance archetypes emerge from the 49 records. The taxonomy itself is the primary output of this cycle.

1. **Grant / project-funded, post-grant uncertainty** (12 records) — ROMI (4-year EU project), L'Atelier Paysan (liquidated 2026), ISOBlue (repos dormant post-2020), REGOSH (2018-2023 window), Vinduino (2015-2017 era), Digital Earth Africa crop-type (Enabling Crop Analytics initiative), CGIAR/AgriLLM, OSE (foundation + Kickstarter), Farm Hack (mid-2010s activity, current unknown), Farm Africa/NECPA (project-bound), SEARICE (project grants), Sinong (university, recent).
2. **Institutional / state-hosted** (10 records) — Ag Data Commons (USDA NAL), WAGRI (MAFF/NARO), Holos (AAFC), AgriStack (GOI), China national data centers (CAS/MARA), CEADS/FSDN (EU programmes), Agri-food Data Canada (university-anchored), UBC Farm Seed, LiteFarm (UBC anchor), Ag Data Coalition/NAPDC (UNL + NIFA).
3. **Hybrid open core + paid services** (6 records) — farmOS (donations + subscription hosting), Open Data Kit (OSS + commercial hosted service), FarmBot (kit sales, benefit corp), Agdatahub (free for farmers, B2B fees), Open Food Network (partners + funders), ArduPilot (community + institutional participation, no identified commercial core).
4. **Membership / cooperative / fee-based** (5 records) — JoinData (fee model), NAPDC (coop), ONDC (seller fees), Agdatahub (overlaps with 3), Oggún (5-year licence fee model).
5. **Volunteer / community / NGO-donation** (11 records) — AgIsoStack++ (no identified backer), Appropedia (volunteer + bots), OpenSourceSeeds/OSSL (NGO donations), OSSI (donations + seed-company partners), Common Voice (foundation), FarmStack (Digital Green), Flying Labs (self-financed hubs), AfricaOSH (EU programme), Bioleft (university-associated), Open Source Ecology (overlaps 1), farmOS (overlaps 3).
6. **Venture / commercial capital** (3 records) — FBN ($917.9M raised, IPO trajectory), Alibaba ET Agricultural Brain (cloud business), OpenSprinkler (adjacent, kit sales).

## Cross-cutting verification findings

1. **Grant-project fragility is the modal pattern** (12 of 49 records). The L'Atelier Paysan liquidation (April 2026) is the strongest instance: a farmer-governed cooperative with 15 years of activity was not financially sustainable in its corporate form. Governance strength does not solve funding durability — the project's central question confirmed at the strongest possible case.
2. **The reverse puzzle**: AgIsoStack++ is actively maintained (repos pushed within weeks of the research date) with no identified company, foundation, or grant — active maintenance without identified funding exists at community scale, but its sustainability is unverifiable.
3. **Institutional/state hosting is the most durable archetype** (10 records: Ag Data Commons, WAGRI, Holos, AgriStack, China centers, CEADS) — but durability is policy-dependent, and farmer agency is absent from these records (ties directly to G-OSA-18).
4. **The hybrid open-core + paid-services model is the only self-sustaining answer** among open projects (farmOS subscription hosting explicitly funds development; ODK's hosted service; FarmBot kits). The recurring tension is the support boundary: FarmBot directs DIY builders to the forum, not company support.
5. **Pledge vs licence economics**: OSSI's pledge has the widest uptake (550+ varieties) but no enforcement mechanism; OSSL binds derivatives contractually but has no located enforcement case (G-OSA-21). Wider uptake vs untested enforceability is a live trade-off.
6. **VC-funded platforms invert the maintenance question** (FBN, ET Brain): maintenance is assured by capital, but value capture and data governance are the contested axes (G-OSA-20).
7. **The donor-funded "farmer-centric" paradigm recommends governance it does not durably fund** — the USAID/Gates reports (Development Gateway/Athena) propose farmer-centric models; the collection's cooperative records (NAPDC, JoinData) show the funding gap in practice.

## Seventh archetype: the pooled co-funding circle (added 2026-09-02)

Provenance: `research/grocery-feedback-2026-08.md` §3 (grocery project,
2026-08-28). Not present in the 2026-08-02 cycle's 49 records; identified
through the grocery concept's software-stack research.

7. **Pooled co-funding circle** — many small co-operatives each contributing
   modest amounts to fund dedicated maintenance of shared tooling, rather
   than relying on volunteers, a single institutional anchor, or a
   commercial open-core revenue stream. Anchor case: the Democratic Tech
   Fund's Foodsoft circle reports 50-60 food co-ops willing to co-fund a
   maintenance team (provenance `grocery/research/open-source-software-stack.md`);
   Co-op Cloud's deployment-recipe commons is the complementary
   infrastructure practice (shared packaging + ops so no operator reinvents
   deployment).

This archetype is the organizational answer to archetype 5's chronic
fragility (volunteer/community: 11 records) — it converts the user
community into a maintenance payer without requiring an institutional or
commercial anchor. Its mechanism (co-ops funding their own infrastructure
commons) has no farm-side instance in the corpus; the closest analogues
are NAPDC (co-op data governance, archetype 4) and farmOS (donations +
hosting, archetype 3). Whether it is durable in practice is unverified —
the Foodsoft circle reports willingness-to-fund, not yet paid maintenance
at scale. Key observation for the G-OSA-08 durability question: it is
member-economics-native (users fund what they use), but it presumes
co-operative customers; it has no tested analogue where users are not
already organized.
