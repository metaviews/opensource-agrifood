# Canadian farmers active in open-source communities — targeted scan

Research date: 2026-08-01

## Scope and evidence rule

This pass searches for named people who can be evidenced separately as:

1. Canadian-connected;
2. active farmers or farm operators, not merely agricultural technologists;
3. meaningful participants in open-source software, open hardware, maker, or collaborative technical communities.

A person is not promoted merely because they use open-source software, appear in a project directory, or work for an agricultural technology company. The strongest cases have a farm biography or independent farm reporting plus a public repository, project page, technical community profile, or documented open-source contribution.

## Strong cases found

### Brian Tischler — Alberta grain farmer and AgOpenGPS originator

Brian Tischler is the strongest case in this pass. Alberta Farmer Express identifies him as a central Alberta grain farmer near Mannville, west of Vermilion. Future of Agriculture describes him as an Alberta farmer farming with a neighbour across a combined 2,500 acres of wheat, barley, oats, canola, flax, and peas.

He created AgOpenGPS after solving a farm problem involving field mapping and section control. ASTech’s profile says he posted the project in agricultural forums, released the code on GitHub, and developed it through feedback from users around the world. The same profile describes AgOpenGPS and AgraBot as open agricultural applications for low-cost precision agriculture, machine control, and autonomous agricultural vehicles.

The project now has an official public repository with hundreds of forks, hundreds of stars, thousands of commits, public documentation, PCB/firmware repositories, and an active contributor community. u-blox describes the project as an open-source auto-steering solution created by Tischler and reports a community of more than 2,500 members. The official repository shows development activity through 2026, although current repository maintenance is not identical to Tischler’s personal activity.

Assessment: curated individual case. He is both a farmer and a meaningful open-source project founder/maintainer whose participation grew from on-farm problem solving.

### Matt Reimer — Manitoba grain farmer, open hardware/software builder, and Hackaday participant

Matt Reimer is independently identified as a Manitoba farmer. MIT News describes him as a grain producer who grew up on his family farm in Manitoba and developed a driverless tractor application. CBC’s search result describes a 2,500-acre rural Manitoba farm growing mostly canola and wheat; Farmers Weekly identifies him as a Manitoba farmer operating a 220-horsepower John Deere 7930 and 24-tonne grain cart.

Reimer used open hardware and source code from a drone autopilot ecosystem, learned programming through an MITx course, and built an autonomous grain-cart system. MIT reported that the driverless tractor unloaded grain from a combine more than 500 times during one harvest and estimated farm savings of at least CAD $5,000 for that season.

His public Hackaday profile describes him as a farmer in Manitoba interested in technology for farming and links to his public GitHub account. The profile includes an Autonomous Tractor project and identifies him as project owner/contributor. His GitHub account contains the public AutonomousGrainCart repository, as well as public Arduino and ArduPilot-related repositories. The public GitHub profile currently shows no contribution activity in the last year, so the evidence supports meaningful open-source participation and public technical work, but not necessarily current active maintenance in 2026.

Assessment: candidate individual case, with a historical-active or ongoing-community caveat. He clearly bridges farm operation, open hardware/software, and maker communities; current personal maintenance should be checked before calling him an active maintainer.

## What this pass changes

The search found two people who meet the core test at different confidence levels:

- Tischler is a strong present-day open-source agricultural community founder and farmer.
- Reimer is a strong historical farmer-maker case, but current personal maintenance activity is not demonstrated.

This is important because “active in open source” needs at least two dimensions:

1. active contribution or community participation by the individual;
2. continuing life in the open project or community itself.

Tischler clearly satisfies the second dimension through AgOpenGPS’s current public repository, but his personal current contribution rate requires separate verification. Reimer clearly satisfies meaningful historical participation, but his current personal GitHub activity is absent or low.

## Search gaps

- Canadian farmers active in Linux, Debian, GNU, Python, Arduino, Raspberry Pi, or maker communities without an agriculture-technology media profile.
- Women farmers and Indigenous farmers participating in open hardware, open data, or free-software communities.
- Canadian small-scale, organic, and market-garden farmers using FarmHack, farmOS, LiteFarm, Home Assistant, or open sensor projects under their own names.
- Farmer contributors in AgOpenGPS forums who can be linked to public farm identities without exposing private personal information.
- Canadian farmer participation in right-to-repair, tractor-hacking, and agricultural robotics communities beyond the two prominent Manitoba/Alberta cases.
