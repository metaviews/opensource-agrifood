# Canada verification pass — v2

Research date: 2026-08-01

This is a new dated revision of the Canada verification pass. The earlier pass remains as history.

## Editorial decisions

1. Promote LiteFarm and Open Food Network Canada to curated status because the licence, active technical project, Canadian institutional or organisational anchor, and agrifood role are now directly evidenced.
2. Keep Holos as a candidate because its core is GPLv3/open source but the official GUI is explicitly not open source.
3. Keep Agri-food Data Canada as a candidate because it is strong semantic and FAIR infrastructure with mixed access conditions and unresolved component licensing.
4. Keep UBC Farm Seed Catalogue / Seed Hub as a candidate, now with stronger evidence of community distribution, donations, participatory trials, workshops, and Canadian seed-security connections.
5. Add Sovereign Seeds as a high-sensitivity lead, not a curated open-source example. Its relevance is Indigenous governance and seed sovereignty, not an assumption of open licensing.

## Verification findings

### Holos: open core, closed GUI boundary

AAFC describes Holos as a Canadian farm GHG and soil-carbon model, continually updated, free to users, available in English and French, and open to feedback from researchers, farmers, and industry. It says the team is preparing the model for collaborative development and seeking external expertise.

The repository README states that the source code is GPLv3 unless otherwise noted, but also explicitly says that the Holos 4 GUI is not open source. The record therefore treats Holos as an open public model core with a closed GUI boundary, not as a fully open stack.

Sources:

- https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/agricultural-science-and-innovation/agricultural-research-results/holos-software-program
- https://github.com/holos-aafc/holos/blob/main/README.md
- https://github.com/holos-aafc/holos/blob/main/LICENSE

### LiteFarm: strong Canadian connection and public GPLv3 project

The official project site describes LiteFarm as free and open source, built by farmers and researchers, and coordinated by the University of British Columbia. Its contributor page links to the public repository, contributor wiki, product and technical guidance, API documentation, and feature-suggestion portal.

The public GitHub repository identifies a GPLv3 licence and active development. The project site reports more than 155 countries, 10,000+ users, and 7,500+ farms. These figures remain project-reported and are recorded as claims rather than independently audited adoption measures.

The Canadian connection is substantial, but the project is international. The record should not imply that its deployments or governance are primarily Canadian.

Sources:

- https://www.litefarm.org/
- https://www.litefarm.org/join-the-community
- https://github.com/LiteFarmOrg/LiteFarm
- https://github.com/LiteFarmOrg/LiteFarm/blob/integration/LICENSE

### Open Food Network Canada: software, standards, and Canadian market validation

The global Open Food Network identifies its flagship platform as open-source software for farmers, food collectives, food hubs, and community food enterprises. Its public repository identifies AGPL licensing and active development. Its governance page describes an open and transparent model, local affiliates, collaborative decision-making, and oversight by the Open Food Foundation.

Open Food Network Canada supplies the Canadian organisational and programme layer. Bioenterprise Canada reports Ontario market-validation work involving farmers, farmers’ markets, food-hub coordinators, marketplaces, farm-management platforms, and logistics providers. The work investigated adoption of the Data Food Consortium open standard and explored integration with LiteFarm.

This makes the Canadian case more than a national website or chapter: it is a Canadian organisational node connecting open software, interoperability standards, local-food commerce, and farmer-facing operational tools.

Sources:

- https://openfoodnetwork.org/about-us/
- https://github.com/openfoodfoundation/openfoodnetwork
- https://about.openfoodnetwork.ca/2025-annual-report/
- https://about.openfoodnetwork.ca/our-team/
- https://bioenterprise.ca/success-stories/simplifying-digital-tools-for-farmers-small-food-businesses/

### UBC Farm Seed Hub: community seed systems, not merely a catalogue

The UBC Farm Seed Hub describes itself as supporting community seed systems in British Columbia through interdisciplinary and community-engaged research and education. It began in 2012 and now operates as part of the Centre for Sustainable Food Systems at UBC Farm.

The Seed Hub reports local on-farm variety trials, field days, farmer workshops, online outreach across Canada, seed production and processing, community seed donations, seed-library support, participatory plant breeding, and collaboration with FarmFolkCityFolk and the Bauta Family Initiative on Canadian Seed Security.

The UBC Farm produces 20–30 varieties from 15–20 crops each year. Some seed is used at the farm, some sold to gardeners and farmers, and some donated to community groups and seed libraries. This strengthens its candidate record substantially.

The record still distinguishes the Seed Hub from Indigenous seed sovereignty. Its public-domain and open-pollinated language does not establish Indigenous governance or unrestricted access to all knowledge and biological materials.

Sources:

- https://ubcfarm.ubc.ca/csfs-research/seed-hub/
- https://ubcfarm.ubc.ca/seed-catalogue/

### Sovereign Seeds: high-value governance lead with a strict evidence boundary

Sovereign Seeds’ own site presents its work through seeds, cultures, learners, leaders, producers, and entrepreneurs. A partner profile describes it as a by-and-for Indigenous initiative working across Canada since 2019 through knowledge transmission, training, mentorship, seed access, leadership amplification, cultural seed revitalisation and distribution, and cultural farm and food entrepreneurship.

This is an important Canadian agrifood commons lead, but it should not be translated into open-source language. The available public evidence does not establish that cultural seeds, teachings, data, or images are open for unrestricted reuse. A future record should be guided by direct, community-controlled sources and the organisation’s own protocols.

Sources:

- https://www.sovereignseeds.org/
- https://www.tamtrust.org/grant-partner/sovereign-seeds/
- https://climateinstitute.ca/publications/indigenous-food-climate-sovereignty/

## Next action boundary

The Canadian anchor set is now strong enough for comparison. The next useful work is not another broad search. It is to:

1. inspect the Canadian deployments and governance of LiteFarm and Open Food Network in more detail;
2. identify the actual repositories, licences, and deployment conditions for Agri-food Data Canada and OCA-related tools;
3. determine whether Standard Data Platform released any open artefacts;
4. seek direct, community-controlled information before expanding the Sovereign Seeds record.
