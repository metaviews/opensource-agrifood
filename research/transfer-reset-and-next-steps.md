# Transfer reset and immediate next steps

Research date: 2026-08-01

## Why this reset is needed

The transfer work has begun to drift from knowledge transfer into open-ended verification of individual projects. That is a poor use of scarce time unless a particular claim is needed for a defined research output.

The purpose of Open Source Agrifood is not to reproduce the Agrifood AI Field Guide, audit every project in it, or build a comprehensive directory of open technologies. The purpose is to establish an independent research and knowledge project that can explain what openness means in agrifood, where meaningful activity is occurring, what institutional and governance patterns matter, and where the important unknowns are.

The field guide remains an adjacent map and research accelerator:

https://agrifood.metaviews.ca/

It should reduce discovery time, not create an obligation to verify every lead immediately.

## Larger goals

### 1. Establish an independent intellectual frame

Open Source Agrifood needs a clear answer to the question:

> What does “open” make possible in agrifood, and under what conditions does it produce farmer, community, public, or ecological benefit rather than simply redistribute control to new intermediaries?

The current provisional frame remains useful:

> From open tools to open agrifood infrastructure: who controls the data, interfaces, maintenance, and benefits?

This frame is more important than accumulating project records.

### 2. Build a small, durable evidence base

The repository should contain enough well-chosen cases to support serious analysis. It does not need to contain every named actor from the field guide.

A useful initial corpus is likely 8–12 strong cases, distributed across a few analytically distinct layers:

- software and field infrastructure;
- standards and interoperability;
- farmer or community data governance;
- open data and language infrastructure;
- biological commons;
- public and multilateral infrastructure.

Each case should earn its place by illuminating a question, not merely by being open or interesting.

### 3. Make the differences between kinds of openness legible

The project should help readers distinguish:

- open-source code;
- open models or weights;
- open datasets;
- open standards;
- digital public goods;
- cooperative or commons governance;
- biological commons;
- public-interest infrastructure.

This conceptual clarification is a core deliverable. It is not a preliminary chore to be completed before the “real” work.

### 4. Identify the structural conditions of success

The project should move beyond “who exists?” toward:

- who controls the system;
- who can enter, exit, or switch;
- who maintains it;
- who funds it;
- who sets the rules;
- who bears risks;
- who captures value;
- whether local knowledge and community authority are respected.

These questions are where the project can add value beyond the field guide’s inventory and beyond a technology catalogue.

### 5. Produce useful research outputs

The near-term outputs should be:

- a concise framing document;
- a selective dossier of anchor cases;
- an initial environmental scan;
- a gap and research-question register;
- later, a small number of deeper comparative notes.

The repository already contains the first four in draft form. The next task is to improve their usefulness and coherence, not to expand the number of verified facts indiscriminately.

## What has already been achieved

The transfer map has been completed. It established the boundary between the Agrifood AI Field Guide and Open Source Agrifood.

The first transfer has also been completed at a foundational level:

- selected field-guide concepts and cases have been carried into an independent dossier;
- original-source links and provenance practices have been established;
- an initial environmental scan has been created;
- gaps and research questions have been separated from settled claims;
- the project has begun making independent judgements instead of copying field-guide descriptions.

This is sufficient foundation for the next stage. The project does not need another transfer plan.

## What to stop doing for now

Until a defined output requires it, do not:

- verify every field-guide lead;
- chase comprehensive project adoption statistics;
- audit every licence, repository, model card, or dataset;
- expand the actor list simply because another name appears upstream;
- treat current web availability as a measure of research importance;
- build a formal taxonomy in the abstract;
- turn the repository into a global directory;
- investigate event planning, programming, speakers, or platforms.

These activities may become appropriate later, but they are not the current bottleneck.

## Immediate next steps

### Step 1 — Consolidate the frame

Read the README, transfer map, dossier, scan, and gap register as one body of work. Resolve contradictions and remove language that implies a larger degree of completeness than has been achieved.

Deliverable: a concise project framing note or revised README section that states the analytical question, scope, exclusions, and current phase.

### Step 2 — Select anchor cases

Choose a deliberately small set of cases—no more than 8–12—for deeper treatment. Selection criteria:

1. The case represents a distinct layer or governance pattern.
2. It has enough evidence for a useful analytical note.
3. It helps answer the project’s central question.
4. It adds something not already covered adequately by another case.
5. It has a clear reason to remain in the corpus even if its current status changes.

The current candidates are OADA, JoinData, Common Voice, OSSI, GODAN, ODK, CGIAR / AgriLLM, and one or two farmer-, Indigenous-, or community-governed cases still to be selected.

This is a selection decision, not a commitment to complete exhaustive verification of all candidates immediately.

### Step 3 — Convert the dossier from catalogue to comparison

For each selected case, use the same short analytical fields:

- problem addressed;
- open layer;
- governance and control;
- evidence of use;
- maintenance and funding;
- who benefits and who may be excluded;
- what the case demonstrates;
- what it does not demonstrate.

The comparison is the knowledge transfer. Individual project facts are supporting material.

### Step 4 — Write one comparative synthesis

Produce a short document—approximately 1,500–2,500 words—answering:

> What patterns recur across the anchor cases, and where do they diverge?

The synthesis should focus on the relationship between openness, control, interoperability, maintenance, and benefit. It should not attempt to summarize every case equally.

### Step 5 — Use gaps to guide targeted research

After the synthesis, promote only the gaps that block an important argument. Defer gaps that are interesting but not load-bearing.

Examples of potentially load-bearing gaps:

- whether open standards create practical exit rights;
- whether farmer-controlled data governance can operate beyond one national context;
- whether open agricultural AI includes the data and governance layers needed for accountable use;
- how maintenance is funded after initial development.

Examples of deferred gaps:

- complete global counts of every implementation;
- exhaustive model-release inventories;
- precise adoption figures that do not change the comparative argument.

### Step 6 — Revise the taxonomy only after comparison

Do not formalize a taxonomy yet. Let the selected cases and comparative synthesis show which categories earn their place. If a taxonomy is created, it should be a small working taxonomy with a visible change log, not a comprehensive metadata system.

## Decision rule for future research

Before doing a new verification task, ask:

1. Which project argument does this support?
2. Will the answer change the comparative synthesis?
3. Is this the strongest available case for that argument?
4. Can the question be answered at the level of the selected anchor set?
5. What will be deferred if this task is undertaken?

If the task does not support a current argument or deliverable, record it in the gap register and defer it.

## Recommended immediate work package

The next work package should not be another broad web search. It should be:

1. select the anchor set;
2. normalize the dossier entries around the comparison fields;
3. write the comparative synthesis;
4. update the gap register based on arguments that remain genuinely blocked;
5. commit and push that coherent revision.

This is the point at which the project shifts from transfer and collection to interpretation.

## Success condition for the next stage

The next stage is successful if a reader can understand, without consulting the full field guide:

- what kinds of openness exist in agrifood;
- why they are not interchangeable;
- which governance and infrastructure patterns matter;
- what the strongest examples demonstrate;
- what remains uncertain;
- why further research should focus on control, maintenance, and benefit rather than on accumulating names.

That is a more valuable milestone than a larger dossier.

## Relationship to the field guide

The Agrifood AI Field Guide should continue to be cited as an adjacent discovery and synthesis resource at https://agrifood.metaviews.ca/. Open Source Agrifood should selectively draw from it, preserve provenance, and independently synthesize only what serves this project’s larger question.

The transfer is therefore not a one-time copying operation. It is a controlled process of selection, interpretation, and re-use.
