# Open Agriculture Discovery Scan: varied web search

Research date: 2026-08-01

Status: discovery scan, not a set of curated example records.

## Purpose

This scan broadens the candidate pool for the Open Agrifood Examples collection. It deliberately searches beyond agricultural AI for open farm-management software, open hardware and robotics, interoperability, geospatial infrastructure, data commons, biological commons, and regionally grounded projects.

The entries below are leads. Inclusion here does not mean that the project will enter the catalogue. Each lead still requires a common record, licence checking, evidence of use, and an assessment of governance, maintenance, and benefit.

## Search approach

Searches covered:

- open-source farm management software;
- open agriculture hardware, sensors, and robotics;
- farmer data cooperatives and data commons;
- open seeds and biological commons;
- open agricultural GIS and geospatial workflows;
- African open agriculture projects and datasets;
- Latin American open agriculture projects and APIs;
- Asian and India-related open agriculture searches;
- open agricultural APIs, standards, and digital infrastructure.

Search results were then narrowed through direct project, institutional, repository, academic, and public-sector pages. Search-engine snippets and directories are discovery aids only.

## Strong discovery leads

### 1. farmOS

- URL: https://farmos.org/
- Region / reach: international
- Possible role: open farm management, planning, records, and agricultural data platform
- Open layer: GPL-licensed software and community-maintained modules/integrations
- Evidence found: the project describes itself as a web-based farm management and record-keeping application; code is available through the farmOS GitHub organisation; self-hosting and community contribution are documented. The site reports a July 2026 4.0.4 release.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a mature open farm-management platform, distinct from ODK’s field-data collection role and OADA’s interoperability focus.
- Needs checking: current adoption and geographic distribution; governance and funding; deployment experience outside North America/Europe; data model and interoperability; licence coverage of modules.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 2. LiteFarm

- URL: https://www.litefarm.org/
- Region / reach: global; developed through the University of British Columbia and farmer/researcher collaboration
- Possible role: sustainable, diversified, agroecological, and organic farm management
- Open layer: free/open-source farm-management software and community contribution
- Evidence found: the official site describes farmer and researcher development, eight supported languages, use in more than 155 countries, and more than 7,500 farms / 10,000+ users on its public counters. It also reports a 2026 Pan-African co-design workshop in Kenya.
- Why it diversifies the collection: farmer co-design, sustainable/agroecological orientation, multilingual support, and a potentially strong bridge between open software and actual farm use.
- Needs checking: repository and licence; current versus historical public counters; what “used” means; governance and institutional dependence; evidence from African and other non-European deployments.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 3. Tania

- URL: https://usetania.org/
- Repository: https://github.com/Tanibox/tania-core
- Region / reach: international; initiated by Tanibox
- Possible role: open farmer journal and farm-management software
- Open layer: Apache 2.0 code, community edition, extensible modules
- Evidence found: the official site identifies Tania as an open-source farmer’s journal, links to GitHub, states that code is Apache 2.0 licensed, and describes community contributors, IoT integration, and Open Collective support.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a smaller community-driven farm-management project that can be compared with farmOS and LiteFarm on maintenance and sustainability.
- Needs checking: repository activity, current release, actual users and deployments, licence scope, community health, and whether the project remains operational at meaningful scale.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 4. FarmBot

- URL: https://farm.bot/
- GitHub organisation: https://github.com/farmbot
- Region / reach: international; precision gardening and small-scale production
- Possible role: open-source agricultural robotics and controlled growing
- Open layer: software, operating system, firmware, and public hardware/software ecosystem
- Evidence found: the public GitHub organisation contains the web app, Raspberry Pi operating system, Arduino firmware, and related client libraries. Several repositories show recent activity and MIT or Apache-2.0 licensing.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a visible open hardware/software case connecting digital control, robotics, fabrication, and growing practice.
- Needs checking: what hardware designs are open versus commercially supplied; user community and production use; affordability; governance; environmental and labour implications; whether it is relevant to agriculture beyond gardening.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 5. ROMI — Robotics for Microfarms

- URL: https://romi-project.eu/
- Documentation: https://docs.romi-project.eu/
- Region / reach: European research project; field sites near Paris and Barcelona
- Possible role: open robotic tools for organic, diversified, human-scale vegetable farms
- Open layer: robotic tools, research, data, documentation, and software/hardware components
- Evidence found: the project describes open-source tools including a weeding rover, crop-monitoring cablebot/aerial system, and 3D plant imager. It reports field testing with professional farmers across multiple seasons.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a direct farmer-tested open robotics project with an explicit small-scale and agroecological orientation.
- Needs checking: current post-project maintenance; licences for each component; deployment beyond project sites; costs and repairability; farmer control of data; actual labour effects.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 6. AgStack Foundation

- URL: https://agstack.org/
- GitHub: https://github.com/agstack
- Region / reach: global; Linux Foundation project
- Possible role: open digital infrastructure for agriculture and food
- Open layer: reusable software, geospatial identity, APIs, data infrastructure, traceability, and application components
- Evidence found: AgStack describes a member-supported, community-maintained agricultural digital infrastructure effort. Its public GitHub profile lists 46 repositories, 21+ active repositories in the last six months, and projects including Asset Registry, GeoIDs, INATrace, and Pancake. The profile shows Apache 2.0 for the organisation’s project catalogue, but each repository requires separate checking.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a large infrastructure ecosystem rather than a single application, useful for examining open agricultural infrastructure, institutional sponsorship, and maintenance.
- Needs checking: project-level licences; production deployments; relationship between open repositories and member interests; governance; public benefit; actual farmer-facing use; overlap with OADA and OpenAgri.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 7. Horizon OpenAgri

- URL: https://horizon-openagri.eu/
- Solutions: https://horizon-openagri.eu/os-solutions/
- Region / reach: Europe; claims collaboration across 10 European countries
- Possible role: open-source software and open-hardware agricultural digital solutions
- Open layer: reusable services, standards, hardware, catalogue, and common semantic model
- Evidence found: the project describes services for farm calendars, weather, pest and disease management, irrigation, reporting, security, and a common semantic model. It reports 500 farmers involved in shaping solutions and a catalogue of open agricultural digital solutions.
- Why it diversifies the collection: an explicit open-software/open-hardware and farmer-participation programme, potentially useful for comparing programme infrastructure with individual open projects.
- Needs checking: project funding and dates; repository and licence evidence; meaning of reported farmer participation and decision-making improvement; actual deployments; durability after project funding.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 8. AgroAPI — Embrapa

- URL: https://www.embrapa.br/agroapi
- Research article: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772375523000771
- Region / reach: Brazil / tropical agriculture
- Possible role: agricultural APIs and data/model infrastructure
- Open layer: APIs, data access, models, and application integration; licence and access terms require checking
- Evidence found: a 2023 open-access paper describes AgroAPI as an Embrapa-led platform providing APIs for productivity, planting dates, soil classification, weather, bio-inputs, and satellite vegetation indices. The paper states that APIs and applications are used by companies, farmers, scientists, and the public.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a strong Latin American and public agricultural research infrastructure lead, with a different model from OADA and AgStack.
- Needs checking: current API availability; actual open-source status of code; data licences; user and application evidence; institutional maintenance; terms of commercial reuse.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 9. Bioleft

- URL: https://www.bioleft.org/es/
- Region / reach: Argentina / Latin America
- Possible role: open seed exchange, participatory breeding, and farmer/community seed commons
- Open layer: biological materials, open seed circulation, collaborative evaluation data, and participatory protocols
- Evidence found: the project describes itself as Latin America’s first open-source seed exchange and breeding community. Its current site reports a publicly downloadable dataset of native tomatoes after four years of networked work, a published participatory evaluation protocol, ongoing 2026–27 collaborative tomato evaluation, and recognition in an FAO report.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a regionally grounded biological-commons case distinct from OSSI, with open datasets and participatory breeding rather than only a legal seed pledge.
- Needs checking: exact seed terms and legal mechanism; participating farmers and institutions; geographic reach; data licence; governance; relation to Indigenous and campesino seed systems; evidence of farmer outcomes.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 10. Digital Earth Africa crop-type mapping framework

- URL: https://digitalearthafrica.org/en_za/creating-an-open-source-framework-for-crop-type-mapping-in-africa/
- Repository: https://github.com/digitalearthafrica/crop-type
- Region / reach: Africa; Zambia, Namibia, with scaling references to Niger, Rwanda, and Mozambique
- Possible role: open Earth-observation workflow, field-data collection, crop mapping, and agricultural analytics
- Open layer: documented notebooks, workflows, open-source analytical environment, and access to analysis-ready satellite datasets
- Evidence found: Digital Earth Africa describes a framework developed with RCMRD and FrontierSI, using field data, model development, and map production. It reports development for Zambia and scaling partnerships involving Niger, Rwanda, and Mozambique.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a concrete African open geospatial and remote-sensing workflow with institutional and country-level partners.
- Needs checking: repository licence; current status of country deployments; data governance and local institutional control; actual operational use; maintenance after the initiative; farmer-facing value.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 11. SAGDA — Synthetic Agriculture Data for Africa

- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13123v1
- Repository: https://github.com/SAGDAfrica/sagda
- Region / reach: Africa-oriented
- Possible role: open synthetic agricultural data toolkit
- Open layer: Python library, synthetic data generation, public data utilities, and reproducible workflows
- Evidence found: the paper describes an open-source Python toolkit for generating, augmenting, validating, visualising, and simulating synthetic climate, soil, yield, and fertiliser data. It presents yield-prediction and NPK recommendation use cases and links to code.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a data-centric African example that raises questions about synthetic data, local relevance, validation, and whether openness can compensate for missing real-world data.
- Needs checking: repository activity and licence; dataset provenance; empirical validation; users; institutional affiliation; limits of synthetic data; whether the project is mature enough for the catalogue.
- Provisional status: `lead`

### 12. Ag Data Commons

- URL: https://www.nal.usda.gov/services/agdatacommons
- Portal: https://agdatacommons.nal.usda.gov/
- Region / reach: United States / USDA-funded research, with broader reuse potential
- Possible role: public agricultural research-data catalogue and repository
- Open layer: public data access, metadata, FAIR infrastructure, and curated research-data discovery
- Evidence found: the USDA National Agricultural Library describes Ag Data Commons as a catalogue and repository for publicly accessible USDA-funded research data. It applies curation, supports FAIR practices, and allows public users to view/download data without accounts.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a public-sector data infrastructure case, distinct from GODAN’s coordination role and Common Voice’s community contribution model.
- Needs checking: scale and reuse; dataset licence variation; relationship between catalogue and underlying repositories; governance and eligibility limitations; geographic and institutional scope.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

### 13. OpenATK

- URL: https://openatk.com/
- GitHub: https://github.com/OpenATK
- Region / reach: United States-origin, broader design relevance
- Possible role: open farm-management apps and farmer-owned data storage
- Open layer: apps, specifications, code, and human-readable data formats
- Evidence found: OpenATK describes open apps for tillage, rocks, and task management. It emphasises interoperable small tools, storage in farmer-controlled cloud services, and data formats usable without the original apps. It links to code, documentation, and a development process.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a direct case of modular software, data portability, and “apps that work forever,” useful for comparison with OADA and farmOS.
- Needs checking: current activity appears historically dated; repository status; present users; licence; whether the project is active or should be archived as a historical case.
- Provisional status: `lead`

### 14. AgroFIMS

- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2021.726646/full
- Region / reach: international agricultural research
- Possible role: open fieldbook and agricultural standards/data-collection tooling
- Open layer: open-source web software, fieldbook standards, and export to mobile data-collection tools
- Evidence found: a peer-reviewed article describes AgroFIMS as an open-source tool for creating fieldbooks that can be exported to multiple free digital collection applications.
- Why it diversifies the collection: connects agricultural research standards, field data, and interoperability; could complement ODK without duplicating it.
- Needs checking: project repository and licence; current maintenance; use in breeding or agronomic programmes; relationship to existing standards; geographic deployments.
- Provisional status: `lead`

### 15. AgGateway ADAPT

- URL: https://aggateway.org/CurrentWork/On-Farm.aspx
- Region / reach: international precision agriculture
- Possible role: agricultural data interoperability and translation
- Open layer: open-source ADAPT software and data interoperability components
- Evidence found: AgGateway describes ADAPT as open-source software enabling agricultural-data interoperability for ag retailers, agronomists, and growers.
- Why it diversifies the collection: a possible industry-coordinated interoperability case to compare with OADA, especially around standards, governance, and commercial adoption.
- Needs checking: repository and licence; current implementation status; governance; actual grower use; relationship to ISOXML and other standards; whether the project is open in code only or also in specifications and governance.
- Provisional status: `candidate`

## Preliminary diversity observations

The search produced more than a handful of plausible additions. The strongest diversification opportunities are not all more AI projects:

1. Open farm-management software: farmOS, LiteFarm, Tania, and OpenATK.
2. Open robotics and hardware: FarmBot, ROMI, and Horizon OpenAgri.
3. Agricultural digital infrastructure: AgStack, OpenAgri, AgroAPI, and Ag Data Commons.
4. African geospatial and data infrastructure: Digital Earth Africa crop mapping and SAGDA.
5. Latin American biological commons: Bioleft.
6. Agricultural interoperability and research standards: AgroFIMS and AgGateway ADAPT.

This changes the likely shape of the next collection review. The current seven examples are not merely missing two or three additions; they sit within a much larger set of possible cases, and several entire currents are currently absent from the catalogue.

## Provisional candidate batches

A later selection pass could evaluate candidates in batches rather than individually:

- **Open farm operations:** farmOS, LiteFarm, Tania, OpenATK.
- **Open hardware and robotics:** FarmBot, ROMI, Horizon OpenAgri.
- **Public and shared digital infrastructure:** AgStack, OpenAgri, AgroAPI, Ag Data Commons.
- **African open geospatial/data systems:** Digital Earth Africa crop mapping, SAGDA.
- **Biological commons beyond OSSI:** Bioleft.
- **Standards and research data collection:** AgroFIMS, AgGateway ADAPT.

No candidate is promoted automatically. The next step is to compare candidates within and across batches, then select those that add the most analytical and geographic diversity.

## What this scan does not establish

- It is not a complete inventory of open agriculture projects.
- Search prominence is not evidence of importance, adoption, or farmer benefit.
- A project’s use of “open-source” on its website does not establish licence completeness across code, data, hardware, models, or documentation.
- Public counters from project websites are not independently validated here.
- Several leads may be inactive, narrowly scoped, commercially supported, or better treated as historical examples.
- Regional diversity is improved but still uneven; Asia, Africa outside the selected geospatial cases, Indigenous governance, and farmer-led projects require further searching.
- No new candidate has yet been added to the curated catalogue.
