# Data cooperative durability beyond JoinData: SAOS and GISC follow-up (G-OSA-20)

Research date: 2026-08-03
Method: bounded follow-up cycle targeting G-OSA-20 / Q-OSA-07 ("Which data cooperatives outside the Netherlands have achieved durable farmer participation and control?"). Seven query sets; sources read in full: SAOS home and Data workstream pages, SAOS 2026 annual report coverage (Co-operative News), Co-operative News SAOS conference report (Jan 2020), Saint Mary's University ICCM case study on SAOS (McMahon 2022), GISC website (maintenance mode), datacollaboratives.org GISC case, Internet Policy Review data-cooperative glossary (Fink 2024). Verification tools: HTTP checks (gisc.coop, saos.coop, agdatacoop.org), web searches for current status. Two records added (SAOS curated, GISC candidate); NAPDC record already covers the US lineage.

## Why this cell

The data-cooperatives cycle (2026-08-02) established NAPDC (US) and Agdatahub (EU) as the cooperative-adjacent records beyond JoinData, and named SAOS (Scotland) and GISC (US) as leads: "cooperative and consent-governance forms exist outside the Netherlands... membership scale, usage, and farmer control outcomes are unverified." This follow-up verifies both leads to resolve G-OSA-20: does the cooperative form achieve durable farmer participation and control anywhere outside the Netherlands?

## Headline finding

**Cooperative data governance outside the Netherlands resolves into two shapes: durable federation-owned infrastructure, and unverified or under-construction farmer-owned exchange.** SAOS (Scotland) is the strongest located case of the first shape: an 18-year-old, government-contracted livestock traceability database (ScotEID, launched 2008, expanded 2026) owned by a wholly-owned subsidiary of the 120-year-old cooperative federation — with the explicit note that farmer share-ownership of the data subsidiaries is deferred by design (Yuill, 2022: "as the subsidiaries mature... SAOS may well then move the 'ownership' to our more traditional understanding of co-op share ownership"). GISC (US) is the second shape: the "world's first aggregated data co-op" (Tiller) is now in maintenance mode on its own website, its lineage folded into GADC/AgXchange/NAPDC, with membership scale never independently verified in any source located. The farmer-owned data-exchange cooperative at verified scale — the JoinData equivalent the gap was looking for — remains not located outside the Netherlands. Q-OSA-07 stays answered in the negative: the cooperative form is durable where it owns regulated infrastructure; cooperative data exchange at scale is still unverified.

## Currents

### Current 1 — GISC and the US lineage: cooperative form, unverified scale, declining public activity

Grower Information Services Cooperative (GiSC), founded by Texas cotton farmer Billy Tiller, pitched aggregation-and-benchmarking ("One person's data isn't worth a lot... when you put it together you can aggregate and benchmark" — SAOS conference, Jan 2020). Verified trajectory: 2015 House Agriculture Committee testimony; 2017 merger with the Ag Data Coalition into AgXchange, commercially available through the Growers Ag Data Cooperative (GADC), with Farmers Edge data-platform partnership; 2023-2026 NAPDC NIFA ODF grant (active, ends Aug 2026) continuing the lineage. Current state (2026-08-03): gisc.coop serves a maintenance-mode page (WordPress infrastructure on gisc.cloud remains, login functional); no membership figures were ever located in any source; the "only grower-owned data cooperative in the United States" claim (Hoosier Ag Today, 2017) is unverifiable today. The durable survivor of the GISC ecosystem is Ag Data Transparent — the certification body Tiller helped found — not the data cooperative.

### Current 2 — SAOS / ScotEID: durable cooperative data infrastructure

The Scottish Agricultural Organisation Society (est. 1905, cooperative federation of ~56 primary agri co-ops, ~600 primary co-op directors, group turnover >£4.5M, 31 staff) runs ScotEID (est. 2008): Scotland's central livestock traceability database, operated on behalf of the Scottish government and the livestock industry, with 18 staff in the subsidiary. 2026 expansion: bluetongue vaccination recording tool, Rural Payments and Inspection Directorate data reporting, pig biosecurity pyramid identification. Value-added products built on statutory data: MyHerdStats (herd performance insights from cattle registration/movement data) and an AMU (antimicrobial use) pilot. The proven outcome: a multi-agency data collaboration (SAOS + NFU + government + abattoirs) that nearly eradicated BVD from Scottish cattle. This is the most durable located instance of farmer-organization-owned data infrastructure outside the Netherlands — with the governance caveat below.

### Current 3 — SAOS's farmer-owned data platform: under construction

SAOS's precision-ag data work is explicit ("co-operative approaches to data management that protect the rights of farmers... unlock the value of precision farming for Scotland") with published principles (farmers own their data; farmer-first design; real-time insights; partnerships; trust — George Noble, 2020). But the farmer-owned data cooperative itself is not yet deployed: SmartRural (SAOS's second subsidiary, now a member co-op under General Manager George Noble) completed a feasibility study in 2026 for a data management platform "which could be used as a basis for farmer-owned data co-ops," and a Scottish Agriculture Data Partnership was announced via social channels in late 2025 ("key organisations across Scotland's agricultural sector have come together") with no press coverage or published governance details located. Feasibility-stage, not operating scale.

### Current 4 — The ownership deferral: subsidiary structure vs cooperative shares

The governance nuance that matters most for the farmer-agency question: SAOS deliberately holds its data subsidiaries as wholly-owned federation subsidiaries rather than farmer-share-owned co-ops. Yuill's stated rationale (2022): the ownership link to data "is much more nebulous" than to a physical commodity, "especially during early development"; ownership may transfer to co-op share structure "as the subsidiaries mature." A SAOS board member sits on both subsidiary boards, with quarterly reporting to the SAOS Board. So even the strongest case has farmer control mediated through federation governance, not direct member ownership of the data infrastructure.

## Contested claims

- **"GiSC is the only grower-owned data cooperative in the United States" (2017).** Asserting: Hoosier Ag Today / Global Ag Tech Initiative launch coverage. Contesting: maintenance-mode site (2026-08-03), no published membership scale in any source, lineage absorbed into GADC/NAPDC. Unverifiable as stated today. Last reviewed: 2026-08-03.
- **"SAOS delivers co-operative approaches to data management that protect the rights of farmers" (SAOS framing).** Asserting: SAOS data workstream. Contesting: ScotEID is federation-subsidiary-owned with share ownership deferred by design (Yuill 2022); the farmer-owned platform is feasibility-stage. Qualified: infrastructure ownership is real, direct farmer share-ownership is not. Last reviewed: 2026-08-03.
- **"Farmers can aggregate and benchmark to gain supply-chain power" (Tiller, 2020).** Asserting: GISC's aggregation pitch at the SAOS conference. Contesting: no verified scale outcome located; the cooperative form did not sustain an active public platform. Aspiration, not outcome. Last reviewed: 2026-08-03.
- **"The cooperative form is the answer to farm data control" (advocacy framing).** Asserting: data-cooperative literature (Fink 2024 glossary; Hutchins 2023). Contesting: this pass — the form is durable where it owns regulated infrastructure (ScotEID), unverified where it depends on voluntary farmer participation (GISC, SAOS platform). The synthesis's "governance without durable funding" finding extends: cooperative form without verified scale. Last reviewed: 2026-08-03.

Plausibility: high that it names a real initiative; details require direct sources. Last searched: 2026-08-03.
- **G-DC-05 — ScotEID / SmartRural ownership-transfer intent.** Whether the subsidiaries move to cooperative share ownership ("as the subsidiaries mature") and on what terms is a tracked intention, not a commitment; no public update located. Last searched: 2026-08-03.
- **G-DC-06 — GADC / AgXchange current operational status.** Post-2017 operational state, membership, and whether AgXchange still functions commercially are unverified; the NAPDC partnership is the only verified current thread. Last searched: 2026-08-03.

## What this scan does not yet do

- **No member-level verification.** No interviews or member-count evidence for SAOS, GISC, GADC, or NAPDC; the records rest on organisational and analytical sources.
- **No ScotEID data-governance audit.** The terms under which the Scottish government contracts ScotEID, and who can access the data, were not read in full.
- **No AgXchange account-level verification.** Whether the platform operates commercially today would require an account or direct statement.
- **No UK/EU regulatory analysis.** The interplay with the EU Data Governance Act and UK data regimes was not assessed.
- **No non-English coverage.** Scottish Gaelic and other local-language sources were not searched; the Data Partnership announcement evidence is English-language social media.
