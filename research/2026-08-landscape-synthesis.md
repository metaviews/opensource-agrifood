# Open-source agrifood landscape: synthesis of the 2026 discovery programme

Research date: 2026-08-03
Method: internal synthesis, not a discovery pass. This document integrates the nine bounded discovery cycles run 2026-08-02 (open hardware/robotics/sensing; machine-data access and telematics; Global South open hardware, two passes; DPI-for-agriculture; China ag digital infrastructure; data cooperatives; biological commons; maintenance/funding profiles) with the 49-record dossier, the gap register (G-OSA-01..21), the research-question register (Q-OSA-01..20), and the contested-claims ledger (C-OSA-01..04). No new sources were read for this document; every position traces to a scan doc, record, or register entry in this repository, and uncertainty is recorded rather than filled. The earlier Canadian coverage and foundation passes enter through the catalogue.

## Why this synthesis

The discovery programme has done its first job: the landscape is now mapped at nine cells with 49 verified records, and the working matrices in the scans are cell-local. What has been missing is the cross-cell layer — the positions that only become visible when the cells are read against each other. This document converts the cell-local matrices into cross-cutting currents, re-scores the contested-claims ledger against the accumulated evidence, and re-ranks the discovery queue by one criterion: which gap, if resolved, would change the synthesis most. It is a framework document, not a verdict on any project.

## Headline finding

**Openness in agrifood is layered, the layers decouple, and farmer agency is the scarcest layer of all.** Across every cell — hardware, machine data, DPI, open AI, cooperatives, biological commons — "open" attaches to code, data, weights, standards, designs, licences, or governance, and openness at one layer consistently fails to imply openness at any other. The two things the collection's central question (who controls the system?) most wants to find — farmer-held structural governance, and durable maintenance under real openness — are the two rarest patterns in the record. Where capital is present, openness is absent or contested (FBN, ET Agricultural Brain); where openness is real, maintenance is fragile (L'Atelier Paysan's liquidation is the strongest case); and the Global South's farmer-led, explicitly licensed open-hardware layer is now evidenced as absent, not merely unsearched. Regulation and standards create access; they do not transfer control. The contested-claims ledger can now be moved from "unresolved" to provisional verdicts — none of the four headline claims survives intact.

## Currents

Seven cross-cutting currents emerge from the cell matrices. Each names the stance and its anchors; the tensions are the load-bearing parts.

### Current 1 — Farmer-maker and cooperative self-build

Openness that originates on farms, driven by repairability, affordability, and appropriateness. Anchors: L'Atelier Paysan (France, cooperative), AgOpenGPS / Brian Tischler (Canada, farmer-origin), Vinduino (US, individual grower), Matt Reimer (Canada, farmer-maker), Farm Hack (US/UK, peer network). This current carries the strongest farmer-control claims in the dossier — and, as L'Atelier Paysan's April 2026 liquidation shows, the clearest maintenance fragility. Governance strength does not solve funding durability; the central question confirmed at its strongest possible case.

### Current 2 — State and supranational infrastructure

States and supranational bodies building digital public infrastructure for agriculture: national stacks (AgriStack, India), government API platforms (WAGRI, Japan), data spaces (CEADS/AgriDataSpace, EU), and China's policy programme (Smart Agriculture Action Plan 2024-2028, national scientific data centers). This is the largest-scale governance form in the collection and the structurally weakest on farmer agency: AgriStack's consent is contested architecture-versus-practice, CEADS holds farmers in advisory capacity, WAGRI treats farmers as service users, and China's plan does not address farmer agency at all. Institutional/state hosting is the most durable funding archetype (10 records) — durability purchased at the price of agency.

### Current 3 — The open-core + paid-services hybrid

The only self-sustaining open model located: open core funded by paid services. Anchors: farmOS (donations + subscription hosting), ODK (open source + hosted service), FarmBot (kits, benefit corporation), Agdatahub (free for farmers, B2B fees). The recurring tension is the support boundary — FarmBot directs DIY builders to the community forum, not company support — and the small-community risk (AgIsoStack++ is actively maintained with no identified backer at all). Six of 49 records occupy this archetype; it is the answer Q-OSA-14 is looking for, and it is a minority pattern.

### Current 4 — Commons governance without licences

Biological commons mechanisms are not interchangeable: OSSL's contractual licence (derivatives bound, seed-law compatible, enforcement untested — G-OSA-21), OSSI's pledge (550+ varieties, no enforcement mechanism), Potato Park's customary-law Indigenous territory governance (the strongest farmer/community control in the entire dossier), and SEARICE's community seed banks (community-defined access, NGO-dependent). Licence, pledge, and customary governance are three different mechanisms with three different protection logics; translating customary governance into open-licensing terms misrepresents it. The licence-versus-pledge trade-off — wider uptake (OSSI) against untested enforceability (OSSL) — is live.

### Current 5 — The donor-funded "farmer-centric" paradigm

The USAID/Gates farmer-centric data governance report, the World Bank/Co-Develop DPI-for-agriculture paradigm, and the DPG building-block movement (FarmStack) recommend farmer-centric governance that the record shows is not durably funded. The paradigm's own deep dives (data cooperatives) land on the collection's cooperative records (NAPDC, JoinData) precisely where the funding gap shows in practice. The paradigm recommends; the records demonstrate. Treat the recommendations as recommendations.

### Current 6 — Openness as trust-and-transfer in the Global South

Where open-manufacturing and capacity models exist in the Global South, "open" functions as a trust and licensing mechanism rather than a free design commons: Think Oggún's 5-year technology licence fee after in-country manufacture, Flying Labs' locally owned service hubs (farmers as clients, not owners), AfricaOSH's continental substrate, REGOSH's open agroecology stack (the closest to community-governed openness located). The direct L'Atelier Paysan/Vinduino equivalent — farmer-led, explicitly licensed, maintained, with public design files — is confirmed absent across two passes (G-GS-05). What occupies the affordability niche instead is closed commercial hardware (MoneyMaker, SunCulture, PAIS Kit, Berin): affordable does not equal open.

### Current 7 — Regulation and standards as access, not control

The regulatory and standards layers open doors without transferring control: the July 2026 FTC/Deere stipulated order mandates repair-resource access on "fair and reasonable terms" for 10 years but does not open Deere's software or data; ISOBUS is a published standard whose vendor-coordinated certification ecosystem coexists with an open implementation (AgIsoStack++) as a separate community effort; the EU Data Governance Act produces intermediation (Agdatahub) with consent design, not ownership. Access, portability, and control are three different properties; only the first is being regulated or standardized into existence.

## Contested claims: re-scored

The four ledger claims move from "unresolved" to provisional verdicts, each traceable to the accumulated evidence. Verdicts are framework-level and re-verifiable.

### C-OSA-01: "Open standards solve vendor lock-in." — Contested; provisional: standards create the possibility of portability, not its practice.

Supporting logic stands (OADA principles, ONDC's open protocol, WAGRI's open interfaces). The counterpoint now has evidence: OADA's implementation and adoption scale is unverified (G-OSA-01); ISOBUS's publication did not produce an open ecosystem without a separate community build; the Deere settlement is repair access, not data portability; no cross-border or cross-vendor export evidence was located (G-DPI-03). Status: leaning contested. Hinge: G-OSA-01.

### C-OSA-02: "Open data creates farmer benefit." — Contested; provisional: benefit depends on the governance of value capture, which is the least evidenced part of the landscape.

Open data exists (GODAN's coordination, Ag Data Commons, national scientific data centers); farmer benefit does not follow automatically. GODAN's impact counters remain placeholders; FBN pools farmer data under venture ownership with an IPO trajectory (G-OSA-20); the records where farmer benefit is structurally designed (NAPDC, JoinData, Agdatahub) have unverified scale and outcomes (G-OSA-20). Status: split — benefit is possible, capture is undetermined. Hinge: G-OSA-20.

### C-OSA-03: "Open-source AI is ready for agrifood deployment." — Contested; provisional: unproven, not disproven.

No sector-specific deployment evidence with an explicit denominator was located (G-OSA-11); global open-AI statistics are not agrifood evidence. Sinong is open weights with a proprietary corpus and no detectable SPDX licence; AgriLLM's asset-level openness is unresolved (G-OSA-07); Common Voice agrifood applications are thin (G-OSA-03); China's open-weight ecosystem is state-adjacent strategic infrastructure, not separable from its politics. Status: remains unproven.

### C-OSA-04: "Open-source seed licensing is equivalent to open-source software licensing." — Rejected, now with cross-cell evidence.

The biological-commons cycle confirms the rejection: OSSL binds derivatives contractually but has no court-tested case, derivative-tracking record, or licensee count (G-OSA-21); OSSI's pledge has uptake without enforceability; Potato Park's customary governance is misrepresented by open-source framing; community seed banks are community-defined, not open access. Biological reproduction, plant-variety rights, and customary law create distinct conditions. Status: rejected as a simple equivalence; the pledge-versus-licence trade-off is the live sub-question.

### Additional contested claims that earned cross-cutting status

- **"Repair access means machines are open" (FTC/Deere coverage).** Rejected as stated: repair-resource access on fair-and-reasonable terms is not open software, open data, or farmer control. Appears in the telematics cell; generalises to every regulatory-access claim.
- **"Open weights = open source" (Sinong press framing).** Rejected: downloadable weights are not source, not training data, not an open corpus. Appears in the China and open-AI cells.
- **"Local capacity = farmer control" (Flying Labs framing).** Contested: service provision is not ownership; localization is structural, farmer control is not. Appears in the Global South and DPI cells.

## Maintenance and value capture (integrated)

The six funding archetypes from the G-OSA-08 pass (grant/project, institutional/state, hybrid open-core, membership/cooperative, volunteer/community, venture/commercial) distribute across the 49 records with three cross-cutting consequences:

1. **Grant-project fragility is the modal pattern** (12 records) — post-grant uncertainty is the default state of open agrifood (ROMI, ISOBlue, REGOSH, Vinduino, OSE, Farm Hack); L'Atelier Paysan's liquidation is the strongest instance.
2. **The reverse puzzle**: AgIsoStack++ is actively maintained with no identified backer — community-scale maintenance without identified funding exists, and its sustainability is unverifiable.
3. **Value capture inverts the openness question**: venture capital assures maintenance (FBN, ET Brain) while making data governance the contested axis (G-OSA-20); donor-funded "farmer-centric" paradigms recommend governance they do not fund; and the hybrid open-core model is the only located answer that aligns maintenance with openness — at minority scale (6 records).

## What this synthesis does not do

- It does not resolve any gap or verify any record; it re-scores claims against existing evidence. The ~15 unverified maintenance statuses remain unverified.
- It does not add new records or new sources; the local-language blind spot (Spanish, Portuguese, French, Hindi, Chinese) is inherited from the cycles.
- It does not rank or score projects; the farmer-agency grades are descriptive of the record, not assessments of intent or quality.
- It does not conduct fieldwork; consent-in-practice, grievance, and opt-out outcomes (G-OSA-18) remain architecture-level evidence.
- It is not a funding, procurement, or policy recommendation.

## Synthesis-driven priority queue

Re-ranked from the register's immediate priority queue by one criterion: which gap, resolved, would change the synthesis most.

1. **G-OSA-01 / Q-OSA-04 — OADA current implementation and adoption.** The hinge for C-OSA-01 (standards and lock-in). Public-source verifiable; the strongest synthesis lever. If OADA shows real compliant implementations, the standards current strengthens; if not, the leaning-contested verdict hardens.
2. **G-OSA-20 follow-up — data cooperative durability (SAOS, GISC).** Tests the one structural-farmer-agency pattern the dossier contains. If a cooperative outside the Netherlands shows verified durable scale, Current 4 gains its empirical anchor; if not, the "governance without durable funding" finding extends.
3. **Local-language cycle (Spanish, Portuguese, French, Hindi) targeting G-GS-05 and G-OSA-19.** The confirmed absence of farmer-led licensed Global South hardware and the unverified China implementation are both English-language findings. This is the collection's largest blind spot (Q-OSA-19) and the only bounded way to test both.
4. **G-OSA-03 / Q-OSA-10 — Common Voice agrifood applications.** The hinge for C-OSA-03's "unproven" verdict; concrete applications would move it, their absence would harden it.
5. **G-OSA-21 — OSSL enforcement and uptake.** Downgraded to track-only: no public court record exists to resolve it; re-check annually rather than run a cycle.

Not moved: **G-OSA-18 (farmer agency in practice)** remains the project's central question, but it is fieldwork-bound; public-source cycles can only add architecture-level evidence.
