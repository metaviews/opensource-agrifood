# Open Agriculture Candidate Batch Review

Research date: 2026-08-01

Status: provisional selection memo based on the varied discovery scan. This document selects candidates for targeted verification; it does not promote them automatically into the curated catalogue.

## Purpose

The discovery scan found more than a few additional projects that could materially diversify the collection. This review compares them in batches and selects the strongest next candidates according to:

- analytical distinctiveness;
- geographic and institutional diversity;
- non-AI contribution;
- evidence already available;
- relevance to the project’s central question;
- ability to compare with an existing example without simply duplicating it.

The central question remains:

> From open tools to open agrifood infrastructure: who controls the data, interfaces, maintenance, and benefits?

## Decision

The next verification batch should contain eight candidates, distributed across five currents:

| Candidate | Current | Why it earns verification priority | Provisional status |
|---|---|---|---|
| LiteFarm | open farm operations | farmer/researcher co-design, agroecology, multilingual use, Kenya connection | selected |
| farmOS | open farm operations | mature open farm-management platform; distinct from ODK | selected |
| ROMI | open hardware and robotics | direct farmer testing; open tools for diversified microfarms | selected |
| AgStack | shared digital infrastructure | ecosystem-scale infrastructure and maintenance/governance question | selected |
| AgroAPI / Embrapa | public agricultural APIs | Brazilian public-research infrastructure and Latin American representation | selected |
| Bioleft | biological commons | Latin American participatory seed and data commons distinct from OSSI | selected |
| Digital Earth Africa crop mapping | African geospatial infrastructure | concrete African institutional partnerships and open workflows | selected |
| Ag Data Commons | public agricultural data infrastructure | curated public research-data infrastructure distinct from GODAN | selected |

These eight are a verification batch, not a new fixed collection ceiling. More examples may be selected after this batch if comparison reveals additional gaps.

## Why these candidates were selected

### 1. LiteFarm — farmer-facing open farm management

LiteFarm is the strongest immediate candidate for a farmer-facing software case. It adds sustainable, diversified, agroecological, and organic farming to a collection currently represented by field-data infrastructure and interoperability rather than day-to-day farm management. Its reported multilingual support, global reach, farmer/researcher development, and 2026 Pan-African workshop create useful lines of inquiry around co-design, language, and institutional support.

Key verification questions:

- What licence covers the code and associated components?
- What does “used in” mean in the public country, farm, and user counters?
- What evidence exists for active use in Africa, Asia, and Latin America?
- Who controls farm data in hosted and self-managed arrangements?
- How is maintenance funded and governed?

### 2. farmOS — mature open farm records and planning

farmOS supplies a missing operational layer: farm planning, records, and management. It can be compared with ODK’s collection role and OADA’s interoperability ambitions without being redundant with either. Its GPL licensing, self-hosting, community forum, and recent release activity make it a strong candidate for studying durability and open-source maintenance.

Key verification questions:

- Which parts of the farmOS ecosystem are covered by which licences?
- What is the current user and deployment geography?
- How do farmers host, migrate, and export their data?
- What governance roles do farmers, developers, researchers, and service providers hold?
- How does the project sustain development?

### 3. ROMI — open robotics for microfarms

ROMI introduces open hardware and robotics while retaining a direct relationship to farming practice. Its explicit focus on organic, diversified, human-scale farms and its field testing with professional farmers make it more relevant to this collection than a generic robotics repository. It also opens questions about repairability, labour, affordability, and whether open hardware changes control in practice.

Key verification questions:

- Which hardware, software, designs, and documentation are actually open?
- What licences apply to each component?
- What happened to the project and tools after the funded project period?
- What do the field trials show about labour, affordability, and usability?
- Who can manufacture, repair, adapt, and govern the tools?

### 4. AgStack — infrastructure ecosystem

AgStack is not a single application but an ecosystem of agricultural digital-infrastructure repositories. That is precisely its value: it can test whether “open agricultural infrastructure” means more than a collection of public repositories. Its Linux Foundation setting, member-supported model, geospatial identity work, traceability projects, and active repository claims make maintenance and institutional control central questions.

Key verification questions:

- Which repositories are active, deployed, and actually used?
- Do repository licences and data terms align across the ecosystem?
- Who governs priorities: members, maintainers, public institutions, or commercial participants?
- What farmer-facing or public-interest outcomes can be evidenced?
- How does AgStack relate to OADA, OpenAgri, and other interoperability efforts?

### 5. AgroAPI / Embrapa — public agricultural API infrastructure

AgroAPI adds Brazil and a public agricultural research institution to a collection currently weighted toward Europe, North America, global networks, and multilateral organisations. The 2023 peer-reviewed account provides a stronger evidentiary starting point than a project homepage alone. It can illuminate how public research institutions expose data, models, and agricultural services through APIs, and whether openness supports a wider innovation ecosystem.

Key verification questions:

- Are the APIs, code, data, and models open under clearly stated terms?
- Which services remain available and maintained in 2026?
- What documented applications and users exist?
- How are commercial reuse and public benefit balanced?
- Does AgroAPI create interoperability or mainly provide access to institutional services?

### 6. Bioleft — Latin American seed and data commons

Bioleft is a particularly valuable addition because it is not simply a second OSSI. It appears to combine open seed circulation, participatory breeding, collaborative evaluation, publicly available datasets, and community activity in Argentina. It would add Latin American and Spanish-language representation while making the biological-commons category more internally diverse.

Key verification questions:

- What legal or social mechanism governs seed circulation and derivatives?
- What exactly is open in the tomato dataset and participatory protocol?
- Which farmers, breeders, researchers, and communities participate?
- How are decisions, attribution, and benefits governed?
- How does Bioleft relate to OSSI and other open-seed networks?

### 7. Digital Earth Africa crop-type mapping

This is a strong African candidate because the evidence describes a concrete workflow, named institutional partners, country-specific development, and an open repository. It adds remote sensing and geospatial analytics without being another generic AI model. It can help test whether open analytical workflows lead to local technical control or remain dependent on external platforms, funding, and expertise.

Key verification questions:

- What licence governs the crop-type repository and notebooks?
- Which country deployments remain operational?
- Who owns or controls field labels, derived maps, and local data?
- What capacity and maintenance arrangements exist with African partners?
- What value reaches farmers, agencies, or local researchers?

### 8. Ag Data Commons — curated public research-data infrastructure

Ag Data Commons would extend the GODAN case in a different direction. GODAN is primarily a coordination and advocacy network; Ag Data Commons is a curated catalogue and repository connected to USDA-funded research. The contrast could clarify the difference between data coordination, data discovery, repository provision, and actual reuse.

Key verification questions:

- What is the scale and pattern of data reuse?
- How variable are dataset licences and access conditions?
- What is held directly in the repository versus merely catalogued?
- How do USDA eligibility rules shape the commons?
- Does the infrastructure serve communities beyond US-funded research?

## Deferred candidates

The following leads remain important but should not enter the first verification batch.

| Candidate | Why defer rather than reject |
|---|---|
| Tania | Useful comparison with farmOS and LiteFarm, but the current evidence suggests a smaller and potentially less active project; verify repository health later. |
| FarmBot | Strong open robotics/software case, but its relevance may lean toward precision gardening and consumer hardware; compare with ROMI first. |
| Horizon OpenAgri | Potentially important open-software/open-hardware programme, but project claims, repositories, licences, and post-funding durability need a larger verification effort. |
| SAGDA | Valuable African data/AI lead, but currently appears early-stage and should be checked for repository maturity, validation, and licence before promotion. |
| OpenATK | Conceptually valuable for modular apps and farmer-owned data, but public evidence appears historically dated; potentially a historical or archived case. |
| AgroFIMS | Strong possible standards and fieldbook lead, but current maintenance and repository evidence are not yet clear. |
| AgGateway ADAPT | Important interoperability comparison, especially around industry coordination, but should be verified alongside OADA after the first batch. |

Deferral does not mean exclusion. These candidates remain in the discovery scan and can be promoted when they answer a gap that the first batch leaves unresolved.

## What this batch changes in the collection

If verified, the eight selected candidates would add:

- operational farm-management software;
- farmer-facing co-design and agroecological orientation;
- open hardware and robotics;
- infrastructure-scale open-source governance;
- public agricultural APIs;
- Latin American biological commons;
- African geospatial workflows;
- curated public agricultural data infrastructure.

The collection would then have a substantially broader non-AI base and a more useful comparison between:

- tools and infrastructure;
- software and biological materials;
- open code and open data;
- farmer-facing systems and upstream platforms;
- community, public, multilateral, and member-supported governance;
- direct deployment and enabling infrastructure.

## Verification order

The recommended order is:

1. LiteFarm and farmOS — establish the open farm-management comparison.
2. Bioleft and Digital Earth Africa — add Latin American and African grounded cases.
3. ROMI — add open hardware and farmer-tested robotics.
4. AgroAPI / Embrapa and Ag Data Commons — examine public data/API infrastructure.
5. AgStack — examine ecosystem-scale infrastructure and governance after the component landscape is clearer.

This order deliberately starts with cases that are easiest to compare and most likely to expose missing fields in the record template before tackling the broadest ecosystem case.

## Decision rule after verification

A selected candidate should be promoted to the catalogue only if targeted verification establishes:

1. a meaningful open layer with identifiable terms or evidence;
2. a direct agrifood role or defensible enabling role;
3. enough evidence to describe use, governance, or institutional context;
4. a distinct contribution to the comparative collection;
5. clear limitations that can be recorded honestly.

If a candidate fails these tests, it should remain a lead, be marked `archived`, or be retained as a useful negative finding rather than being forced into the catalogue.
