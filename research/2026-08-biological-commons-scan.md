# Biological commons beyond OSSI and Bioleft: discovery scan

Research date: 2026-08-02
Method: bounded discovery cycle expanding the biological-commons cell beyond the existing records (OSSI pledge, Bioleft). Six query sets across licence-based seed systems, Indigenous commons, community seed banks, and seed sovereignty movements; key sources read in full (OpenSourceSeeds licence page and registry, SEARICE seed-work page, Navdanya overview); one source timed out (African Centre for Biodiversity, HTTP 504) and is carried as a lead.

## Why this cell

The collection's biological-commons layer had two records (OSSI's pledge, Bioleft's participatory seed data). This cycle tests the wider field: contractual seed licences, Indigenous territory governance, community seed bank institutions, and sovereignty movements — four mechanisms that are all "seed commons" but with very different governance, and none of which is interchangeable with open-licensing in the software sense.

## Currents

### Current 1 — Licence-based open seed systems
OpenSourceSeeds / OSSL (Germany, Agrecol NGO): a contractual licence protecting seed as a commons, extending obligations to derivatives, compatible with seed law (Kotschi 2018, cited 57). Varieties under licence: Sunviva and Vivagrande/Vivaroma tomatoes, Convento C and Nudelwunder wheats, Lisanco sweetcorn, chilli "Black Heart", potatoes. The enforceable counterpart to OSSI's pledge.

### Current 2 — Indigenous-governed genetic commons
Potato Park (Peru): ~600 native potato varieties under Quechua collective governance — general council of elected community leaders, Inter-Community Agreement for Benefit Sharing / Biocultural Heritage Protocol, CIP repatriation of 410 cultivars, in-situ genetic reserve with Crop Trust (2017). Commons governance as territory, not licence.

### Current 3 — Community seed banks as farmer institutions
SEARICE (Southeast Asia): farmer-led seed systems — SEARICE CONSERVE (1,100+ rice varieties), participatory plant breeding and variety selection, farmer field schools, anti-UPOV advocacy for sui generis PVP. The regional CSB institution; the CSB literature (Vernooy et al.) is the academic anchor.

### Current 4 — Seed sovereignty movements and advocacy
Navdanya (India): the largest seed-sovereignty movement, with community seed banks, Seed Satyagraha, and biopiracy documentation. African Centre for Biodiversity (South Africa/Zimbabwe, "Seed Is Life" documentary 2025) as the African advocacy lead. Movements managing commons as political infrastructure.

## Contested claims

- **OSSL protects the seed commons better than OSSI's pledge (licence advocates).** Asserting: the licence literature and OpenSourceSeeds framing (contract, derivatives bound, seed-law compatible). Contesting: no enforcement case located; the pledge movement (OSSI, 550+ varieties) has far wider uptake; enforceability in practice is untested. Last reviewed: 2026-08-02.
- **Navdanya's community seed bank scale.** Asserting: Navdanya's own pages (111 CSBs in 17 states; "more than 150 community seed banks"). Contesting: Wikipedia reports 54. The divergence is unresolved and none is independently audited. Last reviewed: 2026-08-02.
- **Community seed banks are "open access" commons.** Asserting: commons framing in the CSB literature. Contesting: CSBs are community-managed institutions with community-defined access rules — not open to all, and their sustainability depends on NGO support (Vernooy 2024). Last reviewed: 2026-08-02.
- **Potato Park as an "open source seed" case.** Asserting: occasional open-source framing of the Park. Contesting: the Park's governance is customary-law-based Indigenous territory governance; translating it into open-licensing terms misrepresents it (the project's Sovereign Seeds caution applies). Last reviewed: 2026-08-02.

## Gaps

- **G-BC-01 — OSSL enforcement and uptake evidence.** No court-tested case, derivative-tracking record, or licensee count located. Registered as G-OSA-21.
- **G-BC-02 — Open-source-licensed seeds in the Global South.** No OSSL-type licence-based seed system found in Africa, Asia, or Latin America; pledge systems (OSSI) are the only mechanism with Global South reach. Last searched: 2026-08-02.
- **G-BC-03 — Community seed bank sustainability beyond NGO support.** The Vernooy 2024 sustainability-strategies literature documents the problem; independent evidence of durable CSB economics is thin.

## Actors (critical lens)

- **Asociación ANDES + Potato Park communities** — Indigenous territory governance; the strongest governance case.
- **SEARICE** — regional farmer-led seed institutions.

## What this scan does not yet do

- **No Global South OSSL-type licence census.** G-BC-02 is named but unresolved.
