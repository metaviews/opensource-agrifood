# Energy and Biological Byproducts: the Open Layer in Biogas, Digesters, Agrivoltaics, and Farm Microgrids (C4)

**Register:** G-OSA-40 · **Workstream:** C4 (energy and biological byproducts — application) · **Date:** 2026-10-14

Method: broad sectoral discovery scan and same-day primary-source verification pass across five sub-cells — (1) anaerobic digestion, biogas, and biological byproducts (from DIY/smallholder digesters and open-science process simulators to farm-scale failures and carbon-credit lock-in), (2) agrivoltaics (open optical/crop simulation engines and OSHWA-certified wood/cold-frame racking hardware), (3) farm microgrids, off-grid DC power electronics, and open energy management systems, (4) rural and agricultural energy cooperatives (biomethane, community-bond biogas, farmer solar gardens, statutory rural utilities), and (5) the Canadian frame (the 43-of-75,000 farm digester reality, purged failure records, the FIT-to-RNG/CFR policy whipsaw, and Ontario's prime-agricultural-land solar ban). ## Currents in the field

Four broad currents organize activity across farm energy and biological byproducts:

1. **Appropriate-technology & open-hardware self-reliance (hardware + local operability)** — treats farm energy structures, solar racking, charge controllers, and sub-metering as tools farmers and local fabricators should be able to build, inspect, repair, and run offline without vendor cloud lock-in. Anchors: **Western University FAST Lab / WIRED** (`CA000008`, `CA000025`, `CA000071`), **Libre Solar** (`DE000153`), **OpenEnergyMonitor** (`emon32`, `emoncms`), **OpenEMS** (`OpenEMS Association e.V.`), and **Solar CITIES** (DIY IBC biodigesters).
2. **Open-science simulation & public facility transparency (models + public-domain census data)** — builds permissive Python/RADIANCE/ODE simulation engines and public-domain facility registries so agrivoltaic shading, anaerobic digestion biochemistry, and digester mortality can be audited independently of developer claims. Anchors: **NLR (formerly NREL) InSPIRE & `bifacial_radiance`**, **University of Liège `PASE`**, **US EPA AgSTAR Livestock Anaerobic Digester Database**, and the **IWA ADM1 Python stack (`PyADM1`, `PyADM1ODE`, `QSDsan`)**.
3. **Cooperative infrastructure aggregation (ownership without open artifacts)** — uses producer cooperatives, solidarity cooperatives, community bonds, and statutory rural utility associations to cross the capital ($10M+ RNG upgrading skids) and grid-interconnection (transformer capacity) walls that block individual farms, while purchasing conventional turnkey commercial hardware and software. Anchors: **Coop Agri-Énergie Warwick** (with **Coop Carbone**), **ZooShare Biogas Co-operative**, **AGRIS Solar Co-operative**, and Alberta's **EQUS REA** / **Federation of Alberta Gas Co-ops**.
4. **Carbon-credit & compliance-fuel enclosure (proprietary hardware + MRV/CI gatekeeping)** — shifts the economic engine of biological byproducts from on-farm heat and electricity to tradable carbon credits (Gold Standard, federal Clean Fuel Regulations, BC LCFS) and pipeline-injected renewable natural gas (RNG). In the Global South this finances patented digesters locked to PAYG mobile-money and IoT smart meters; in North America it consolidates farm digestion into developer-owned upgrading plants whose lowest carbon-intensity (CI) scores are treated as commercial secrets. Anchors: **Sistema.bio** (proprietary IoT + Gold Standard carbon credits), **HomeBiogas** (patented domestic digesters + carbon credits), and the **Canadian Biogas Association / CFR RNG regime**.

## Sub-cell 1: Anaerobic digestion, biogas, and biological byproducts — DIY knowledge, open-science simulators, and the failure/carbon wall

- **Open-science biochemical simulation (`ADM1` ecosystem)**: The International Water Association's Anaerobic Digestion Model No. 1 (ADM1, Batstone et al. 2002) has an active, permissively licensed Python layer:
  - **`CaptainFerMag/PyADM1`** (`MIT` verified via GitHub API, 55 stars, 27 forks): Python implementation of ADM1 ring-tested against Benchmark Simulation Model No. 2 (BSM2) for agricultural manure and maize-silage co-digestion (`verified-primary`).
  - **`dgaida/PyADM1ODE`** (`MIT` verified via GitHub API, pushed 2026-09-09): component-based simulator from TH Köln modelling complete agricultural biogas plants — fermenters, substrate pumps, agitators, CHP gensets, heating loops, and gas storage (`verified-primary`).
  - **`QSD-Group/QSDsan`** (`NCSA` licence verified from `LICENSE.txt`, 48 stars, 31 forks, pushed 2026-10-12): UIUC platform coupling ADM1 anaerobic digestion with techno-economic analysis (TEA) and life-cycle assessment (LCA) (`verified-primary`).
- **Smallholder and DIY biodigester designs — workshop openness without formal licences**:
  - **Solar CITIES** (`solarcities.solutions`, led by Dr. T.H. Culhane): global community of practice building intermediate bulk container (IBC) tote and ARTI-hybrid biodigesters from commodity PVC and 1,000-litre totes. Described as an "open-source DIY platform for Biogas Innovators," but **no formal open-hardware licence (`CERN-OHL`/OSHWA) or CAD repository exists**; the primary build manual (*How to Build a Solar CITIES IBC Tank Biodigester*) is distributed as a commercial Lulu print/ebook manual, and troubleshooting runs in a Facebook group ("Methanogens") (`verified-primary`). Like Kon-Tiki and Compost for Good in `G-OSA-23`, openness lives in workshop transfer and commodity-part buildability rather than legal licensing.
  - **`goscommons/Biogas`** (`GPL-3.0` verified via GitHub API, 3 stars, 2018): small-farm mesophilic biodigester documentation, hobby/single-build scale (`verified-primary`, `not-evidence-of-scale`).
  - **Adjacent thermal byproduct layer already in corpus**: **Open Source Ecology** (`open-source-ecology.md`) and **IBI Open Source Biochar Technologies** (`ibi-open-source-biochar.md`, including the Liberty Biofuel switchgrass gasifier and the 2024 32-page open-source pyrolysis plant engineering guidelines).
- **Commercial household and smallholder enclosures (counter-examples)**:
  - **HomeBiogas Ltd.** (Israel, TASE: HMGS): markets modular domestic/smallholder digesters (2, 4, and 7 m³) and bio-toilets; **protected by multiple utility patents** (Patent #1 published 2014, expires Dec 2033; Patent #2 on exoskeletal flexible casing published 2019, expires Dec 2037; patented mechanical gas-pressure regulation), chaired the creation of ISO 23590 (2020) for household biogas systems, and finances developing-world deployments through Gold Standard carbon-credit projects (e.g., GS13147 in Rwanda) (`verified-primary`).
  - **Sistema.bio** (Mexico-founded B Corp, >50,000 modular digesters across Kenya, India, and Latin America): pairs prefabricated linear low-density polyethylene (LLDPE) digesters with **proprietary IoT smart biogas meters** (developed with Inclusive Energy), Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) mobile-money lockout controls, and Gold Standard carbon-credit monetization (`verified-independent`). Where `G-OSA-15` found farmer-led licensed hardware absent in the Global South, smallholder biogas scales through **carbon-credit-financed proprietary hardware with remote IoT telemetry**, not open designs.
- **Farm-scale digester mortality — what the US EPA AgSTAR `Shutdown` sheet reveals**:
  - The **US EPA AgSTAR Livestock Anaerobic Digester Database** (`epa.gov/agstar/livestock-anaerobic-digester-database`, US public domain, downloadable `.xlsx` verified 2026-10-14; data through June 2024, page updated 2026-07-10) records **473 active/construction rows** (400 Operational, 73 Construction) in Sheet 1 and **333 shut-down farm digesters** in Sheet 2 (`Shutdown`) (`verified-primary`).
  - The 333-row `Shutdown` tab is the single best public dataset on why farm-scale biological byproduct systems fail: **vendor bankruptcy and abandonment** (*Keewaydin Farm*, VT: "Faulty mixing design (prototype digester); lack of company support (company defunct)"; *Riverview Farm*, VT: "Engine corrosion from lack of H2S control. Lack of support from genset provider (2G Energy) and digester company"; *Westpoint* & *Whitesides Dairies*, ID: "Intrepid bankrupt"), **utility interconnection fees and poor buyback rates** (*Double S Dairy*, WI: "The digester was too small and the utility interconnect fee too high (and rates too low) for project expansion"; *Eden-Vale* & *CAL-Denier Dairies*, CA: "Operable, but poor buyback"), **operator knowledge lock-in** (*Swine USA / Bell Farms*, IA: "The current owners do not have the technical knowledge to run the digester"), and **developer capture/pivot away from manure** (*Linkenmeyer Family Feeders*, IA: "System bought by Big Ox Energy, feedstock switched to industrial waste").

## Sub-cell 2: Agrivoltaics — the corpus's strongest open-science and OSHWA-certified hardware layer

- **Open optical, irradiance, and crop-growth simulation commons**:
  - **`NatLabRockies/bifacial_radiance` & `NatLabRockies/InSPIRE`** (National Laboratory of the Rockies, formerly NREL / US DOE): both **BSD-3-Clause** (verified via GitHub API and `LICENSE.md`, 2026-10-14; `bifacial_radiance` 109 stars, 57 forks, pushed 2026-09-25), built atop **`pvlib/pvlib-python`** (`BSD-3-Clause`, 1,694 stars, 1,254 forks, pushed 2026-10-08) (`verified-primary`).
  - **InSPIRE Agrivoltaics Map (`openei.org/wiki/InSPIRE/Agrivoltaics_Map`)**: **CC0 1.0 Public Domain** (verified on OpenEI page footer, last edited 2026-10-13, with direct CSV export), tracking **367+ US agrivoltaic sites totalling 5,076 MWdc across 35,157 acres** by crop species, livestock grazing type, habitat restoration, PV technology, and array geometry (`verified-primary`).
  - **`PASE` (Python Agrivoltaic Simulation Environment, University of Liège — DEAL / TERRA)**: **MIT License** (verified from `gitlab.uliege.be/deal-public/pase/-/raw/main/LICENSE`, active 2026-10-13; Bruhwyler et al. 2024 *Agroforestry Systems*), coupling 3D VTK ray-tracing and `pvlib` with spatialized crop growth models (GRASSIM, STICS) and microclimate evapotranspiration physics (`verified-primary`).
- **OSHWA-certified open agrivoltaic hardware in Canada — Western University FAST Lab / WIRED**:
  - Led by Dr. Joshua M. Pearce at Western University (London, Ontario), the Free Appropriate Sustainability Technology (FAST) Research Group and its 1-hectare **Western Innovation for Renewable Energy Deployment (WIRED)** field station directly overturn `G-OSA-13`'s finding that no agricultural hardware in the corpus held OSHWA certification:
    - **OSHWA UID `CA000008` — Parametric Open-Source Cold-Frame Agrivoltaic Systems (POSCAS)** (certified 2021-11-12): Hardware **CERN-OHL-P-2.0**, Documentation **CC BY 4.0**, OSF `osf.io/k6xwv/` (`verified-primary`).
    - **OSHWA UID `CA000025` — Open-Source Vertical Swinging Wood-Based Solar Photovoltaic Racking Systems** (Vandewetering, Hayibo & Pearce, certified 2023-03-03): Hardware, Software, and Documentation **CERN-OHL-S-2.0** (Appropedia `CC-BY-SA-4.0`, OSF `osf.io/qd4ca/`, *Designs* 2023). Engineered to National Building Code of Canada wind and snow loads using domestic pressure-treated wood and a swinging hinge mechanism, cutting capital cost by 22% vs commercial metal vertical racking and LCOE to 77% of conventional racking (`verified-primary`).
    - **OSHWA UID `CA000071` — Open-Source Customizable Thermal Energy Storage Module** (certified 2026-01-15): Hardware, Software, and Documentation **GPL-3.0-only**, OSF `osf.io/rucqy/` (`verified-primary`).
  - **The `G-OSA-14` replication reality**: Even where `G-OSA-13` is solved with formal `CERN-OHL-S-2.0` licensing and OSHWA certification (`CA000025`), Appropedia's own project status box records **`Status: Designed, Modelled | Verified by: FAST | Made: No | Replicated: No`** (while `CA000008` is recorded as **`Status: Prototype | Made: Yes | Replicated: No`**) (`verified-primary`, `not-evidence-of-scale`). Physical outdoor deployment is taking place at Western's WIRED field station, while commercial farm adoption in Ontario is blocked by provincial procurement and land-use rules (Sub-cell 5).

## Sub-cell 3: Farm microgrids, off-grid DC power electronics, and open energy management systems

- **OpenEMS (`github.com/OpenEMS/openems`, OpenEMS Association e.V., Germany)**: **1,588 stars, 690 forks, active 2026-10-13**. Dual-licensed by component (verified from `README.md`, `LICENSE-EPL-2.0.txt`, and `LICENSE-AGPL-3.0.txt`): **EPL-2.0** on `OpenEMS Edge` (local on-site PLC/IPC control) and `OpenEMS Backend`, and **AGPL-3.0** on `OpenEMS UI` (`verified-primary`). Includes over 200 modular OSGi bundles with native local Modbus/CAN control for **Victron Energy** (`io.openems.edge.victron`), SMA, Fronius, Kostal, GoodWe, **biogas/natural-gas CHP gensets** (`io.openems.edge.controller.chp.soc`), heat pumps, dynamic tariffs, peak shaving, and off-grid islanding. Where `G-OSA-22` found no open software at commercial greenhouse scale, `OpenEMS` is a consortium-backed, production-proven industrial controller deployed across tens of thousands of European commercial and agricultural storage systems (via FENECON and Association members).
- **OpenEnergyMonitor (`openenergymonitor`, Wales, UK)**: resolves the licence question flagged in `cea/research/cold-climate-energy-readings.md`. While legacy `openenergymonitor/emonpi` (279 stars) and `avrdb_firmware` return `license: null` on the GitHub API, the time-series web platform **`emoncms/emoncms`** (1,322 stars, 531 forks, pushed 2026-10-11) is **AGPL-3.0**, **`EmonLib`** (629 stars) is **AGPL-3.0**, and the current-generation STM32 hardware/firmware stack — **`emon32`** (emonPi3 & emonTx6 hardware) and **`emonHP`** (Heat Pump Monitoring System V2) — is explicitly licensed **CC-BY-SA-4.0** (hardware) and **GPL-3.0** (`emon32-fw`, pushed 2026-10-14) (`verified-primary`). It provides the verified open measurement instrument for the CEA case study's northern energy-cost spine.
- **Libre Solar (`github.com/LibreSolar`, Germany / EnAccess Foundation)**: open-hardware DC microgrid power electronics — MPPT/PWM solar charge controllers and LFP battery management systems. **`Libre Solar BMS C1`** holds OSHWA certification **`DE000153`** (certified 2025-05-15: Hardware **CERN-OHL-W-2.0**, Firmware **Apache-2.0** across `bms-firmware` [267 stars] and `charge-controller-firmware` [190 stars, pushed 2026-07-07], Documentation **CC-BY-SA-4.0** on `learn.libre.solar` [243 stars]) (`verified-primary`). Funded by the EnAccess Foundation to enable local manufacturing and repair of off-grid solar controllers without PAYG cloud lockouts.
- **Victron Energy `venus` OS (`github.com/victronenergy/venus`)**: 781 stars, 308 forks, active 2026-10-13 (`license: null` on the meta-repository, assembling GPL/MIT/Apache Linux packages) (`verified-primary`). Runs on Victron GX devices across off-grid farm solar installations, exposing local Modbus-TCP, MQTT, and SignalK interfaces that allow full local operation and integration with `OpenEMS` or `Home Assistant` without cloud dependency, though the underlying inverter/MPPT hardware remains proprietary.

## Sub-cell 4: Rural and agricultural energy cooperatives — ownership without open artifacts

Testing the corpus's recurring governance pattern (`G-OSA-33` IGFC, `G-OSA-35` Terre de Liens, `G-OSA-39` Arctic Co-operatives):

- **Coop Agri-Énergie Warwick (Warwick, Centre-du-Québec, `agrienergie.coop`, operational 2021)**: **Canada's first agricultural producer cooperative dedicated to renewable natural gas (RNG)**. Unites **12 dairy farms and a local cheese producer** in MRC d'Arthabaska with **Coop Carbone** (a Quebec solidarity co-op that developed and operates the plant); **$12M capital project** ($3M Quebec Technoclimat grant + $1.7M CED repayable contribution), processing **50,000 tonnes/yr** of manure and regional organic residues into **2.3 million m³/yr (~83,500 GJ/yr per CBA 2026 Appendix B)** of RNG injected into the **Énergir** network under a **20-year off-take contract**, returning digestate fertilizer to member farms (`verified-primary`). Digesters (Dominion & Grimm) and upgrading skids (Air Science) are commercial turnkey systems; zero open hardware, software, or open data artifacts are published.
- **ZooShare Biogas Co-operative Inc. (Toronto, ON, `zooshare.ca`, operational 2021-04-01)**: Canada's first community-bond-financed biogas co-op (**800+ members, >$5M raised in community bonds**, Limited Partnership with Oshawa Power; Amended REA No. 4501-9VJK3N issued 2025-04-22). Processes **17,000 tonnes/yr** (2,000 t Toronto Zoo manure + 15,000 t GTA grocery/food waste) in a commercial **Fitec Self-Cleaning digester** to generate **500 kW** under an Ontario FIT2 contract expiring 2038 (`verified-primary`). Community-bond model and educational materials are shared; plant technology and operating data are closed.
- **AGRIS Solar Co-operative (Chatham-Kent / Southwestern Ontario, `agrissolarcoop.ca`)**: spun out by AGRIS Co-operative (a farmer-owned grain/input co-op) and managed with GECO by Red Jar Energy Partners; **1,000+ farmer and rural landowner members** owning ~**15 MW across 250 solar sites**, including **20 multi-owner "Solar Gardens"** created so neighbours could pool capital on a single connection point to bypass rural distribution transformer bottlenecks under Ontario's FIT/microFIT programmes (`verified-primary`).
- **Alberta's statutory rural utility cooperatives (*Rural Utilities Act*)**: **EQUS REA Ltd.** (Canada's largest member-owned Rural Electrification Association, serving farm and rural members across 26 Alberta municipal districts) and the **Federation of Alberta Gas Co-ops Ltd.** (>100,000 km of member-owned rural gas pipelines supplying >25 million GJ/yr to >165,000 rural customers — the world's largest rural gas co-op system) (`verified-primary`). Both prove that cooperative ownership can hold rural energy distribution grids at massive scale, while running conventional closed utility software.

## Sub-cell 5: The Canadian frame — 43 digesters out of 75,000 farms, purged failure records, and the Ontario agrivoltaics ban

- **The 43-of-75,000 agricultural digester reality**:
  - Per the Canadian Biogas Association's NRCan-funded *2026 Canadian Biogas & RNG Market Report* (published 2026-04-30, covering 2024 operational data), Canada has **292 total operational biogas/RNG facilities** generating 32.3 PJ/yr, of which **only 43 are operational agricultural AD facilities** (**31 in ON, 3 in BC, 3 in QC, 3 in NS, 1 in AB, 1 in NB, 1 in NL; 0 in SK, MB, PE**), processing >1,170,000 wet tonnes/yr of manure and on-farm organics to generate 2 PJ (`verified-primary`).
  - Against ~75,000 Canadian farms with livestock (CANZA / Énergir House of Commons FINA brief, Sept 2025), **fewer than 0.06% of Canadian livestock farms operate an anaerobic digester**, and only 14% of Canada's readily available organic feedstock potential is tapped (`verified-primary`).
- **Closed industry surveys and purged failure history vs. US EPA AgSTAR**:
  - Unlike the US EPA AgSTAR database (which publishes every active and shut-down farm digester in a public-domain Excel file), Canada has no public facility-level registry of agricultural digesters — only CBA's aggregate provincial counts (Appendix A) and a 30-row list of dedicated RNG plants (Appendix B).
  - Crucially, **Footnote 8 of the 2026 CBA report discloses**: *"Based on 2024 operational data. The updated database removed smaller, pilot-scale facilities that are no longer operational."* (`verified-primary`). By purging shut-down small and pilot facilities rather than publishing a `Shutdown` sheet like EPA AgSTAR, the Canadian industry record erases small-scale failure evidence from public view.
- **Policy whipsaw from farm-scale electricity (FIT) to centralized RNG and confidential carbon intensity**:
  - Ontario's 31 farm digesters (72% of Canada's total) were built largely under Ontario's 2009–2016 Feed-In Tariff (FIT) electricity programme; when FIT ended, small farm-scale CHP digester builds stalled.
  - Current federal and provincial incentives — the federal **Clean Fuel Regulations (CFR)** (credit average $157.07 in 2024, $142.19 in Q2 2025), **Bill C-15** (enacted 2026-03-26, expanding the 30% Clean Technology Investment Tax Credit to waste-biomass electricity/heat retroactive to Nov 2023), Quebec's **Bill 69** (June 2025) and Énergir's 10%-by-2030 RNG mandate (up to $45/GJ for small projects <5M m³), and BC's **LCFS** ($238.52/credit average Nov 2025) — reward **pipeline-upgraded RNG and verified lifecycle Carbon Intensity (CI)** rather than local farm electricity (`verified-primary`).
  - In ECCC's June 2025 CFR Credit Market Report (cited in CBA 2026 Table 1, p. 12), RNG had the lowest average carbon intensity of any low-carbon fuel (-20.4 gCO₂e/MJ across 9 pathways), with a range down to -106 gCO₂e/MJ — **and the lowest individual facility CI values are withheld as confidential** (Table 1 note *d*) (`verified-primary`). Meanwhile, in Ontario, the Ontario Energy Board (OEB) **denied Enbridge Gas's Low-Carbon Voluntary Program ratepayer cost-recovery in May 2025**, leaving Ontario farmers without a mandated RNG off-take mechanism (`verified-primary`).
- **The Ontario agrivoltaics policy wall**:
  - Ontario hosts Canada's leading open-source agrivoltaics hardware group (Western University FAST Lab / WIRED) and ~94% of Canada's historical solar PV capacity — yet Ontario's August 2024 ministerial direction for the IESO **Long-Term 2 (LT2) procurement bans ground-mounted solar in prime agricultural areas (Classes 1–3) and bans all projects in specialty crop areas**, with no dual-use agrivoltaics exemption (`verified-independent`). While Alberta (AUC Rule 007 interim agro-voltaic information requirements, 2024–2025) and BC (Agricultural Land Commission pilot approvals) have opened narrow regulatory lanes, Ontario's ban forces commercial developers onto non-prime land (e.g., Sturgeon Lake Agrivoltaics, 13 MWac sheep-grazing project in Kawartha Lakes) and leaves FAST Lab's OSHWA-certified wood racking (`CA000025`) without a commercial grid-procurement pathway on prime farmland.

## Contested claims

- **C-OSA-07 — "Smallholder biogas in the Global South is an open, appropriate-technology commons"**:
  - *Claim in plain language*: Domestic and small-farm biodigesters in low- and middle-income countries scale as open, locally reproducible appropriate technology.
  - *Sources asserting*: Solar CITIES practitioner literature and appropriate-technology wikis (`solarcities.solutions`, Appropedia biogas pages) framing IBC and ARTI digesters as an open grassroots movement.
  - *Sources contesting*: Patent and market records for **HomeBiogas** (utility patents running to 2033–2037; ISO 23590 standardisation; Gold Standard carbon projects) and **Sistema.bio** (>50,000 installations financed by carbon credits and monitored via proprietary IoT smart biogas meters and PAYG mobile-money lockouts), plus the absence of any formal open-hardware licence on Solar CITIES' own Lulu manual.
  - *Provisional reading (2026-10-14)*: DIY workshop designs (`Solar CITIES`) are real at community-workshop scale without formal licences, but scaled deployment (>50,000 units) is overwhelmingly driven by carbon-credit-financed proprietary hardware with remote IoT metering.
- **C-OSA-08 — "On-farm anaerobic digestion is a viable decarbonization and revenue pathway for typical Canadian livestock farms"**:
  - *Claim in plain language*: Anaerobic digestion offers broad on-farm economic and climate returns across Canadian livestock agriculture.
  - *Sources asserting*: Canadian Biogas Association (*2025 Biogas Insights*, *2026 Canadian Biogas & RNG Market Report*) and CANZA/Énergir policy briefs arguing that agricultural RNG and Clean Fuel Regulations (CFR) credits unlock major farm revenue and tap an 86%-untapped feedstock reserve.
  - *Sources contesting*: The CBA 2026 report's own denominator (**43 operational agricultural digesters across Canada** out of ~75,000 livestock farms, with shut-down small/pilot plants purged in Footnote 8), the **US EPA AgSTAR `Shutdown` sheet** (333 shut-down US farm digesters citing excessive operating cost, H₂S corrosion, defunct vendors, and poor utility buyback), and the fact that Quebec's flagship farmer success (**Coop Agri-Énergie Warwick**) required pooling 12 dairy farms with a technical solidarity co-op (`Coop Carbone`), $4.7M in public grants/loans, and a 20-year Énergir RNG contract.
  - *Provisional reading (2026-10-14)*: Single-farm AD is economically fragile below very large herd sizes or specialized co-digestion tipping fees; where smaller farms succeed in the RNG era, it is through multi-farm cooperative aggregation (`Warwick`) backed by state capital grants and mandated utility off-take.
- **C-OSA-09 — "Prime-agricultural-land solar bans protect farmers and food production from energy sprawl"**:
  - *Claim in plain language*: Prohibiting ground-mounted solar on prime farmland (Classes 1–3) protects agricultural capacity.
  - *Sources asserting*: Ontario's August 2024 Ministerial Direction to the IESO for the LT2 procurement and municipal "unwilling host" resolutions aimed at preventing conventional fenced solar farms from removing cropland.
  - *Sources contesting*: **Western University FAST Lab** peer-reviewed research, **Agrivoltaics Canada**, and the **Ontario Federation of Agriculture (OFA)** (which supports dual-use agrivoltaics only when agricultural production remains primary), demonstrating that elevated or vertical bifacial agrivoltaics (`CA000025`, `PASE`) maintains crop/forage yields while diversifying farm income — and that blanket prime-farmland bans without an agrivoltaics definition block farmer-owned and research-grade dual-use systems on the 94% of Canadian solar demand located in Ontario.
  - *Provisional reading (2026-10-14)*: Blanket bans conflate single-use utility solar sprawl with dual-use agrivoltaics; without a performance-based agrivoltaics standard (requiring sustained crop yield), the policy protects farmland from solar developers by also locking farmers out of open-hardware dual-use generation.

## Resolution

G-OSA-40 (C4) resolves into **four structural shapes**:

1. **A sharp split between solar/electrical openness (the most legally rigorous open hardware and software in the corpus) and biological-byproduct openness (academic models + unlicensed DIY workshops).** On the solar and microgrid side, C4 has the strongest formal licensing stack in the entire repository: OSHWA-certified `CERN-OHL-S-2.0` / `CERN-OHL-P-2.0` agrivoltaic racking (`FAST Lab`, `CA000008`/`CA000025`), OSHWA-certified `CERN-OHL-W-2.0` / `Apache-2.0` DC BMS and MPPT controllers (`Libre Solar`, `DE000153`), `EPL-2.0`/`AGPL-3.0` industrial microgrid control (`OpenEMS`), `AGPL-3.0`/`CC-BY-SA-4.0`/`GPL-3.0` energy and heat-pump monitoring (`OpenEnergyMonitor`), and `BSD-3-Clause`/`MIT` ray-tracing and crop simulation (`bifacial_radiance`, `pvlib`, `PASE`). On the biological-byproduct side (anaerobic digestion, biogas), openness is restricted to **academic `ADM1` differential-equation simulators** (`PyADM1`, `PyADM1ODE`, `QSDsan`) and **unlicensed DIY appropriate-technology workshops** (`Solar CITIES`). Zero open-licensed farm-scale digester hardware or PLC control software exists.
2. **The `G-OSA-13` / `G-OSA-14` inversion: OSHWA certification is solved in the Canadian lab, while farmer replication is blocked at the regulatory and grid wall.** C4 overturns `G-OSA-13`'s finding that no Canadian agricultural hardware holds OSHWA certification or uses `CERN-OHL` (`CA000008`, `CA000025`). Yet `CA000025` (the farmer-buildable wood vertical bifacial rack) sits at `Made: No | Replicated: No` on Appropedia while Ontario's IESO LT2 procurement bans ground-mounted solar on prime agricultural land without an agrivoltaics carve-out. Here the open design arrived before the land-use and grid-interconnection pathway allowed farmers to build it.
3. **Carbon credits and RNG upgrading as the new enclosure of biological byproducts.** Both at smallholder scale in the Global South (`Sistema.bio`, `HomeBiogas`) and at commercial farm scale in North America (federal CFR, BC LCFS, pipeline RNG), the economics of anaerobic digestion now depend on **monetizing methane avoidance and lifecycle Carbon Intensity (CI)** rather than local farm electricity or heat. In the Global South, carbon finance underwrites patented/prefabricated digesters locked to PAYG mobile-money and proprietary IoT smart meters; in Canada, it shifts farm digestion from 500 kW FIT electricity engines to $12M+ RNG upgrading skids where the lowest facility CI scores are withheld as commercial secrets in federal CFR reports.
4. **Public failure records vs. curated industry surveys — and cooperative aggregation as the only small-farm bridge across the capital wall.** The US EPA AgSTAR database (public domain, 473 active/construction + 333 shut-down digesters) makes the mortality of farm-scale AD legible: defunct vendors, H₂S engine corrosion, utility interconnect fees, and poor buyback rates killed over 300 US farm digesters. Canada's NRCan-funded CBA Market Report (2026) keeps its facility database closed and silently purged shut-down pilot/small facilities from the 2024 count (leaving 43 agricultural digesters across ~75,000 livestock farms). Where smaller farms do cross the capital and transformer threshold — **Coop Agri-Énergie Warwick** (12 dairy farms + Coop Carbone in QC), **AGRIS Solar Co-op** (1,000+ Ontario farmers, 20 Solar Gardens), **ZooShare** (800+ community bondholders), and Alberta's statutory REAs/Gas Co-ops — they do so through **cooperative ownership on top of closed turnkey equipment**.

**Headline:** *In farm energy and biological byproducts, the solar and microgrid stack holds the most formally licensed and OSHWA-certified open hardware and software in the corpus — stalled on Ontario farms by grid and land-use rules — while anaerobic digestion is enclosed at both ends by carbon-credit MRV, $10M+ RNG upgrading skids, and closed industry databases that purge small-farm failures.*

## What this scan does not do

- **It does not cover liquid first-generation grain/oilseed biofuels (corn ethanol, canola biodiesel) in depth**: liquid transport fuels in Canada (3.3B L/yr ethanol, 0.5B L/yr biodiesel per NRCan June 2026) operate at industrial refinery scale (Bunge, Viterra, Greenfield Global) with no farm-scale or open-source layer; this scan focuses on on-farm and community-scale energy and biological byproducts (biogas/AD, digestate, pyrolysis/biochar, agrivoltaics, farm microgrids).
- **It does not verify field-level performance of smallholder PAYG digesters in East Africa or India beyond published Gold Standard / Inclusive Energy documents**: independent farmer-level interviews on PAYG lockout rates or carbon-credit revenue sharing in Sistema.bio deployments would require fieldwork (`G-OSA-15` residual).
- **It does not audit municipal or utility interconnection queues across all ten Canadian provinces**: the regulatory analysis focuses on the federal CFR/ITC layer and the four provinces that account for almost all Canadian agricultural biogas and solar activity (Ontario, Quebec, Alberta, and British Columbia).
- **It does not model a farm-specific microgrid or digester cost spine**: this document is an environmental scan (`G-OSA-40`), not a viability case study; if a viability case on an open farm microgrid or cooperative digester is opened later, it should be framed separately with its own cost spine.
