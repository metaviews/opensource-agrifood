# Scan: Agroecology and open source — the correlation structure (G-OSA-24)

Research date: 2026-08-12
Method: bounded analytical/mapping pass (first cycle on this cell). The cell is a *correlation structure* between two currents — agroecology (as a science, practice set, and social movement) and open source (as a licensing, design, governance, and knowledge-sharing idiom) — not a search for a single physical object. Sources: (a) existing corpus records already in `examples/records/` (L'Atelier Paysan, REGOSH Open Agroecology Lab, Farm Hack, Bioleft, OSSI, OSSL, Potato Park, SEARICE, Navdanya, Open Source Ecology, Think Oggún, Appropedia/OSAT, LiteFarm — named in the catalog and register as agroecology-relevant); (b) two current-state references pulled for this cycle — the AGROECOLOGY European Partnership (three co-funded calls 2024-2026, agroecology living labs and research infrastructures, member-state co-funding, no open-source or commons framing located) and the IPES-Food paper "Head in the Cloud" (Feb 2026, critical of digital-ag enclosure, names commons/open-source/gift-economy frameworks as counter-current); (c) two conceptual anchors — Sievers-Glotzbach et al. (2020) "Diverse Seeds — Shared Practices: Conceptualizing Seed Commons" (International Journal of the Commons, 4 Seed Commons criteria: collective responsibility, protection from private enclosure, collective polycentric management, sharing of formal and practical knowledge; CC BY 4.0) and Prager I. (2021) "'open source' agriculture" (Agriculture and Human Values, 38:947-962, mapping the rationales + tensions). Purpose: establish what the correlation between agroecology and open source looks like in the record — where it is strong, where it is rhetorical, where it is absent, and what the tensions are.

## Headline finding

**Agroecology and open source correlate most strongly at the knowledge-and-governance layer, weakly at the hardware-design layer, and are structurally in tension at the digital-platform layer.** Four correlation shapes emerge from the record:

1. **Open-source seed as a deliberate bridge** — OSSI (pledge), OSSL (licence), Bioleft (Argentina, participatory commons + open data), and the Sievers-Glotzbach Seed Commons criteria (collective responsibility, protection from enclosure, polycentric management, knowledge sharing) all explicitly marry a commons/open-source idiom to seed biology. This is the one sub-domain where "open source" is the *designed* instrument of an agroecological goal (seed sovereignty, anti-enclosure). The bridge is real but contested: OSSI pledge has uptake without enforcement; OSSL has contractual teeth with no located enforcement case (G-OSA-21); Bioleft adds open data to the seed commons; the Seed Commons criteria are a conceptual bridge, not a movement-wide instrument.

2. **Open-source farm machinery and tool design as a structural but fragile bridge** — L'Atelier Paysan (France, SCIC cooperative, 2009-2026, liquidated April 2026) is the strongest located case: open designs for agroecologically-appropriate machinery, farmer self-build training, cooperative governance. Farm Hack (US/UK, FAO agroecology hub record, peer design-sharing, candidate) is the looser decentralized counterpart. Think Oggún (machine-data/telematics) is the inverse: "open system" manufacturing model with a 5-year licence fee. Open Source Ecology (Global, GPL/CC-BY-SA tractor and farm machinery set, curated record) is the open-hardware engine but not agroecology-specific. The bridge is structurally coherent — agroecological farmers need tools the mainstream won't make, open design addresses that — but the Fragility finding from G-OSA-08/L'Atelier Paysan is the bridge's limiting fact: farmer-governed open hardware can be liquidated. The successor structures (Communs Paysans, Soudons fermes!) are attempting to protect the design assets post-liquidation; whether they survive intact is unresolved.

3. **Open-source digital infrastructure as a partial, contested bridge** — REGOSH's Open Agroecology Lab (Argentina, farmOS + SurveyStack + open sensing collaborations, co-designed data governance, Shuttleworth-funded, activity documented 2018-2023, post-2023 unverified) is the strongest located case of an open-source *digital* infrastructure deliberately built for agroecological knowledge systematization. LiteFarm (open farm operations, farmer/researcher co-design, agroecology, multilingual, candidate) and farmOS itself (producer-owned FMIS, curated) extend the thread. But the IPES-Food "Head in the Cloud" paper (2026) frames digital agriculture as a vector of enclosure — "over 70% of agrifood innovations were digitally [delivered]" and "platforms… standardize and digitize farming knowledge" — and names commons, gift economies, and open-source frameworks as counter-currents. The correlation is real at the infrastructure-design layer but contested at the deployment layer: open-source farm software exists; whether it is the dominant or countervailing force in agroecological digital practice is not settled.

4. **The "open source" label is absent from institutional agroecology framing** — The AGROECOLOGY European Partnership (Horizon Europe Cluster 6, three co-funded calls closed 2024-2026, agroecology living labs + research infrastructures, member-state co-funding) does not surface any open-source, commons, or license-framing in its public materials located for this cycle; it frames the transition in terms of living labs, research infrastructures, value-chain and policy assessment, genetic diversity, and farmer empowerment — cooperative and participatory methods, but not "open source" as a term or instrument. FAO's scaling-up paper (2020) explicitly mentions "open source data and technology" as one element of agroecology scaling, but FAO's broader agroecology framework (10 elements, HLPE 2019) does not center open source. This absence is itself evidence: the institutional agroecology current does not naturally speak "open source"; the two movements overlap at practice and knowledge-exchange but not at vocabulary or funding instrument.

**Tension named:** the open-source idiom travels better into agroecology's seed and machinery sub-domains (where a license or a design file is a concrete anti-enclosure tool) than into its digital-platform sub-domain (where "open source" software coexists with platform enclosure and the IPES-Food critique). And the open-source movement's own vocabulary — "free," "open," "commons," "forkability" — does not automatically map onto agroecology's — "food sovereignty," "peasant," "territory," "La Via Campesina," "indigenous knowledge," "knowledge sovereignty."

## Why this cell matters for the project

Three reasons. First, it tests the *comparative synthesis's* headline finding against a different axis: farmer agency is the scarcest layer, but agroecology is the current most explicitly organized around farmer/peasant agency — does the open-source layer strengthen or dilute that agency across sub-domains? Second, it connects several existing records that have not been read against each other as a correlation set: L'Atelier Paysan + REGOSH + Farm Hack + Bioleft + OSSI/OSSL sit on the "open-source tools for agroecology" axis; Agroecology Partnership + FAO agroecology framework + IPES-Food sit on the "institutional agroecology vs digital enclosure" axis; the Seed Commons criteria are the conceptual bridge. Third, it surfaces the vocabulary/tension question directly — which matters for the project's own framing discipline (Q-OSA-03: when is "open-source agrifood" accurate vs "open agrifood infrastructure").

## Contested claims

### "Open source and agroecology are natural allies."

- Asserter: REGOSH project language ("open source technologies and participative practices" for "agroecological transition"); FAO scaling paper ("open source data and technology"); Prager (2021) mapping; OSSI/OSSL as deliberate seed-sovereignty tools.
- Contester: IPES-Food "Head in the Cloud" (2026) frames digital ag as enclosure; the AGROECOLOGY Partnership does not surface open-source framing; agroecology's vocabulary (food sovereignty, peasant, territory) does not map onto open-source vocabulary; OSSI pledge has uptake without enforcement and OSSL has teeth with no located enforcement case.
- Status: **partial, sub-domain-dependent**. Strongest at seed (designed bridge) and machinery (structural but fragile). Contested at digital infrastructure (IPES-Food critique vs REGOSH case). Absent at institutional-vocabulary layer. Not a natural alliance across the board; a correlation that varies by sub-domain and by whether "open source" is the designed instrument or an incidental attribute.

### "Farmer-governed open-source hardware for agroecology is sustainable."

- Asserter: L'Atelier Paysan's 15-year activity, training tours, self-build workshops, successor-structure pledge to protect knowledge from commercial appropriation post-liquidation.
- Contester: L'Atelier Paysan's judicial liquidation (April 2026) is the direct counter-evidence — governance-model strength does not by itself solve funding/maintenance; the survival of the design commons through liquidation is unresolved.
- Status: **contested, evidenced by L'Atelier Paysan's trajectory**. The design-assets survival question is the live hinge.

### "Open-source digital farm infrastructure supports agroecology's knowledge sovereignty."

- Asserter: REGOSH co-design method, farmOS producer-ownership, LiteFarm co-design; FAO open-source-data mention.
- Contester: IPES-Food "Head in the Cloud" — digital platforms standardize and enclose farming knowledge; "open source" software on a platform owned by someone else does not establish knowledge sovereignty; REGOSH's post-2023 activity and the durability of its co-designed governance are unverified.
- Status: **contested, unresolved**. The infrastructure-design layer is open-source; the knowledge-sovereignty outcome is not evidenced.
