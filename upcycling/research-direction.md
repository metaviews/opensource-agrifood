# Research direction

A note on what this concept wants to know, so research can begin informed by the concept
rather than running in parallel to it.

## What the research is for

The concept has structure and it has a thesis (gradual openness, staged; viability rather
than novelty; a micro plant as pilot for a federation). What it does not have is grounding
in Canadian regulatory and business reality. Cost claims made without that grounding would
be the failure mode of every upcycling pitch deck.

The research is bounded on purpose. `research/2026-09-upcycling-subcell-scan.md` (G-OSA-37)
and its six curated records already cover the sector: who the incumbents are, what is
closed, what the standards layer costs, and what open artifacts exist. This concept's
research does not repeat any of that. It answers questions the concept raises that the
parent corpus has never asked, most of which are about a specific place, a specific
jurisdiction, and specific price tags.

## What we are looking for, in priority order

### 1. The regulatory cost of a micro plant in one named jurisdiction

Pick one pilot jurisdiction and get concrete. For Ontario or British Columbia, what does a
small preserving operation actually have to do and pay: food premises requirements under the
provincial regime, municipal licensing, certified food handler obligations, inspection
frequency, and what changes if the operation later sells across a provincial border.

The structural fact is already established (see `economics.md`) and it is a fork, not a
detail: intraprovincial manufacture and sale triggers no CFIA licence, while interprovincial
trade of preserved product does. What that fork costs in practice — in documentation,
facility, and time — is the research question.

### 2. What "validated process" actually requires in Canada

The concept excludes low-acid canning partly because the validation regime is heavier, but
what the high-acid/acidified path requires has not been established for Canada. What must a
small producer do to have a defensible shelf-stable process: laboratory testing, pH and water
activity targets, a process authority arrangement, record-keeping obligations. The United
States has a well-documented statutory model; Canada's equivalent must not be assumed to
mirror it.

This is the highest-value research target in the list, because it determines whether the
second rung of the openness ladder (shared food-safety documentation) is even legally
coherent. If process validation cannot be shared, the concept's central economic argument
weakens substantially.

### 3. Input availability in a real place

Actual volumes, grades, and seasonality of farm-level second-grade and surplus in a named
region: what crops gluts, in what weeks, in what quantities, at what current disposition
(livestock feed, compost, landfill, donation). Include who currently takes it, and on what
terms — the food bank, the feed broker, nobody.

The concept's handling of the contested-stream tension in `tensions.md` is a hypothesis about
grade and timing. This is where it gets tested against a real region rather than argued.

### 4. Packaging and the per-unit cost structure

Jars, lids, labels, cases: real supplier pricing at realistic order quantities, and how much
MOQ forces a small plant to over-buy. The concept's current suspicion — that packaging, not
food, is the largest per-unit input for preserved goods — has no source and needs one, either
way.

### 5. Facility and equipment economics

Two paths, both needing numbers: renting shared certified kitchen time by the hour, versus
leasing and fitting out a small dedicated space. Including the equipment list a high-acid
operation needs, what it costs used versus new, and what a seasonally-idle plant does to the
arithmetic.

### 6. Open artefacts adjacent to a processor

What already exists that a processor could adopt rather than build: open HACCP/PCP templates
or food-safety documentation commons (the corpus believes these are close to absent — verify),
open batch-record or lot-tracking software, open recipe or formulation commons, open label
compliance tooling. Licence verification per house practice — repository metadata, not
marketing claims. Note that the software the corpus has already catalogued is mostly
ordering and structure (Foodsoft, Open Food Network, Odoo), not production records.

### 7. Ownership forms and how they are capitalized

Worker co-op, multi-stakeholder co-op, nonprofit, and conventional small business in Canada:
how each raises money, what each can and cannot do, and what each implies for a pilot that
needs a facility. This connects to the corpus's maintenance-funding findings and its
observation that co-operative forms in food are governance-open and systems-closed by default.

### 8. Demand, priced honestly

What actually sells and at what price: farmers' markets versus independent retail versus
institutional buyers, wholesale margin structure, and whether an upcycled framing carries a
premium or a discount. The market-size literature currently contradicts itself by roughly
half for the same year, and at least one consumer-acceptance study suggests weak hedonic
appeal — both need reading rather than citing.

## What this research must not become

- Not a business plan. The concept wants to know whether the ground is firm, not to project
  three years of revenue.
- Not a re-run of the sectoral scan. Incumbent and standards-layer detail lives in G-OSA-37.
- Not a survey of the whole country. One pilot jurisdiction, chosen, is worth more than
  twelve surveyed.

## What we do after this research

The concept sharpens against it in one of three ways, and the choice should follow from what
is found rather than being decided now:

1. The costs close and the ladder holds — then the concept can state a conditional viability
   claim and name the rung where openness actually pays.
2. The costs close but a rung does not hold — then the concept publishes which rung it drops
   and why, which is still a real result.
3. The costs do not close at micro scale — then the honest output is the negative finding,
   which is a legitimate and valuable result for a project asking whether open agrifood
   operations are viable, and should be recorded as such rather than written around.
