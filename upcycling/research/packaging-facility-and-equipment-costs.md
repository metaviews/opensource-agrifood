Method: quote and list-price gathering for `upcycling/research-direction.md` targets 5 and 6.

limits stated, are recorded. Everything else is recorded as an open line with a named source
to quote from. This is the file where the temptation to fill gaps with plausible numbers is
strongest, so the gaps are left visible.

### Where pricing cannot be obtained without a quote

Canadian packaging distributors that stock food jars — **Richards Packaging**, **Salbro**,
**Consolidated Bottle** — publish catalogues, not prices. Any real cost figure for a plant
has to come from a quote, and the quote's shape matters more than its headline: MOQ, case
breakpoints, pallet quantities, delivery, whether lids are included, and whether a custom
mould or a stock item is being bought.

This is also where the concept's **stage 5 (shared procurement)** argument lives or dies. If
packaging is the largest per-unit input — still an assumption, now testable — then group
buying across a federation is the largest margin lever available, and the MOQ structure of
Canadian distributors is what would decide whether a single micro plant can even reach the
good price tier. That is a question a single quote request can answer, and it has not been
answered here.

### Labels

Published US print-service pricing found 2026-09-11:

- UPrinting: 1,000 roll labels at about **18¢ each**, against 73¢ each at 250 and 68¢ each in
  a 25-quantity configuration — i.e. the unit cost falls by roughly three-quarters between a
  250-run and a 1,000-run.
- PrintRunner: cut-to-size food labels at about **23¢ each** at the quoted quantity.

The pattern is the finding: **label unit cost is dominated by run length, not by the label.**
A plant that cannot commit to a large run pays several times the per-unit price, which is the
same MOQ trap as packaging and reinforces the case for shared procurement.

Canadian sources exist (StickerYou, Toronto, sells food and drink labels) but no Canadian
label price was read in this pass. Unverified for Canada.

### Lids, closures, cases, and secondary packaging

Not priced in this pass. Lug lids, twist caps, tamper seals, shipping cases, dividers, and
pallets are all real per-unit lines, and the jar price above appears to include a lid on the
Uline item and may not on the TricorBraun one. This is a gap, not a rounding error.

### One conclusion that is already firm

Because the low-risk/home-based exemption does not clearly cover canned preserves (see
`ontario-regulatory-path.md`), the facility is a **licensed commercial premises** — whether
that is dedicated space or hourly shared access to an approved kitchen. That removes the
cheapest option (a home kitchen) from the arithmetic before any numbers are filled in, and it
is a consequence of a document, not a preference.

## Equipment

No prices obtained in this pass. This is the weakest section of the file and it is stated as
such rather than filled with plausible figures.

What has to be priced for a high-acid water-bath operation, as a starting list: a
steam-jacketed or direct-fire kettle for batch cooking; a pulper/finisher or food mill for
fruit prep; a filler (manual or piston) and a capper; a water-bath pasteurizer or immersion
unit for sealed jars; a pH meter and, if the process requires it, a water-activity meter;
stainless work tables, sinks to the health unit's standard, and washable shelving; scales
(and note Measurement Canada's requirement that measuring devices used in trade be inspected
by an authorized service provider before use); dry storage, pallet jack, and transport
containers.

Named sources to quote: used food-equipment dealers and auction houses in Ontario, equipment
financing through a Canadian lessor, and any Ontario food-processing technology centre that
advises small processors (Ontario's guide points users to the Agricultural Information Contact
Centre, 1-877-424-1300, and OMAFRA's inspection and guidance pages).

## Insurance

Only US figures were located, and none is transferable:

- General liability for food businesses: published US averages of about $47/month ($563/year)
  and a US provider quoting from $299/year.
- Food trucks: US sources cite $400–$2,000/year.

**No Canadian premium was obtained.** For a food manufacturer, general liability is not the
only or even the main line — product liability and recall coverage are the ones that matter
for a shelf-stable product with a one-year shelf life, and the corpus's own grocery work
flagged insurance as a cost small food businesses systematically under-budget. A Canadian
broker quote is required and is not in this file.

## What this file does not demonstrate

- It does not produce a per-unit cost for a jar of preserves. It produces several verified
  list prices, two verified documents, one secondary Ontario rate, and a list of what still
  requires a quote.
- It does not establish that packaging is the largest per-unit input. That remains an
  assumption; the verified comparison price for a jar ($1.40–$1.50) against an unverified
  food input cost near zero is suggestive but not an analysis.
- It does not price labour, freight, distribution, listing fees, or the cost of the surplus
  itself.
- It does not price equipment at all.
