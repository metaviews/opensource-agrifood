## Suggested next targets, in priority order

1. **Resolve the process-authority line for a high-acid water-bath product in Ontario.** This
   is now the single highest-value question in the concept, because the openness ladder's
   strongest rung depends on it and because it is answerable by asking one named authority.
   Named ask: the Ontario Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Agribusiness's Agricultural
   Information Contact Centre (1-877-424-1300) and the food inspection branch it points to;
   then the public health unit of the chosen pilot municipality; then a laboratory or
   technology centre that performs thermal process validations.
2. **Ask the pilot municipality's health unit whether a rented premises — a shared kitchen or
   a former ghost kitchen suite — can hold approval to *manufacture* shelf-stable preserves.**
   Premises approval attaches to a food premises and its activities, not to a marketing
   category, so this cannot be answered from documents, and it decides whether the cheapest
   facility route is open at all.
3. **Request an AgriKit quote** for a 20-foot unit configured for preserving (steam kettle,
   filler, multiple sinks, walk-in cooler). Named ask: agrikit.ca contact. This is the single
   most useful capital figure obtainable, and AgriKit's grant support for non-profits,
   charities and Indigenous communities makes the funding path part of the same conversation.
4. **Get three real quotes** — one Canadian packaging distributor (case price with lid, at two
   quantity tiers), one label printer (1,000 and 5,000), and one named Ontario shared kitchen
   (hourly rate, minimum booking, equipment and storage included). These are the figures that
   convert this research from documents into a cost model. Manning Canning is the first call.
5. **Price the equipment list** in `packaging-facility-and-equipment-costs.md` against used
   equipment dealers and auctions, and ask one Ontario food-processing technology centre what
   a small high-acid line typically costs to fit out. Verified Canadian used-equipment price
   points exist (steam kettles listed from $6,500 CAD) and should be collected properly.
6. **Ask a Canadian broker for a product liability and recall quote** for a shelf-stable
   food manufacturer. No Canadian figure exists in the tree; the only figures located are US
   and the only GTA figure is a booking platform's claim about general liability.
7. **Then** consider target 4 (commercial surplus terms) with named people, and targets 7–9.
