# Succession — research mapping

The research tree for the succession program. Each cycle is bounded: a named question, a
fixed query budget, one pass, and a statement of what it is expected to change about the
frame in `../concept.md`. Cycles are opened at the user's direction, one at a time or in
parallel, per the project's habit of bounded gap-targeted discovery.

Status vocabulary: `candidate` (framed, not run) → `run` (pass complete, document exists)
→ `verified` (findings cycled through the corpus's verification discipline).

## C1. Succession knowledge as a commons — what open planning knowledge exists? (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c1-succession-knowledge-pass.md`. Headline:
**curation-and-adaptation, not build-from-zero** — Canadian succession knowledge is
rich (provincial guides, the FMC national toolkit, FCC's toolset, CRA rollover
explainers, Quebec-language guides) and nowhere open-licensed; the sector's real
adaptation practice is one-off private permission (Alberta's Agdex 812-22: "Adapted
with permission from the Manitoba Government"); FCC's tools are lender-authored and
terms-verified forkless; the one open-licensed succession asset found anywhere is Land
For Good's CC BY-SA 4.0 workbook (verified in-document) — the legal adaptation base
for a Canadian layer. Three records curated (collection 152). L1 and L3 resolve onto
one shared build object: an open-licensed Canadian adaptation commons for knowledge
and legal documents together.

- **Question:** What open-licensed succession-planning knowledge and tooling exists for
  Canadian agriculture — jurisdictional guides, tax-rollover explainers, family
  negotiation frameworks, open financial models, open worksheets — and where the
  verified absence ("the data is open, the tools are consultants' proprietary
  software", G-OSA-35) actually bites?
- **Cells:** (1) Canadian federal/provincial extension material (OMAFRA, provincial ag
  ministries, FCC's own publications — noting FCC's lender conflict); (2) farm
  organization and nonprofit material (Farm Management Canada, CDRAB/Quebec, Farm
  Centre legacy work); (3) US farm-transition resources (state extension, Farm
  Transition frameworks) as the comparison corpus; (4) open-licensed *tools* —
  spreadsheets, models, worksheets — versus readable-but-closed PDFs, using the land
  scan's asset-by-asset openness discipline.
- **Expected frame change:** whether L1 is a *curation and gap-filling* layer (knowledge
  exists, scattered and closed-licensed) or a *build-from-zero* layer (it does not
  exist).
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, four cells.

## C2. Farm-link platforms — anatomy of the closed layer (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c2-farm-link-pass.md`. Resolution: the
anatomy of the closed layer. The absence re-confirmed (no open-source land-matching
platform exists; GitHub searched); Young Agrarians BCLMP is the best operator found
(free, publicly funded, best outcome reporting: "437 matches on more than 13,210
acres" verified verbatim) but registration-gated and licence-silent; FarmLINK.net
proprietary under FarmStart; L'ARTERRE's login-free Répertoire des offres the closest
Canadian open layer (verified live). **Load-bearing correction to design-doc P7,
applied in place:** no operator publishes matching criteria and no evaluation study
identifies criteria opacity as a failure mode — the documented failure modes are
trust, readiness, external conditions, follow-through (Hersey & Adams verified) — so
P7 is reframed as accountability infrastructure, not a match-rate lever. Two records
curated (`young-agrarians-bclmp.md`; `arterre-repertoire.md` documented in pass;
collection 163).

- **Question:** What do the existing farm-link/land-matching platforms actually do —
  Young Agrarians land matching, FarmLINK Ontario, US state farm-link programs,
  International FarmLink — and what would an open one change?
- **Cells:** (1) platform-by-platform anatomy: who runs it, data ownership, matching
  criteria, success reporting, funding; (2) the verified Canadian absence (no open
  farm-link platform anywhere) re-confirmed against current state; (3) an honest
  alternative-model sketch: is matching an information problem openness can improve, or
  a trust-and-volume problem it cannot (platforms' own reported match rates are the
  datapoint to find)?
- **Expected frame change:** converts L2 from a slogan ("build an open farm-link") into
  a model judgment — including the real possibility that the honest finding is that
  openness helps least at the matching step and most at the criteria/data step.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells.

## C3. Open legal assets for farm tenure — do they exist anywhere? (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c3-open-legal-assets-pass.md`. Headline
finding: a **licensing gap, not a drafting gap** — model legal documents (Agrarian
Commons bylaws/equitable lease, Schumacher CLT toolkit, ELC example lease, government
succession guides) exist free-to-read, but only SELC publishes farm-adjacent legal
resources under a genuine share-alike open licence; Farm Commons licenses only to
paying members under CC BY-ND (no adaptation even for members); The Farmers Land
Trust's "open-source" templates carry a use agreement requiring adoption of its
framework and name. **Verified and curated 2026-11-18**: six records curated
(`examples/records/`: agrarian-commons-legal-guide.md, schumacher-clt-legal-toolkit.md,
selc-legal-resources.md, farm-commons.md, farmers-land-trust-templates.md,
young-agrarians-bc-transition-toolkit.md) plus the Canadian state layer as a seventh
candidate record (canadian-gov-farm-legal-documents.md — OMAFRA Pub 70 explicit Crown
copyright; Manitoba .docx lease and BC workbook carry no licence statement; verified
from downloaded document text layers). Collection now 148 records on disk. The
"OGL status of Canadian government documents" verification target is resolved
(negatively — the state layer is free-to-read, not open). **Residual pass complete
2026-11-18**: other-province audit done (Saskatchewan booklet also unlicensed; QC, PEI,
NS inventoried, licences unverified); Young Agrarians toolkit fully read (process
knowledge only, no legal instruments — legal drafting deferred to the transition team
by design); UK layer checked (LexisNexis paywalled precedents, CLA member-only, and the
National CLT Network's Model Rules 2025 — free to download but explicitly forbidding
reproduction — curated as `uk-clt-network-model-rules.md`, collection 149). The
licensing gap is now documented across the US, Canada, and the UK, sector, movement,
and state alike. C3 is closed as a cycle; L3's build object stands precisely defined.

- **Question:** Does any farm-tenure vehicle publish open-licensed legal assets —
  bylaws, ground leases, resale-cap clauses, jury criteria, incorporation documents —
  given that the land scan verified the absence across every trust it examined?
- **Cells:** (1) the US legal-toolkit ecosystem (Farmland Access Legal Toolkit, Farm
  Commons, sustainable-economics law centres) at asset-by-asset licence level; (2)
  cooperative-farm and community-farm incorporations anywhere in the world; (3) the
  replication-cost argument: Agrarian Trust's 400-acres-in-12-years as the scaling
  failure, tested against whether template cost is actually the bottleneck.
- **Expected frame change:** determines whether L3 is a documentation gap (assets exist,
  unlicensed) or a genuine production gap (the sector has never written its legal layer
  as shareable assets). Either finding is load-bearing for the whole program.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells.

## C4. The entry-cost floor — what share of becoming-a-farmer does the open stack address? (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c4-entry-cost-pass.md`. Resolution: the
headline fraction is uncomputable — Canada publishes no itemized beginning-farmer
cost structure (recorded absence; Laforge et al.'s 1,326-farmer survey, curated:
barriers are affordable land and financing, not technology). Verified pieces:
farmland +9.3% (2025), 25% land-loan down payment and the FCC Transition Loan
(verified — the lender's own answer to the floor), DFO quota cap $24,000/kg with the
August 2026 exchange cancelled at 0.00 kg (verified), machinery sharing ~36%/acre
(Harris & Fulton, dated), 64 Québec CUMAs / 2,025 members with a
reproduction-with-attribution licence (verified — the only permissively licensed
Canadian state succession-adjacent document found), OPACA corn combining $65/acre
(verified). Corpus synthesis: open substitutes bite hardest on recurring cost rather
than entry capital; FarmBot (open but costlier) and L'Atelier Paysan's liquidation
are the honesty checks. **Resolution: the program's centre of gravity stays with
tenure and visibility; the open stack's honest role is lowering the cost of staying
in, not getting in.** Three records curated (`young-agrarians-bclmp.md`,
`cuma-quebec.md`, `laforge-new-farmer-survey.md`; collection 165).

- **Question:** What does it cost to enter farming in Canada today, itemized, and what
  fraction of that cost does open technology (machinery access models, right to repair,
  open hardware, FarmOS-class software, open agronomy) plausibly remove or share?
- **Cells:** (1) beginning-farmer cost structure from Canadian sources (census, FCC
  beginning-farmer material, farm organization entry studies); (2) synthesis over the
  corpus's own cost spines — dairy robot case, CEA synthesis, open-hardware records;
  (3) shared-capital models (machinery co-ops, equipment-sharing platforms) as the
  non-ownership route.
- **Expected frame change:** quantifies the honest ceiling of L4. If the open stack
  addresses 5% of entry cost, the program's centre of gravity moves decisively to
  tenure (L3) and visibility (L6); if it addresses a third, L4 becomes a headline.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells, with a synthesis pass over existing corpus
  documents first (they are already paid for).

## C5. Farm data and knowledge continuity across transfer (run 2026-11-19)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c5-farm-data-continuity-pass.md`.
Resolution: **the verified absence holds, and it now has a legal shape — farm-data
continuity at transfer is nobody's documented process.** The open stack has full
egress but no handover (farmOS CSV/API export verified with its own completeness
warning; LiteFarm ownership/deletion policy verified — no transfer workflow); the
closed stack has ownership language without continuity (FieldView export "in the
same format as uploaded" verified; Trimble's sharpest formulation verified:
ownership survives, "Customer will not have access to Customer Data after
termination" — at farm sale the data is owned by someone who cannot reach it); the
legal layer: Nebraska LB525 (2026) makes ag data producer-owned property (first such
statute; verified via NALC analysis, second-hand) with producer-following consent
architecture, and the one executed agreement examined (Northland/Fifield, SEC-filed,
verified) expressly excluded "corporate books and records" from conveyance. Three
matching absences recorded with search evidence. **The open stack is plausibly the
natural first mover here** — the adaptation commons gains a data-continuity annex
(export profile + model records-conveyance clause). Two records curated
(`nebraska-lb525-ag-data-act.md`, `northland-fifield-purchase-agreement.md`;
collection 170).

- **Question:** What happens to a farm's records, agronomic history, and data when it
  changes hands — and can an open substrate (FarmOS, open standards, data-coop
  structures) make the farm's institutional memory transferable rather than dying with
  the operator or being captured by a platform vendor?
- **Cells:** (1) what successor/landlord/vendor contracts say or don't say about data at
  transfer; (2) FarmOS and the open-ag-data stack's portability story; (3) data-coop
  precedents applied to succession (the G-OSA-27/data-cooperatives lineage).
- **Expected frame change:** establishes whether L5 is a real design surface or a
  speculative one; likely produces the program's most novel claim if the honest answer
  is that nobody — closed or open — handles farm-data succession at all.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells.

## C6. Seeing the transition — visibility and policy design (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c6-visibility-policy-pass.md`. Headline:
the transfer wave is still unseen (federal public beneficial-ownership registry
absent — ISED consultation live; BC LOTR still Canada's only public land registry of
its kind; Québec's corporate registry flagged unverified) — but the Saskatchewan JSGS
brief (curated as `examples/records/jsgs-saskatchewan-farmland-brief.md`, collection
153) proves the open-analysis method works from public titles data: 84 large owners /
1.44M acres / +140% in five years, CPPIB fully divested by 2023, **no evidence of
non-Canadian ownership** (correcting the "foreign ownership" frame). Tax layer
verified (AAFC QP note + OFA + PMO): LCGE $1.25M, inclusion-rate increase cancelled,
architecture **family-shaped** — nothing shelters non-family transfer to entrants,
co-ops, or community vehicles — and the federal answer to succession routes farmers
to FCC (the policy-level statement of C1's lender-independence finding). **Next
move: a design document in the `docs/policy/` genre** — candidate title "Seeing the
transition: open farmland-ownership analysis and the non-family succession gap" —
at the user's direction. **Design document written 2026-11-18 at the user's direction:**
`docs/policy/succession-visibility-design.md` — "Seeing the transition: open
farmland-ownership analysis and the non-family succession gap" (four faults, five design
principles, seven proposals: P1 Farmland Transition Observatory, P2 price-paid data +
beneficial ownership, P3 Canadian open succession-knowledge commons, P4
publish-the-legal-specification, P5 non-family transfer shelter, P6 successor-neutral
information service, P7 farm-link criteria/outcome publication; administrative/statutory
split in §5). Five new cycles added 2026-11-18 at the user's direction: C7 conflict and
dispute resolution, C8 alternative financing models (international), C9 longevity and
post-sale support, C10 gender/longevity/power, C11 decision-making and media influence.

- **Question:** What would an open picture of the transfer wave actually require —
  beneficial-ownership coverage beyond BC, transaction-level price data, investor
  ownership identification — and what policy design follows from it?
- **Cells:** (1) current state of Canadian beneficial-ownership and land-registry
  transparency (updates the land scan's BC-only finding); (2) the policy-design genre
  entry (`docs/policy/`): what a provincial or federal design proposal for transition
  visibility would contain; (3) tax treatment of farm transfer (intergenerational
  rollover, capital gains exemption) as the policy lever that most directly shapes
  whether farms transfer to entrants or to capital.
- **Expected frame change:** connects the program to the corpus's policy-design genre
  and the event's Canadian-policy audience; may become the program's first design
  document rather than a scan.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells.

## C7. Conflict and dispute resolution in farm succession (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c7-conflict-dispute-pass.md`. Resolution:
**a human-service layer with an open-document floor.** The institutional layer exists
and its criteria are published law — the USDA certified mediation programs make
"Family farm transition" an eligible public topic (verified; Ohio reports ~70% of
docket is farm transition, verified as reported), while Canada's public layer
(FDMS) is insolvency-only and no farm-succession ombudsman exists anywhere. The
professional competency layer is the most enclosed found in this program (IFTN
certification $900–$999; Froese commercial). The buildable open surface: model
dispute-resolution clauses for tenure/transfer instruments (Purdue's mediation
clause, the NZ Order, the Schumacher lease as instances) — added to the adaptation
commons as a conflict annex. One record curated (`usda-certified-mediation-programs.md`,
collection 159).

- **Question:** Succession is a family-conflict process — unequal inheritances between
  on-farm and off-farm siblings, buyout disputes, will contests, negotiation breakdowns.
  What dispute-resolution knowledge and mechanisms exist (mediation frameworks,
  facilitation curricula, ombudsman models), are they open or proprietary, and does
  openness help (published criteria, adaptable frameworks) or does the trust problem
  require a person, not a document?
- **Corpus hooks:** the Schumacher model lease's Section 11 (mediation and arbitration
  built into the tenure instrument itself — dispute resolution as a clause, not a
  service); the Young Agrarians toolkit's acknowledgement of Elaine Froese (the
  farm-family-conflict specialist, whose frameworks are books and paid workshops);
  the C1 finding that family-negotiation frameworks are one of the two gaps nothing
  covers openly.
- **Expected frame change:** determines whether conflict support is a buildable open
  layer (adaptable frameworks) or a human-service layer openness can only document.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells (frameworks/curricula; institutional
  models — provincial farm-mediation services, ombudsman; the tenure-embedded model —
  mediation clauses as open legal assets, connecting to L3).

## C8. Alternative financing models for farm succession — the international scan (run 2026-11-18)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c8-alternative-financing-pass.md`. Headline:
the world's non-family farm-transfer financing models are real, operating, and
documented — and none is open, with one statutory near-exception: **NZ's Sharemilking
Agreements Order 2011**, a complete statutory standard income-share contract published
openly (verified verbatim: 21% share floor protected against erosion except through the
agreement's own dispute-resolution mechanism). Verified operating models curated as
five records (collection 158): Kulturland eG (interest-free member capital), Iroquois
Valley REIT (patient capital at scale: 131 investments, 36,000+ acres, lease-to-
purchase), Fordhall Farm (community shares bought the freehold; 100-year tenancy),
the FAO land-banking study (`CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO` — Latvia's purchase-lease-back with
buy-back right; France's SAFER whole-farm lease intermediation), plus Austria's
Leibrente documented in the pass (care labour priced into the transfer — the C9 answer
before C9 runs). Design notes carried to P5: the USDA guarantee's uptake caution
(pilot: 2 guarantees); state intermediation solves what private capital cannot.

- **Question:** What financing models exist elsewhere in the world for transferring
  farms to non-family successors — vendor financing/land contracts, share-milking
  (New Zealand), rent-to-own (the UK Ecological Land Cooperative's model, already in
  the corpus), land pensions (European retirement-in-place arrangements), community
  bonds and patient capital, cooperative share purchase plans — and could any be
  open-sourced (open financial models, term sheets, calculators) for Canada?
- **Corpus hooks:** the land scan's Agrarian Trust and Terre de Liens records;
  the ELC's rent-to-buy (C3); the corpus's cooperative-finance records (Coop
  Agri-Énergie Warwick, ZooShare community bonds); C6's finding that Canada's
  transfer-finance answer is a Crown lender's loan products.
- **Expected frame change:** turns "the capital wall" from a complaint into a design
  space; likely produces the program's first concrete international import candidates.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, four cells (vendor financing; share-milking and
  equity-partnership; community/patient capital; retirement-in-place models), with
  licence/formality recorded per model.

## C9. Longevity and post-sale support (run 2026-11-18 — with C10, one pass)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c9-c10-demography-gender-pass.md`.
Resolution: the post-sale window is long and longer for women (life expectancy at 65:
females 22.3 years vs males 19.7, StatCan 2023, verified); succession is a years-long
process (JRC, CC BY 4.0, curated); the seller-longevity failure mode is documented
inside installment-sale guidance ("if the seller outlives the term of the contract,
income problems could result"); Canada has no farm-specific post-sale income layer
(absence); Leibrente remains the only verified care-in-transfer instrument. Design
note: vehicles must be designed for the seller's remaining life, not the transaction;
care-in-transfer provisions join the adaptation commons as a named template element.

## C10. Gender, longevity, and power in farm succession (run 2026-11-18 — with C9, one pass)

**Run** — same pass document. Resolution: the hypothesis is **half-confirmed**.
Confirmed by open data (verified from the downloaded Agriculture–Population Linkage
CSV): 9,685 widowed farm operators, **Women+ 58.6%**, 95.5% aged 55+; female
operators 30.4% (first rise since 1991). Quantified where measured (Iowa State,
curated): 57% of identified successors are sons vs 8% daughters — but the
transferor's gender doubles daughter-choice probability. Not confirmable: widowhood
→ effective control (no study tests it; Canadian peer-reviewed work on women and
farm succession is absent — the pass's central absence). Design implication: the
build objects must serve a female-controlled, longevity-stressed succession
population; the patriarch-to-son default is empirically stale and demographically
mismatched. Three records curated (`statcan-agriculture-population-linkage.md`,
`iowa-women-and-land.md`, `jrc-farm-succession-dynamics.md`; collection 162).

## C11. How succession decisions are made — and media's role (run 2026-11-19)

**Run** — pass document: `research/2026-11-c11-decisions-media-pass.md`. Resolution:
**the decision environment is adviser-led, crisis-framed, and licence-silent.**
Advisor-side evidence (FMC research as quoted on FCC's site, verified): only 32% of
farmers seek business advisors, and precedence is accountants/lawyers first,
bankers/lenders second — complicating C1's lender-default finding (the lender is the
default content producer, not the first advisor). The plan-adoption disagreement
widens to four figures (12% Census, 8.4% FMC, 66% no-plan RBC via trade press — all
verified as reported, none averaged). Media framing: FrameWorks 2020 verified
verbatim ("overwhelmingly negative and fatalistic"); Canadian trade press leads with
the plan-gap crisis frame (RBC 66%, Froese "Procrastination and conflict avoidance is
killing agriculture" — verified). The counter-model: AFT's LANDED podcast (free-to-air
verified, but no licence, no reach data); and the pass's sharpest datum — Young
Agrarians' sibling Land Access Guide carries an explicit CC BY-NC-SA 2.5 Canada
licence with a remix invitation (verified) while its own succession toolkit is
licence-silent: the licence gap is a choice, not a capacity limit. **Honest wall:
no succession-specific farmer-side trust survey exists online — who farmers actually
listen to remains correspondence-bound** (who-to-ask: FMC research team, FCC audience
team, AFT LANDED producer). Reframing carried to P6: an information-environment
problem with no opportunity-framed incumbent. Three records curated
(`aft-landed-podcast.md`, `frameworks-farming-media-analysis.md`,
`young-agrarians-land-access-guide.md`; collection 170).

- **Question:** Who do farm families actually listen to when making succession
  decisions — extension services, accountants, lenders, other farmers, farm media,
  "farmtok"? What information environment frames the decision (crisis narrative vs
  opportunity framing), and what would an open information commons for transition
  decisions look like against the current mix of lender content, trade press, and
  proprietary advisor marketing?
- **Corpus hooks:** C1's finding that the default knowledge source is the lender;
  the corpus's own media-analysis posture (the project is itself a media
  organization — Metaviews/Future Herd — and the grocery concept's premise that
  operations are media environments); FCC's content operation (C1 record).
- **Expected frame change:** reframes succession knowledge from an asset problem to
  an information-environment problem; connects the program to the parent project's
  media competence and might define the "publication" layer of the adaptation commons.
- **Budget:** one discovery pass, three cells (trusted-source evidence; media framing
  analysis; the open-publication model), with correspondence flagged where audience
  evidence is thin.
