## 0. The question

Layer L1 asked whether succession-planning knowledge exists as scattered closed assets
(a curation-and-gap-filling job) or barely exists (a build-from-zero job). The pass
answers it with a sharper result: **Canada has a rich free-to-read succession-knowledge
layer and nothing open-licensed in it — while the one genuinely open-licensed succession
workbook found anywhere is American, and exactly the model a Canadian layer would fork.**

## 1. What was found

### 1.1 The Canadian layer: rich, free, and closed

Every level of the Canadian system produces succession knowledge, and none of it is
open-licensed (all licence findings below verified from downloaded document text layers
or live site terms, 2026-11-18):

- **Provincial guides.** OMAFRA Publication 70 (120 pages: farm business organizations,
  operating agreements, ownership transfer methods, taxation, wills) — Crown copyright,
  "© Queen's Printer for Ontario, 2020". Manitoba's Transition Planning Guide for
  Agribusiness (chapter PDFs with worksheets; chapter text layers show no licence line).
  Alberta's Agdex 812-22 Transition Planning Guide. BC's succession planning checklist
  (November 2023, no licence line in text layer). Saskatchewan's succession pages.
  Quebec: MAPAQ's multi-part "Étapes pour réussir un transfert de ferme" and the UPA
  (Mauricie) 18-page "Réussir votre transfert de ferme" — no licence lines found in
  text layers.
- **The federal lender's closed toolset.** Farm Credit Canada's transition hub
  (estate-planning worksheets, Pathway to Transition Checklist, will and estate quiz)
  sits under terms verified verbatim from the live page: "you are entitled to copy,
  download and print any content for your own personal use but you may not otherwise,
  copy, modify … redistribute, republish or reproduce, in whole or in part, any such
  content … or create any derivative works based on such content, without FCC's prior
  written consent." FCC is a federal Crown-corporation **lender** — its planning tools
  come from a party with a lending interest in the transition.
- **The national umbrella.** Farm Management Canada's Farm Transition Toolkit
  (farmtransitionguide.ca) aggregates Canadian transition tools; no licence stated
  anywhere on its site; its own framing is "Over the next 10 years, 75% farms will
  change hands, yet only 8% of farmers have a written transition plan" (verified
  verbatim). Note the disagreement with the corpus's other figures: StatCan's 12.0%
  plan coverage (2021), the CBA's 88% without written plans, FMC's 8% — different
  questions, different denominators; recorded as disagreement, not averaged. FMC's
  toolkit partners and funders include FCC — the same lender.
- **Federal tax explainers.** CRA's rollover, lifetime capital gains exemption, and
  capital gains reserve pages are free to read under Crown copyright — "For property
  used in a farming or fishing business that is transferred to a child, the transfer
  may be made on a tax-deferred rollover basis" — the mechanistic knowledge every
  successor needs, forkable by no one.

**The Alberta datapoint — the sector already reuses, through private permission.**
Alberta's Agdex 812-22 states (verified in the PDF text layer): "This publication and
the format are based on the 'Transition Planning Guide for Agribusiness', published by
Manitoba Agriculture. **Adapted with permission from the Manitoba Government.**" That
single line is the C1 finding in miniature: inter-provincial knowledge adaptation is
normal practice — and it runs on one-off private permission instead of a licence. An
open licence is simply the generalization of what Alberta and Manitoba already did once,
by email.

### 1.2 The open exception: one American workbook

**Land For Good, "Farm Succession Planning: Where Do I Start?" workbook** (© 2022 Land
For Good; USDA NIFA / Northeast SARE-funded, sub-award ENE19-155) states **inside the
PDF** (verified 2026-11-18): "This work is licensed under the Creative Commons
Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International License." Eleven worksheets (goal-setting,
retirement budgeting, family meetings, continuation checklist) — the only succession
worksheet set found anywhere in this pass that can legally be adapted and republished.
It is the model object for a Canadian adaptation layer: CC BY-SA permits the exact
jurisdictional fork that every Canadian asset forbids.

### 1.3 The open-but-unforkable middle

Oregon State University's open textbook *Introduction to Farm Succession Planning*
(chapter "Getting Organized" verified in-PDF: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, Brekken & Hobson ©2019)
is redistributable but the NoDerivatives clause blocks the adaptation a Canadian fork
needs. AgTransitions (University of Minnesota Center for Farm Financial Management) is
a free interactive transition-plan web app — "free of charge for use individually or in
educational programs" (verified verbatim) — with no licence stated; free-of-charge is
not open. Wisconsin Extension's succession workbook (with linked Excel spending-plan
worksheets) is all-rights-reserved ("Copyright ©2024 by the Board of Regents of the
University of Wisconsin System… All rights reserved", verified).

## 2. The finding

L1 resolves as **curation-and-adaptation, not build-from-zero** — with the adaptation
obstacle being the same licensing gap C3 found on the documents side:

1. The knowledge exists, at every level: provincial, national, lender, farm-organization,
   university, and Quebec-language.
2. In Canada, **no open-licensed succession knowledge asset was found in any category**
   (four-cell pass; recorded as a pass finding, not an exhaustive absence — text-layer
   search can miss page-image licence statements, and site-level terms were checked
   only where load-bearing).
3. The open assets that do exist are US-based and either fully forkable (Land For Good,
   CC BY-SA) or forkless by licence design (OSU, CC BY-NC-ND).
4. The adaptation precedent already exists inside Canada — Alberta adapting Manitoba's
   guide "with permission" — so an open-licensed Canadian layer is not a new behaviour,
   only a licence replacing a permission slip.

## 3. What this changes about the frame

- **L1's build object is concrete:** a Canadian adaptation of the Land For Good CC
  BY-SA workbook — legally permitted by its licence, needing only jurisdictional
  substitution of the tax/rollover content and Canadian case material — plus filling
  the two gaps no source covers openly (family-negotiation frameworks; open financial
  models/calculators).
- The **FCC conflict** sharpens L1's design constraint: the country's most-used
  transition tools are authored by its largest farm lender, under terms that forbid
  forking. An open Canadian layer is also a lender-independence layer; this belongs in
  the concept document's honesty section.
- L1 and L3 are one program: the C3 legal documents and the C1 knowledge layer have
  the same defect (free-to-read, unlicensed) and would share the same vehicle (an
  open-licensed Canadian adaptation commons).
- FMC's 75%/8% figures enter the corpus as **verified as reported, disagreement
  recorded** against StatCan's 12.0% and the CBA's 88% — three numbers for
  "succession-plan coverage" from three methodologically distinct sources.

## 4. What this pass does not do

- It does not read OMAFRA Pub 70's 120 pages, Manitoba's full TPG (8.24 MB download
  failed in the subagent pass; assessed from a chapter PDF), or the full FMC toolkit —
  licence findings for those rest on text-layer searches of sampled documents.
- It does not inspect the licences of Wisconsin's linked Excel spreadsheets or Iowa
  State's financial-statements spreadsheet directly.
- It does not audit every province (NL and the territories unexamined; PEI's series
  inventoried in C3, licence unverified).
- It does not treat AgTransitions' licence as absent — it is **unstated**; a
  correspondence-grade question for the "who to ask" list.
