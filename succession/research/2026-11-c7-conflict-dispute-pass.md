## 0. The question

Succession is a family-conflict process: unequal inheritances between on-farm and
off-farm siblings, buyout disputes, negotiation breakdowns. The design document's
adaptation commons (P3) needs to know whether the conflict layer is documents or people.

## 1. What was found

### 1.1 The institutional layer exists — and its criteria are published law

- **USDA Certified Mediation Program** (verified from the program page): family
  succession conflict is already an *eligible topic* in US state agricultural
  mediation — the page lists "Family farm transition" and "Farmer-neighbor disputes"
  among mediable issues. The certification criteria for state programs are published
  federal regulation (7 CFR 785.3: mediator training, confidentiality, notification
  duties) — the process standard is openly published law.
- **The caseload evidence:** Ohio Farm Resolution Services reports "About 70% of our
  mediation docket consists of working with families on farm transition and family
  communication needs" (verified as reported — OSU Extension blog, not an annual
  report). Succession conflict is already the core institutional caseload where a
  program exists. Oregon's program subsidizes the first four hours free — a hybrid
  free/paid model.
- **Canada's public layer is insolvency-only.** The Farm Debt Mediation Service is
  free, statutory, and well-documented — but triggers on financial obligation
  difficulties. No Canadian jurisdiction operates a succession- or family-conflict
  mediation scheme. Ireland's farm mediation is a paid member service (€300 initial
  consultation, IPAV/IFA/MII); Teagasc's state guide describes mediation triggers but
  provides no scheme. The UK embeds statutory arbitration/determination procedures in
  agricultural tenancy law (Agricultural Holdings Act 1986 s.6; GOV.UK guidance under
  OGL) — procedure as published law, services paid.
- **No operating farm-succession ombudsman exists in any jurisdiction searched.** The
  only ombudsman-adjacent item is a proposed New Jersey bill (A2745, 2026) whose remit
  is regulatory navigation, not dispute resolution.

### 1.2 The professional layer is enclosed in paid credentialing

The main facilitation pipeline — the International Farm Transition Network's Certified
Farm Succession Coordinator — is paid proprietary training (verified from the training
PDF: "The early-bird registration fee is $900 per person before February 1, 2026 and
$999 thereafter"), with no open curriculum published. Canada's best-known farm-family
conflict specialist (Elaine Froese, acknowledged in the Young Agrarians toolkit)
operates commercially — books and paid webinars ("How to have more harmony and less
conflict on family farms … $38.75 CDN", verified from her shop page). University
curricula exist free-to-read (Mississippi State's "Communication and Conflict in
Succession" module) but are unlicensed and embed proprietary instruments (the
Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument). **The competency layer — who is taught to
hold a farm-family conflict conversation — is the most enclosed layer found in this
program so far, more enclosed than the legal documents (C3).**

### 1.3 The document layer: dispute resolution is already being written into instruments

The tenure-embedded model is real and recurring:

- The **NZ Sharemilking Agreements Order 2011** (verified in C8): the share floor is
  enforceable only through the agreement's own dispute-resolution mechanism.
- **Purdue Extension's** farm-succession guidebook embeds a model LLC operating
  agreement with mediation-then-arbitration (verified from the PDF: "the matter shall
  be submitted to mediation") — free-to-read, unlicensed.
- A **UK council farm tenancy** publishes a full escalation ladder in the tenancy
  itself (30-day negotiation → CEDR mediation → RICS arbitration) — a published
  contract, structurally parallel to the NZ Order.
- **NC State Extension explicitly advocates the practice**: "Disagreements should be
  subject to a clear dispute resolution process such as a mediation requirement" —
  official guidance recommending tenure-embedded ADR, with no open model clause set
  to copy.
- The one open artifact remains **Land For Good's CC BY-SA workbook** — its
  family-meeting and conflict-facing worksheets are the only adaptable conflict
  instrument found ("They may surface strong emotions… and conflict among family
  members and/or other stakeholders. This is all quite common.").

## 2. The finding

**Conflict support is a human-service layer with an open-document floor.** The
institutional models exist and their quality criteria are published law (the US
certification regime); the caseload evidence shows succession conflict is the core
demand where the service operates. But three enclosures stack on top of it: Canada's
public mediation is insolvency-only (no succession scheme); the professional
competency layer is locked in paid credentialing; and the model clauses are
free-to-read but unlicensed. The buildable open surface is therefore narrow but
real: **open-licensed model dispute-resolution clauses for tenure and transfer
instruments** (the C3 adaptation commons gains a conflict annex), plus the published
US certification criteria as the design reference for any future public scheme. What
openness cannot do is hold the family meeting — that stays human.
