## 0. The question

Succession's obstacles are substantially about access to professional services and
proprietary legal documents. If open-licensed legal assets existed — bylaws, ground
leases, resale-cap clauses, incorporation documents, that a group could legally adapt
and reuse — they would attack that bottleneck directly. The land scan's absence finding
was blunt: nothing open-licensed, anywhere it looked. This pass re-ran that absence
against the specific landscape of farm-tenure and farm-succession legal material.

## 1. The landscape found

Asset-by-asset, licence level. "Free to read" means downloadable without payment; it
does **not** mean open — the licence determines whether anyone may adapt and reuse.

### 1.2 The open-licensed layer (thin, and not farm-specific)

| Source | What it licenses | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Sustainable Economies Law Center (SELC) | Legal guides, sample documents, toolkits for solidarity-economy entities — including **Resources for Land Stewards** ("Radical Real Estate Law School": legal tools to democratize land, housing, food, and energy) | Site-wide statement, repeated across resource pages: "Feel free to share, use, and/or build upon any of these cartoons and these legal resources, which are licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0" (verified across multiple theselc.org pages) |
| Farm Commons | The most farm-law-dedicated library found (business structures, sales agreements, LLC operating agreements for farming couples) | Copyright, with **CC BY-ND 4.0 granted only to paid Institutional and Legal Professional members** (legal professional membership $299/yr, verified from membership page) — and NoDerivatives even then: members may copy and redistribute but not adapt |

**The finding: in the entire landscape examined, exactly one organization publishes
farm-adjacent legal resources under a genuine share-alike open licence — SELC — and its
work is solidarity-economy law generally, not farm tenure or succession specifically.
Farm Commons, the sector's own legal-resilience organization, licenses conditionally to
paying members under a no-derivatives licence that forbids the very adaptation
(jurisdictional tailoring) farm legal documents always require.**

## 2. The finding: a licensing gap, not a drafting gap

The G-OSA-35 absence ("no open-source farm-succession tooling; the data is open, the
tools are consultants' proprietary software") refines rather than simply holds. The
documents largely **exist** — bylaws, equitable leases, donation agreements, succession
guides, even editable lease .docx files. What almost nothing grants is the right to
**adapt and redistribute** them. Free-to-read without derivative rights means every
real-world use still requires a lawyer to redraft from scratch — which reproduces
exactly the professional-services bottleneck this cycle set out to test.

The distinction matters for the program's thesis: **the open legal infrastructure gap in
farm succession is a licensing gap, not a drafting gap.** The corpus's definition
document's control/funding/value-capture test applies cleanly: a template library nobody
may legally fork is not open infrastructure, no matter how free its landing page.

## 3. Replication cost: what the evidence says (cell 3)

- The hard-won legal learning is real and is now published free-to-read: the Agrarian
  Commons guide narrates how 501(c)(2)/(c)(25) structures were expected to work,
  and were found too narrow (educational programming risks tax-exempt status; the
  parent's 51% board-control requirement is "at odds with AT's goal of supporting a
  local community to have local control"). That learning cost a law school partnership
  (CAFS) plus a national nonprofit years to produce — and the resulting documents
  carry no licence that would let the next group build on them.
- Illustrative legal costs (from the Farmland Access Legal Toolkit's lawyer-cost page —
  **illustrative examples, not survey data**): ~$2,500 flat fee for farm business LLC
  formation; ~$4,000 for an estate plan (will and trust) for a farming couple;
  ~$250/hour typical, with a 20-hour farm-sale transaction ≈ $5,000.
- Agrarian Trust's own FAQ names "high upfront financial burdens and ongoing expenses
  and costs which can lead to burnout and high member turnover" as core barriers to
  commons sustainability (verified as reported).
- The corpus's scaling datapoint stands: Agrarian Trust at 400+ acres across 10
  commons in 8 states after ~12 years (verified, per G-OSA-35).

Reading: the cost is not primarily *drafting a template* — templates exist to copy. The
cost is (a) the legal judgment to choose and tailor a structure to jurisdiction and
tax law, and (b) the organizational startup burden. Open licensing attacks (a)'s
replication cost; nothing found currently permits it. This is consistent with the
land scan's sharpest land-cell conclusion: the binding constraint is governance and
licence, not tooling or knowledge production.

## 4. What this changes about the frame

- L3 is confirmed as a real and sharply bounded surface: **the program's buildable
  object is an open-licensed adaptation layer over documents that mostly already
  exist** — Canadian-jurisdictional versions of equitable leases, commons bylaws, and
  succession structures, licensed to permit adaptation, with the SELC licensing
  practice as the only in-sector precedent found.
- The Young Agrarians BC Transition Toolkit is the closest Canadian analog to
  "succession knowledge as a commons" and should be read in full before any L1/L3
  design work (it is a 79k-character page; recorded, not yet fully mined).
- The Farmers Land Trust's framework-and-brand conditions are a new shape for the
  program's honesty section: openness used as a **membership model** (Farm Commons)
  and as an **adoption covenant** (Farmers Land Trust) — two more ways the sector
  gestures at openness without granting it.
- A Canadian-specific hole is now documented rather than assumed: government
  succession guides (OMAFRA Pub 70 et al.) are free but their reuse terms are
  unverified, and no Canadian open-licensed farm legal document was found in this
  pass — recorded as **unscanned-partial, not verified absence** (one pass, not an
  exhaustive audit).
