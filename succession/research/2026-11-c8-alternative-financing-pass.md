## 0. The question

Canada's transfer-finance answer is a Crown lender's loan products (C6's verified finding).
This pass asks what the rest of the world does — and which structures are replicable
enough to import, adapt, and publish openly.

## 1. Cell 1 — Vendor financing / land contracts / seller-held mortgages

- **USDA FSA Land Contract Guarantee Program** (verified from the program factsheet): a
  national structure that de-risks seller-held land contracts to beginning farmers —
  "The guarantee provides an incentive to sell to individuals in these groups as it
  reduces the financial risk to the seller due to buyer default on the contract
  payments." Structure: prompt-payment guarantee (3 installments) or standard guarantee
  (90%), 5% down, 20-year amortization. **The scale caveat is the finding:** the pilot
  produced "only 2 guarantees" (Federal Register, 2010), with ~140/year projected
  nationally — a well-designed public de-risking instrument that barely moved the
  market. Recorded as a caution for P5's design (a shelter without uptake infrastructure
  does not transfer farms).
- **New Zealand:** vendor financing remains a recognised farm-purchase mechanism, but
  "their popularity has declined" (2025 research, reported in trade press — verified as
  reported). **Canada:** vendor take-back mortgages exist in Canadian practice but no
  open-licensed term sheet or model was found (absence recorded).

## 2. Cell 2 — Share-milking, equity partnerships, herd-ownership pathways

The pass's headline: **New Zealand publishes a complete, statutory, ready-to-sign
income-share farm-entry contract.** The Sharemilking Agreements Order 2011 (SR 2011/295,
in force, latest version as at 2023-12-23, administered by MBIE) sets the full standard
agreement text — minimum share floors, feed and stock clauses, annexes — publicly, under
Crown copyright (no open licence in the text). Verified verbatim: "The sharemilker must
receive not less than 21% of income derived from milk supplied to the dairy company
during the duration of this Agreement… The 21% is net of the sharemilker's farm
operating expenses and **must not be reduced in any way or by any means other than
under clauses 63 to 66 or by the result of dispute resolution as provided for under
this Agreement**." Note the embedded C7 hook: dispute resolution is written into the
income-share instrument itself, as a constraint on the stronger party.

- DairyNZ documents equity partnerships and herd-owning sharemilking (HOSM) as
  industry-standard pathways — "Equity partnerships offer the opportunity to take an
  ownership stake in a farm business, with lower outlay required to purchase a business
  outright" (verified). Its contract templates, however, are member-gated ("Contracts
  are available from a variety of sources: Federated Farmers, Lawyers, Farm Advisers").
- **The honest trend finding:** the traditional ladder is weakening — "sharemilking
  opportunities have reduced and may not, on their own, produce sufficient equity"
  (2025 NZ research, verified as reported). The model that works is eroding in its home
  jurisdiction; an import would import the contract form, not the current economics.

## 3. Cell 3 — Community and patient capital

- **Kulturland eG (Germany), verified:** a registered cooperative whose
  "Genossenschaftsmitglieder stellen hierfür zinsloses und langfristiges Kapital ohne
  Gewinnbeteiligung zur Verfügung" (members contribute interest-free, long-term capital
  without profit participation) so farms changing hands convert to community ownership
  with long-term leases to successor farmers — with a detailed published model
  (eG + KG + charitable foundation structure, tax treatment of below-market sale,
  heritable building right) and a published cooperative statute. Free-to-read,
  unlicensed; the structure is replicable, the documents not forkable.
- **Iroquois Valley Farmland REIT (US), verified:** patient capital at scale — 131
  investments, 36,000+ acres (as of 2024-12-31), ~950 investors, B-Corp public-benefit
  structure — offering "six-year leases, and in the seventh year, farmers have the
  option to either purchase the land or continue renewing long-term leases with us
  indefinitely." Its offering documents are regulated securities — the model is
  documented, the instruments are proprietary.
- **Fordhall Farm (UK), verified:** ~20 years operating a community-benefit-society
  freehold — "The Fordhall Community Land Initiative has existed since 2005… renting
  the land to a tenant farmer on a 100-year Farm Business Tenancy" — the community-
  shares-fund-the-succession-sale precedent (shareholder count recorded as
  verified-as-reported; the page confirms the 20th anniversary and the tenancy).
- **Latvia ALTUM (verified via the FAO study):** a state **purchase-and-lease-back with
  buy-back option** service (launched 2017): "Land sale, leaseback and buy-back option
  agreements are concluded at the same time… The farmer leases back the land and also
  has the option to exercise the buyback right after five years. The lease prices are
  market-based." Public patient capital as a cash-flow bridge for farmers in distress
  or transition.
- **France SAFER lease intermediation (same FAO study):** the statutory land agency
  collects lease promises from all of a retiring farmer's landlords so the farm
  transfers **whole** — an institutional answer to the fragmentation problem that
  defeats many transfers.
- Land van Ons (Netherlands, ~30,000 members from €20/year) recorded as a replicable
  citizen-capital structure, but mission-mismatched (biodiversity restoration, not
  succession).

## 4. Cell 4 — Retirement-in-place / land-pension / life-lease

- **Austria's Leibrente (verified):** the classic extrafamilial succession contract —
  codified in the Civil Code (§§1284–1286) and documented in peer-reviewed research as
  "the classic and most popular form of extrafamilial farm succession in Austria": the
  successor pays a monthly annuity against the farm's value and "can reduce this monthly
  amount by the duty of care for the transferor. Care in kind" — **the retiring farmer
  stays housed on the farm, and care labour is contractually priced into the transfer**.
  This is the instrument that answers cycle C9's question before C9 is run: a
  transfer vehicle designed for the seller's remaining life, not the transaction.
- **France (framework level, verified as doctrine):** sale with reserved usufruct plus
  lease to the buyer of bare ownership is legally admissible for agricultural property
  (notaires' congress doctrine: "le montage consistant pour le vendeur à se réserver
  l'usufruit d'un bien agricole et à consentir un bail au cessionnaire de la
  nue-propriété n'est pas caractéristique d'une fraude") — and viager (life-annuity
  sale) is a recognised modality. Usage scale for non-family farm transfer not found.

## 5. The finding

The world's alternative financing models for non-family farm succession are **real,
operating, and documented — and none is open**. Every cell resolves the same way as C1
and C3: operating structures at meaningful scale (Kulturland, Iroquois Valley, Fordhall,
ALTUM, SAFER, Leibrente, the NZ Order), none releasing its instruments under an open
licence. The one near-open artifact is statutory: **New Zealand's Sharemilking
Agreements Order**, which does openly what this program's adaptation commons would do
privately — publish a complete, ready-to-adapt income-share contract with minimum-share
floors and embedded dispute resolution, as a public instrument. It is the strongest
single import candidate and a template for what a Canadian equivalent would look like
(a statutory or quasi-statutory standard agreement, published, with floors and dispute
resolution built in).

For the design document's P5, the pass adds two design notes: (1) the USDA guarantee
caution — public instruments without uptake infrastructure produce 2 guarantees, not
140; (2) the ALTUM/SAFER insight — state intermediation (purchase-lease-back, whole-farm
lease assembly) addresses problems private capital structurally cannot (cash-flow
bridges, farm fragmentation).

## 6. What this pass does not do

- It does not verify USDA guarantee uptake beyond the 2010 pilot figure (correspondence
  target); does not analyse Iroquois Valley's replication cost for Canada; does not
  access the underlying NZ studies behind the trade-press reporting; does not quantify
  French viager usufruct usage; treats the Kulturland/Land van Ons German/Dutch quotes
  as verified verbatim originals without verified translations.
- It does not assess securities-law barriers to a Canadian community-shares regime (the
  Fordhall structure's import blocker) — flagged as the natural follow-up question for
  a design addendum or C8 residual.
