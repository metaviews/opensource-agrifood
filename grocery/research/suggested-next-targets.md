# Grocery concept — suggested next research targets

Date: 2026-08-26
Purpose: A single consolidated list of follow-up targets from the research passes,
so the next steps are visible in one place.

This is a working list. It is not a plan or a prioritization. Targets are
organized by theme, not by pass order, and are not ranked. Some are deep dives,
some are narrow lookups, some are engagements.

---

## A. NCG and the co-op sector

1. **Read the NCG Food Co-op Impact Report PDFs.** The 2024 PDF is currently unreachable
   (returns "Page not found"); the 2025 PDF download page exists at grocery.coop/article/
   food-co-op-impact-report-2025/. Confirm the figures against the primary source and look
   for any member-engagement or participation data in the reports themselves.

2. **Read the Korpeoglu et al. paper and related papers for the viability framing.** The
   paper is published in MSOM as "Supply Chain Competition: A Market Game Approach"
   (pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2019.3511). The SSRN version has been removed.
   Related papers: "Market Entry of a Socially Responsible Retailer" (UCL Discovery,
   discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10175417/) and "Should an Incumbent Store Deter Entry of a
   Socially Responsible Retailer?" (Sage, 2023,
   journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10591478231224935). The UCLA Anderson Review summary
   (Totty, 24 March 2021) is the current accessible framing, but the paper itself is the
   grounding. The Yale "Competitive Conduct of Consumer Cooperatives" paper (Duarte,
   Magnolfi, Roncoroni, Jan 2020, economics.yale.edu) is a related empirical question on co-
   op behavior and should be read for the viability framing.

3. **Read Hale 2018 in full.** "Digging into the process of cooperation in food and..."
   (Food Systems Journal, cited 37 times) is the clearest academic statement of the
   co-op participation gap ("little to no involvement of the consumer members"). Read the
   full paper rather than the search summary.

## B. OFN governance and structure

4. **Read the OFN handbook and community pledge.** The OFN handbook (ofn-user-guide.gitbook.io/
   ofn-handbook/) is wiki-style and open to revision; the community pledge v2.0 (May 2019) is
   at community.openfoodnetwork.org/t/community-pledge-v2-0-may-2019/1666. Get the current,
   detailed governance model and the instance-connecting agreement. This is the concept's
   closest structure reference and is not yet fully documented. The governance model is already
   well-grounded from the "A new way of working" page, the 2021 Discourse decision thread, and
   the P2P Foundation wiki (Schumilas), but the handbook may contain more current detail.

---

## C. DFC current state and architecture

5. **Read the DFC standard documentation in detail.** The GitBook is at docs.dfc-standard.org/
   dfc-standard-documentation. Get the ontology, taxonomy, API, and connector tool as concrete
   architectural references for the concept's interoperability layer. Confirm current
   participating platforms and the scope of the standard. The DFC has a v2.0.0 major release
   (Feb 2026), a separate documentation site (dfc-standard.org), an ontology (github.com/data-
   foodconsortium/ontology/), taxonomies (github.com/datafoodconsortium/taxonomies/), a
   prototype (github.com/datafoodconsortium/prototype, running at proto.datafoodconsortium.org),
   and a connector code-generation tool (github.com/datafoodconsortium/connector-codegen) in
   TypeScript, Ruby, and PHP. The standard is at v1.9 in implementation.

## D. Canadian co-op inventory

6. **Do a Canadian grocery-co-op survey.** Use the CMC "Co-operation across Canada" directory
   (crm.canada.coop/en/map) and the Ontario Co-op Association directories (ontario.coop/co-op-
   directories) to get a real inventory of Canadian food co-ops, their structures, and their
   participation models. This fills the Canada gap and grounds the concept in the corpus's home
   jurisdiction. The Wikipedia "List of food cooperatives" gives named examples (Karma Co-op,
   Kootenay Co-op, East End Food Co-op, Ottawa Valley Food Co-op, Co-op Atlantic) but is "not
   exhaustive" and "limited to notable food cooperatives." The CMC directory has DEI search
   functions (Indigenous, ethnocultural, women, youth, LGBTQ2S) and procurement search functions
   (retail, food, accommodation, etc.) that are directly relevant to the concept's culture/meaning
   and food-justice layers.

7. **Deep-dive one or two named Canadian food co-ops.** Karma Co-op (Toronto, one of Canada's
   oldest, ~800 members per a Toronto Star piece) is a natural first candidate. Look at its
   participation model, sourcing practices, and cultural/meaning dimensions as a concrete
   Canadian case.

## E. Culture/meaning — broader grounding

8. **A dedicated pass on culture/meaning outside Indigenous food sovereignty.** Look for
   operations, movements, and writings that treat food as cultural substance and structure an
   operation around it — diaspora food traditions, regional food cultures, family and community
   food practices. The Indigenous food sovereignty frame is strong but is one frame; the concept's
   culture layer wants broader grounding. Search signals from this pass: ethnic grocery stores as
   cultural pillars (McMurtrey, Yes! Magazine, Dec 2022; the Food and Culture Center's Ethnic
   Grocery Store oral history project); the academic literature on food and migration (a 2025
   ScienceDirect scoping review finds that grocery stores are "vital nodes for the sharing and
   transmission of migrants' culinary traditions," not merely points of sale); and any community
   food operations with explicit cultural framing.

9. **Engage Indigenous food sovereignty as a design reference, not a values add-on.** A
   dedicated engagement pass: what does the concept actually take from NAFSA's six pillars, the
   Mercille (Université de Montréal) Canadian scoping review, and the 2024 ScienceDirect systematic
   review of successful IFS interventions, and what does it commit to in relation to Indigenous
   food sovereignty as a design constraint (not just a solidarity nod)? The NAFSA six-pillar
   framework (Food Security, Food Access, Food Justice, Food Policy and Governance, Food
   Sovereignty, Land Access/Justice/Sovereignty) is a fully developed framework for food as
   cultural substance, governance, and power. The Mercille review names grocery stores as a food
   environment that Indigenous food sovereignty engages with — often problematic, sometimes a site
   of intervention. The "food is sacred" / "not culturally appropriate to sell" point is a direct
   challenge to the commodity frame. The seed rematriation point connects to the concept's open-
   source-seeds reference and the access-vs-control distinction. This engagement should be concrete
   enough to shape the concept's culture/meaning layer, not just acknowledge Indigenous food
   sovereignty as a touchstone.

## F. Platform cooperatives in food

10. **Look for platform cooperatives applied specifically to food in depth.** platform.coop is the
    hub; concrete food platform co-ops are not yet identified in this pass. The Platform
    Cooperativism Consortium's principles (broad-based ownership of the platform, democratic
    governance, co-design of the platform, aspiration to open source development and open data) are
    directly relevant to the concept's structure layer, but the concept wants to know which food
    platforms are genuine platform co-ops and which are conventional platforms with a different
    label.

---

## G. Operations combining multiple layers

11. **Look for any existing operation that combines several of the concept's layers in one.** Most
    precedents in the scan address one or two layers, not the full stack. OFN has a platform,
    open-source/Commons model, governance model, structural transparency at the consumer level, and
    interoperability (through DFC) — but it is a platform, not a grocery operation, and it does not
    have a continuous participatory information environment or a culture layer. NCG has federation
    at scale and community benefit — but it is a pre-internet federation and does not have internet-
    era interoperability or a continuous participatory environment. Co-ops have participation
    (intermittent formal) and community ownership. Indigenous food sovereignty has a fully developed
    culture/meaning framework. The concept is synthesizing these into a single grocery operation that
    addresses all of them. A search for any operation that moves further toward that synthesis would
    be informative — whether it exists or whether the concept's synthesis is genuinely novel.

---

## H. Sourcing transparency and traceability — deeper

12. **Deeper on sourcing transparency projects as structural vs. feature.** The scan found IBM Food
    Trust, Provenance, Sourcemap, and a substantial academic literature on blockchain-based food
    traceability. The concept's distinct move is sourcing-transparency-as-structural (where sourcing
    information is surfaced by default and is part of the operation's identity to eaters) rather than
    a transparency feature added to a closed supply chain. A deeper pass should distinguish the two
    and identify any operation that does sourcing-as-structural at the eater level — OFN's consumer-
    level transparency (who grew the food, how, and the economics of the chain) is the closest
    existing example, but it's a platform feature, not a grocery operation.
