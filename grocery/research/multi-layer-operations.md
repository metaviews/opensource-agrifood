# Multi-layer operations — first-pass search (target #11)

Date: 2026-08-28
Scope: The search for any existing operation that combines several of the
concept's layers (open sourcing, open information, open culture, open
participation, open structure) in one — the concept's novelty stress-test
from suggested-next-targets #11, and the synthesis's central empirical
question.

Method: parallel searches across transparent-sourcing grocery, radical price/
margin transparency, member-participation supermarkets (Park Slope wave),
open-source food retail software, producer-profile platforms, and named
candidate operations (Unicorn, The People's Supermarket, La Louve,
SuperCoop, Bees Coop, CoopCycle). Sources extracted where outcome-bearing.

## The result of the search

**No operation was found that combines all five layers, or even the
concept's distinctive four-way combination (sourcing + information +
participation + structure) in one operation.** This is a negative result
after a targeted search, consistent with the cross-cutting finding of the
whole research tree — but the search did surface operations that combine
two or three layers, and one layer-pair (structural transparency of
margins) with a stronger existing practice than the tree had recorded.
These are the concept's real nearest neighbours, and they define the
precise remaining gap.

## The nearest neighbours, by layer-combination

### 1. The Coop Supermarket Network (CSN), Spain — structure + transparency of margins + participation

The strongest new find of this pass. The Frontiers in Sustainable Food
Systems study (2024, "Are agroecological cooperative supermarkets an
alternative for scaling sustainable food?") documents the Spanish CSN: a
network of ~11 agroecological cooperative supermarkets (Landare with 2
shops/3,600 members/29 staff down to Biolíbere with 180 members), Park
Slope/La Louve-descended, with:

- **Transparency in gross margin policy as a defining feature**: "one or a
  very small number of fixed margins (2-3) on all products, which is
  around 20-25%." This is structural economic transparency — the
  operation's own markup is simple, known, and uniform, rather than
  per-product optimized for extraction.
- Member work shifts, commissions and work groups covering purchases,
  communication, governance, gender, IT, and environmental measures;
  democratic decision-making named alongside transparency and social
  responsibility as the model's core.
- Inter-cooperation through the network (management professionalization,
  IT adoption).
- The study's cautions: scaling requires more members and more member
  work (organizational sustainability pressure); the member base skews
  medium/high-income, highly educated, activist — "which limits the
  potential social base."

Significance: the CSN demonstrates that a network of real supermarkets can
make its margin policy structurally transparent — the closest existing
practice to the concept's "open structure" at the level of economics. It
does NOT do open sourcing at the record level, has no participatory
information environment (no producer data layer, no comments/news surface),
and no culture layer beyond its activist base. Two-plus layers, and the
two it does (structure + economic transparency + participation) are the
hardest ones — which is itself informative about feasibility ordering.

### 2. Unicorn Grocery, Manchester — structure + sourcing ethos + public price comparison

Workers' co-operative (76 members, all company directors, flat management,
equal pay), twice BBC Food & Farming Best Food Retailer. Five "Principles
of Purpose" (secure employment, equal opportunity, wholesome consumption,
fair and sustainable trade, solidarity in co-operation) with concrete
mechanisms: 4% of wage costs to a trade-justice fund, 1% to a solidarity
fund. Sourcing is relationship-based (direct grower relationships, own
sustainability criteria). Its distinctive public artifact: **in-store and
online price-comparison boards against the "big five" supermarkets** — an
open information surface about its own pricing relative to the concentrated
chains. Also published the "Grow a Grocery" guide spreading its model.
What it does not do: no record-level sourcing data (farm/route/conditions
surfaced per product), no participatory information environment, no open
data.

### 3. La Louve (Paris) and the Park Slope wave (Bees Coop Brussels,
SuperQuinquin Lille, SuperCoop Berlin/Madrid/Bordeaux, Alter Coop
Luxembourg, La Osa Madrid, MILA Vienna)

- La Louve (2017, ~5,000 members): member-work model (one shift/four
  weeks, 20-40% savings), "seeks transparency in all its actions:
  purchasing, sales, management and administration" (its own fundraising
  text), member-suggested product selection, aims to pay producers fair
  prices, and "easily becomes a cultural and social centre" (MILA's
  description of its role-model). Aims and reputation, not published
  structure: no evidence of published economics, sourcing records, or a
  participatory information layer.
- Bees Coop (Brussels): "triple commitment" (owner + worker + customer),
  3h/month member work, ~800 active members, works closely with local
  producers, reduces waste, promotes bulk.
- The academic literature (Frontiers 2024; VertigO 2023 on Québec SCPs;
  AIMS conference ethnography of La Louve's labour process) confirms the
  model's strengths (participation, community, margins) and its named
  weaknesses (labour-process strains, member-base skew, scaling).

### 4. Open-source food retail software — the technology layer exists in
pieces

- **Open Food Network** — the open-source e-commerce platform for food
  enterprises (ofn-and-dfc.md remains the deep reference). Platform, not
  operation.
- **Foodsoft** (foodcoops.net; github.com/foodcoops/foodsoft) — open-source
  web software for food co-operatives: product catalog, ordering,
  accounting, **job scheduling** — i.e., the member-work coordination tool
  the Park Slope-wave co-ops run on. Open source, community-maintained.
- **FoodCoopShop** (github.com/foodcoopshop/foodcoopshop) — open-source
  software for food co-ops and local shops (AGPL-3.0, active: 10,052
  commits, updated August 2026).
- **Grocy** — open-source household grocery management (adjacent, home
  scale).
- **Foodsharing.de** — open-source coordination of food rescue
  (food-waste-canada.md K22).

Significance: the concept's software layer is genuinely open and modular —
shopfront (OFN), co-op management including work-shift scheduling
(Foodsoft, FoodCoopShop), waste coordination (Foodsharing), interoperability
(DFC). No one has combined these into an open retail operation, but the
building blocks are free-licensed and maintained. The "open source" in
open source grocery is the most buildable layer.

### 5. CoopCycle — the federation+license hybrid (structure innovation
outside food)

Federation of bike-delivery co-operatives that built their own platform and
govern it collectively. Its license is deliberately NOT open source in the
permissive sense: the software is on GitHub but "cannot be used for
conventional commercial purposes" (CoopCycle case study) — a protective
license restricting use to co-operatives. This is a structural-design move
the concept should register: openness with an anti-appropriation clause,
the software-level analogue of the CARE/not-for-sale boundary the tree
keeps finding at the knowledge level.

### 6. What the negative space shows

Searched and not found: any grocery operation that publishes record-level
sourcing (farm, conditions, economics) with a participatory information
environment on top of co-op governance; any platform that attaches
community discussion to product-linked traceability data at retail; any
operation publishing its own waste/diversion data (the PCFWC model is
anonymized pre-competitive pooling, not openness — food-waste-canada.md
K23); any operation combining OFN-style software with Unicorn-style
ownership and CSN-style margin transparency. The synthesis's claim — every
layer exists somewhere, the joint realization nowhere — survives this
targeted search.

## Findings for the concept

- **The novelty claim holds after a dedicated search.** No five-layer, or
  even four-layer (sourcing+information+participation+structure),
  operation was found. The nearest neighbours combine: structure +
  economic transparency + participation (CSN Spain); structure + sourcing
  ethos + a public price-comparison surface (Unicorn); participation +
  community culture (La Louve wave); open software + governance (OFN,
  Foodsoft, Foodsharing).
- **The feasibility ordering is informative.** The layers that existing
  operations combine most successfully are the hardest ones conceptually —
  co-op governance, member work, margin transparency. The layers that
  remain uncombined are the internet-era ones — record-level sourcing
  data, participatory information environments, open data publication.
  The concept's distinctive work is exactly the part nothing has built;
  the co-op foundations it would rest on are proven.
- **Margin transparency is an existing practice, not a concept invention.**
  The CSN's fixed-margin policy (2-3 margins, ~20-25%) and Unicorn's
  price-comparison boards are working precedents for the concept's open-
  economics move. The concept should cite them rather than claim the move.
- **The software stack is open and modular.** Shopfront, co-op management,
  work scheduling, waste coordination, interoperability standards all
  exist under free licenses. A new operation would assemble rather than
  build — and could contribute back.
- **The protective-license precedent (CoopCycle)** gives the concept a
  structure-level answer to appropriation: openness of code/knowledge
  paired with license terms that reserve use to co-operative or
  values-aligned forms. This connects the software layer to the CARE/
  not-for-sale boundary: both are refusals of unconditional openness.
- **The CSN's member-base skew finding** (educated/affluent/activist)
  repeats the participation-gap caution: existing multi-layer operations
  scale poorly across class. The concept's design would inherit this
  problem as its hardest scaling question.

## What remains thin or unverified

- The CSN finding rests on one academic study (Frontiers 2024, plus its
  UCM mirror); the network's own materials were not read.
- Landare, La Osa, and Biolíbere were not individually verified beyond
  the study's tables.
- The People's Supermarket (London) — found and noted (community interest
  company, member-run, local sourcing), not deep-read; its trajectory
  (founded 2011, high profile, later struggles) would be informative as
  a caution case.
- Québec SCP named examples (from VertigO 2023) remain unenumerated —
  carried over from canadian-co-op-survey.md.
- No search was able to surface negative claims exhaustively; "not found"
  is search-bounded, not proof of non-existence. The claim is "no
  operation found," not "no operation exists."

## Suggested next targets from this pass

- Read the full Frontiers 2024 study (the extraction covered results and
  methods; the discussion/limitations would sharpen the CSN caution).
- The People's Supermarket trajectory as a caution case (founded 2011,
  media profile, what happened after).
- Landare (CSN's largest, 2 shops/3,600 members) as the closest existing
  analogue to the concept's full ambition — one deep dive would test the
  "two-plus layers" reading against reality.

## What this is for

This pass converts the synthesis's novelty claim from an inference (from
the shape of the landscape) into a searched negative result (no operation
found combining the layers), with the search bounds stated. It also
upgrades the concept's evidence base with three working precedents it
should cite rather than invent: CSN margin transparency, Unicorn price
comparison, CoopCycle protective licensing. The concept's remaining gap —
the internet-era information and participation layers on top of proven
co-op structure — is now precisely located.
