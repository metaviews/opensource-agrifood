# Deep dives — Frontiers CSN study, The People's Supermarket, Landare

Date: 2026-08-28
Scope: The three follow-ups from the multi-layer-operations search:
(1) the full Frontiers 2024 CSN study (discussion and limitations),
(2) The People's Supermarket trajectory as a caution case, and
(3) Landare (CSN's largest member) as the closest existing analogue to the
concept's full ambition.

These extend `multi-layer-operations.md`; the layer-combination framing and
the nearest-neighbour inventory are defined there.

## 1. The Frontiers 2024 CSN study — read in full

Sanz-Cañada, Yacamán-Ochoa & Pérez-Campaña (2024), "Are agroecological
cooperative supermarkets an alternative for scaling sustainable food?",
Front. Sustain. Food Syst. 8:1395819. Participatory action research
(2022-2023) inside the CSN and La Osa (Madrid, opened Dec 2020). Open
access (CC BY).

### What the full study adds beyond the earlier extraction

**The scaling framework.** The study frames the CSN as a "second
generation" alternative food network (Sage et al. 2021) — distinguished
from first-generation AFNs (consumer groups, farmers' markets, CSA) by
full-basket capability: supermarkets where a family can do its whole
shopping. Vertical scaling (scaling up) = political/institutional
influence; horizontal scaling (scaling out) = more members and
geographic spread; scaling deep (Moore et al. 2015) = values and
cultural shift. This vocabulary maps cleanly onto the concept's layers:
the CSN has some vertical and weak horizontal scaling; the concept's
information/participation/culture layers are "scaling deep" strategies.

**The margin-transparency detail, sharpened.** The fixed-margin policy
(one or 2-3 margins, ~20-25%) is confirmed as the model's crucial
differentiator, and the study adds the why: the supermarkets "reject the
margin compensation policy used by the large-scale retail sector for
reasons of fairness in the value chain." That is — the Big Retail
practice of varying margins per product (high on staples, compensating
elsewhere) is not just opaque, it is structurally extractive from
producers; the single-margin policy is a fairness commitment to the
producer side, not merely a transparency gesture. The concept's open-
economics layer should be framed the same way: margin simplicity as a
justice mechanism, not a disclosure feature.

**The economics of the model — the viability numbers.** The study's most
load-bearing findings for the synthesis's viability condition:

- Break-even: La Osa took **three years to break even** and still carries
  debt from fixed-capital investment.
- The 150€ rule: members need to spend **~150€/month** for the operation
  to cover its costs. At La Osa, only **22.5% of members reach that
  threshold**; average spend is 99€/month, and 45% of members spend
  ≤50€/month. "Many members' spending falls short of covering
  operational costs, a fact that jeopardizes the profitability of the
  project."
- The distance-decay: member counts, purchase frequency, and expenditure
  all decay with distance (correlation of membership with driving time
  r = -0.98); but monthly spend is distance-independent — distant
  activists shop rarely and buy more per visit.
- Engagement paradox: high satisfaction (92.6% rate the co-op 5-7 of 7;
  71% enjoy the mandatory shifts) coexists with weak economic commitment.

**The member-base finding, quantified.** La Osa members: 90% university-
educated, 60% women, mostly 30s-50s, 62.8% in 1-2 person households;
78.9% joined for fairer distribution channels; 78% "to align purchases
with values." The study names the segment (Piracci et al.'s
"sustainability-focused consumers") and contrasts it with "naturalness
and health driven" non-activist consumers, for whom distance, price
perception, and mandatory shifts are barriers (over 1-1.5 km becomes an
impediment). The activist segment within a 20-minute drive is
"relatively saturated" — recruitment beyond it is the unsolved problem.
The study's own limitation statement (bias toward members; non-member
perspectives needed) flags that the barrier findings rest on surveyed
members and near-members.

**The SWOT (from the CSN founding Congress workshop).** Weaknesses:
economies of scale/scope vs. Big Retail's IT-plus-logistics machine;
limited financial capacity blocking professionalized communication,
marketing, IT; "huge transaction costs in relation to suppliers, stocks
and references"; member-profile skew limits the social base; high
turnover in member work shifts "can sometimes jeopardize efficiency."
Threats: price competition with an oligopoly that influences regulation;
**greenwashing and localwashing co-opting the increased demand** the
alternative networks created. Strengths: SSE-principled social
innovation, bottom-up governance, strong community ties, values-based
differentiation, member shifts + non-profit status offsetting unequal
price competitiveness. Opportunities: growing organic demand (Spanish
organic spending +187% 2012-2022, though only 2.4% of volume), REAS
network support.

**The network's strategic agenda.** Governance: Governing Body, annual
assembly, technical secretary, work groups (purchases, communication,
IT, subsidies, coordination). Priorities: joint purchasing (started with
non-local products — tropical fruit, Canary bananas, cleaning products),
a common supplier database, shared tech (the **Odoo ERP** named as the
candidate shared system — notably NOT Foodsoft/OFN), a **collective
brand** to certify shared agroecology values in origin and socio-
environmental commitment, an incubator for new co-op supermarkets,
training programmes. Political influence (bulk sales, reusable packaging
legislation) is a named network goal.

### Findings for the concept

- The viability numbers (3-year break-even; 150€/member/month threshold;
  22.5% meeting it) are the concept's most concrete viability datum —
  they quantify what "viability requires participation" means in euros.
  An open operation that publishes member-spending viability in real
  time would be doing what no co-op currently does — but the CSN data
  shows why that publication is also a governance burden: the numbers
  are unflattering.
- The margin story is a justice framing, not a transparency feature —
  adopt that framing in the concept.
- The greenwashing/localwashing co-option threat is the concept's
  competitive-attention problem (open-in-food.md §7c) in institutional
  form: the demand the concept would serve is the demand Big Retail is
  already absorbing.
- The CSN's shared-tech agenda (Odoo) and collective-brand proposal are
  exactly the open-structure instruments the concept describes — being
  attempted right now by the concept's nearest neighbours. Watch the
  CSN: if its collective brand + shared ERP lands, the two-layer
  nearest-neighbour becomes three-layer.

## 2. The People's Supermarket — the caution case

TPS (London, Lamb's Conduit St, founded June 2009 by Arthur Potts
Dawson (chef), Kate Bull (ex-senior retail executive), David Barrie
(urban creative); community interest company; Channel 4 documentary Feb
2011; endorsed by PM David Cameron's "Big Society"). An academic
longitudinal study (Muñoz, Farny, Kibler & Salmivaara, Business &
Society, 2024, drawing on 6 years of data 2009-2015) documents the
trajectory in depth.

### The trajectory (from the study)

- Ideation: founders pitched a "harmonious-yet-deviant" hybrid —
  community-and-environment goals inside a real supermarket, anchored
  by a "Theory of Unsustainable Britain" (Big Retail as shaper of a
  wasteful, unjust society), amplified by the TV documentary and TED
  presence. Early wins: donations, below-market rent, Cameron
  endorsement.
- Organization: the "controlled-yet-empowering" tension — founders
  publicly embraced member democracy and community empowerment while
  privately vetoing community decisions, choosing external funding,
  and planning franchising. (The quote is blunt: "you can't show them
  a previous model.")
- Scale-up: **three years to break even**; "ethical and environmental
  aims became secondary in the face of commercial unviability"; the
  offering broadened beyond original scope; **volunteering — the core
  of the model — "dropped significantly over time"**; after "several
  failed attempts to keep the business afloat," members voted on a new
  legal structure, new commercial partnerships, and new funding
  sources. Stakeholders split between "growth-focused but drifting
  from community interests" and "persistent but hopeless."
- Later status: ambiguous. A 2024 r/london thread reports the shop
  shut with a sign about "moving to a new location" with no visible
  follow-up. The 2024 paper says the model "remained financially
  viable to the present day" (as of writing, covering to ~2015).
  Current operational status: unverified — flagged.

### Findings for the concept

- TPS is the closest thing to a full-tension case study of a
  multi-layer grocery venture, and its documented failure mode is
  precise: **participation (volunteering) decayed under commercial
  pressure, and the venture drifted from its social layer to survive
  commercially** — the exact participation-vs-viability tension the
  synthesis names as condition one. The drift was decided by
  governance: founders chose commercial survival, community decisions
  got vetoed.
- The TPS lesson for the concept is a design constraint: the
  participation layer needs structural protection from the viability
  imperative (like the fixed-margin policy protects producers) or it
  will be the layer that gets sacrificed — predictably, not
  exceptionally.
- TPS also demonstrates the media-amplification failure mode: a
  high-profile launch (documentary, PM endorsement) built expectations
  the operation couldn't sustain, and the study shows "conjuring" —
  projecting success — as a coping practice that deferred the reckoning.
  The concept's public-facing artifacts (the research tree itself) carry
  the same risk in miniature: claims outrun the operation at the
  concept's peril.

## 3. Landare — the closest existing analogue

Landare (Pamplona/Iruña, Navarra; founded 1991 by people from
antimilitarist and anarchist groups; consumer association, non-profit;
2 shops; figures vary by source and year: Frontiers 2024 gives 3,600
members/29 staff; Soberanía Alimentaria n.25 gives 2,600 families/25
workers; Interreg SME ORGANICS (2018) gives 2,800 families/3,000+
references — all snapshots, flagged).

### What makes it the closest analogue

- **Scale with values intact**: from ~100 members to 2,600-3,600 across
  30+ years, in a city of 200,000 — meaning "over 5% of the people
  living in Pamplona buy at least one product in Landare" (Garnatxo,
  SA n.25). The only operation in the whole research tree that
  demonstrates the concept's ambition at city scale over decades.
- **Structural economics, published in principle**: sale prices set so
  that **80% goes to producers and 20% to management costs** (Interreg)
  — a value-chain split stated as policy, the same fixed-margin justice
  logic as the CSN norm, stated more radically. "In this way, a lower
  price is obtained for consumers and a greater margin for producers,
  forging a win-win relationship."
- **Sourcing as relationship**: direct purchase from **122 producers**
  with "long-lasting relationships based on mutual trust"; 67% of
  purchases bought directly, **95% in dairy and cheese** — record-level
  short-chain sourcing achieved operationally (if not published as
  data).
- **A defined "proximity" that is ideological, not just geographic**:
  150 km radius, but explicitly also "people with similar ideological
  views who are also open to debate" — proximity includes producers
  further away physically but closer in values. A culture-layer
  definition avant la lettre.
- **Governance**: horizontal division of responsibilities, participatory
  decision-making through assemblies; 25-29 staff (worker-side
  professionalism inside a consumer association).
- **No external capital**: no public or private funding, no loans ever —
  entirely member fees and own activity (Interreg). Financial
  sovereignty as a founding discipline.
- **Growth as reflexive practice**: gave up advertising deliberately
  (word of mouth only), and treats scale-up as continuous renegotiation:
  "we are not afraid to admit when we make mistakes"; the "constant
  influx of new members" as the standing challenge.

### What it still does not do

No open data (its 80/20 split is a stated policy, not a published,
auditable ledger); no participatory information environment (no
product-linked community layer); no open-source technology (its systems
are conventional); no culture layer beyond the association's internal
debating culture — foodways and cultural appropriateness are not part
of its documented frame. Landare realizes structure + sourcing-
relationship + partial economic transparency, at scale, with decades of
evidence — and no more. The four-layer combination remains unbuilt.

### Findings for the concept

- Landare is the existence proof for the concept's viability-with-values
  claim — the synthesis's hardest condition — at real scale: 30+ years,
  no external capital, 5%+ city penetration, 80/20 value-chain split,
  122 direct producer relationships. The concept does not have to
  argue feasibility from theory; it can argue from Landare.
- The 80/20 split is the most radical published value-chain commitment
  found anywhere in the tree (the CSN's 20-25% single margin is the
  same move expressed as margin; Landare's is expressed as producer
  share). The concept's open-economics layer has two existing
  framings to learn from and one obvious extension: publish the actual
  split per product, in real time, auditable — which neither does.
- The ideological-proximity definition is a culture-layer instrument:
  it shows a grocery operation can define its sourcing community by
  shared values, not just geography. The concept's culture layer can
  be operational (a sourcing criterion), not decorative.
- The reflexivity norm ("we are not afraid to admit when we make
  mistakes") is an organizational practice the concept's open
  information layer implies at the operational level: an operation
  that publishes its mistakes is the institutional version of what
  Landare does in assembly.

## What remains thin or unverified

- Landare membership figures: three sources, three numbers (2,600
  families SA n.25 undated; 2,800 Interreg 2018; 3,600 Frontiers 2024)
  — all snapshots, none authoritative; the trend is what matters.
- TPS current status unverified (2024 paper covers to 2015; 2024
  Reddit thread reports closure-and-move with no follow-up).
- La Osa member-survey findings rest on surveyed members; the study
  flags non-member bias itself.
- The CSN collective-brand and shared-ERP proposals are plans, not
  facts.

## What this is for

This file completes the three follow-ups from the multi-layer search and
upgrades the concept's viability evidence from theoretical to empirical:
the CSN/La Osa numbers quantify the participation-viability tension, TPS

value-proximity) and one obvious extension (publish the split, audibly).
