# Research direction

A note on what research we are heading toward and what we are looking for.

## What the research is for

The concept documents so far — `concept.md`, `levels.md`, `tensions.md`, `system.md`, `internet-patterns.md` — are structural and broad. They sketch a design posture and an architecture. They do not yet point to anything real.

The research is to find existing grocery alternatives — cooperative, community, platform, and otherwise — that instantiate pieces of this structure, that have solved pieces of the scaling or federation problem, that have run into the tensions the concept sketches name, and that can teach us what is real, what is partial, what is promising, and what is missing.

The research is not to find an operation that already is the concept. It is to find operations that have done pieces of it, to learn from what they built and what they ran into, and to let the concept sharpen against the real.

## What we are looking for

### Cooperative and community grocery operations

Existing cooperative grocery stores, community buying groups, food cooperatives, and community-supported food operations — especially ones that have thought about structure, participation, and sourcing in ways that are relevant. What did they build? How did they scale, or why didn't they? What tensions did they run into?

### Platform and network models in food

Existing food platforms, networks, and interoperability experiments — producer cooperatives with shared infrastructure, regional food networks, open data and open protocol experiments in food. Which of these are relevant to the federation and interoperability questions? Which are just conventional platforms with a different label?

### Sourcing transparency and traceability experiments

Existing operations and projects that have worked on sourcing transparency, provenance, traceability, and supply-chain visibility — especially ones that treat this as structural rather than decorative. What did they build? What depth of sourcing information is actually feasible? What did they run into?

### Participation and community-governed retail

Existing retail operations, platforms, and communities that have worked on participation, community governance, and consumer-as-participant models. Which are genuinely participatory, and which are just the usual review box? What did they learn about quality, abuse, and governance?

### Open-source and commons approaches to food

Existing open-source, commons, and cooperative approaches to food that are relevant to the structure question — open-source seeds, biological commons, data cooperatives, community-owned infrastructure. Which are relevant, and which are adjacent rather than directly applicable?

### The counter-concentration question

Existing operations and movements that have explicitly addressed the concentration problem in food retail and processing — counter-concentration moves, regional food-system rebuilding, decoupling from consolidated supply chains. What did they build? What did they run into? What does the concept learn from their experience?

## How the research relates to the concept

The concept is the lens. The research is the real. The concept should sharpen against the real — confirming what is sound, revealing what is missing, and forcing the concept to name what it cannot yet say.

The research should not be used to decorate the concept with examples. It should be used to stress-test the concept against what actually exists. Where an existing operation has solved something the concept assumes, the concept should say so. Where an existing operation has run into a tension the concept names, the concept should say so. Where the real contradicts the concept, the concept should revise.

## What we do after the research

After the research, the concept has two possible next steps, and we should decide which one (or both) is next:

1. Sharpen the concept. Revise the concept documents against what the research found — revise the architecture, revise the layers, revise the tensions, add specificity where the research supports it.

2. Move toward something concrete. If the research surfaces a real operation, a real pattern, or a real next step that the concept can point to, the concept can begin to point toward something buildable, testable, or demonstrable — not necessarily a full operation, but a concrete next step that is more than a concept document.

We should not decide this now. The research comes first, and the decision about what comes after should follow from what the research finds.
